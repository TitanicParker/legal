# Patient Guiding Statement

## Purpose

This statement records the patient's experiential centre and personal reason for pursuing the case. It is not a substitute for the canonical clinical record and it does not decide diagnosis, breach or causation. Where it refers to events capable of objective verification, those propositions must be tested against `REPAIRED_CANONICAL_V2.md`.

Its purpose is simpler: to prevent the case from becoming so clinically elaborate that it loses sight of what the patient was actually seeking help for.

## The patient's starting point

I did not go to the doctor because I knew I had bradykinesia, dystonia, pes planus, altered smell, micrographia or a genetic movement disorder.

I went because my feet hurt.

I loved walking. The pain had reached the point where I did not want to walk on my feet anymore. That was the practical problem I was trying to solve when I sought medical help, when I followed the Neurology referral, and when I entered hospital.

Everything else the clinicians found may have been medically important. But from my position as the patient, the test of whether the admission had dealt with the problem was much simpler: **had anyone actually worked out what was happening to my feet and what could be done about the pain?**

By the end of the admission, I believed they had not. That is why I said so. My recollection is that, before discharge, I told the neurologist in substance:

> **You have not looked at my feet. You have brought me in here and examined everything else, but you have not dealt with the reason I came.**

I did not need a theory of dystonia or PRKN to know that the feet still felt unfinished. I knew because they were my feet, the pain was still there, and I was the person who had to walk on them.

## What happened afterwards

The foot problem did not disappear.

Treatment after discharge showed that the feet were affected by neurological treatment state. When procyclidine was withdrawn, the outside-left-foot phenomenon returned rapidly and the broader feet–gait–tension state deteriorated. Podiatry then treated genuine structural pathology for years. That care was real and important, but the complete Podiatry record ends in 2019.

The central patient experience is therefore not that the feet had one simple cause. It is that **the pain remained important while responsibility for understanding the whole problem became fragmented**.

By 2022 the feet still required prolonged warming-up before walking normally. By 2023 the pain had become severe enough to be associated with death-related language and profound loss of quality of life. By 2024 my life had become markedly smaller because of the pain and the limits it imposed.

For me, that change matters. I had lived with foot pain for many years. It had been bad enough to bring me to doctors and eventually into hospital. But it had not previously brought me to the point of psychological collapse.

## Why March 2025 matters to me

By March 2025 the pain had become intolerable. I told the treating service that I was not willing to live this way and that foot pain was the dominant issue in my mind.

Then I took Sinemet CR for the first time.

The following morning the pain that had become psychologically threatening was gone or profoundly relieved.

That experience changed my understanding of the previous years. When I later obtained and read the records, I could see that for roughly two years I had been living with severe foot pain, taking high-dose pregabalin, losing function and quality of life, and experiencing serious psychological distress while the neurological treatment itself was materially affecting the feet.

That is the point at which this ceased, for me, to be only a story about an old diagnosis or an unresolved 2017 examination.

It became a question of **care**.

## Why I am pursuing the case

I trusted the clinicians to look after me.

What I expected was not perfection and not certainty. I expected that when a symptom became severe, persistent, treatment-responsive and psychologically dangerous, somebody would take responsibility for understanding it, treating it, and keeping me safe.

My complaint is not that nobody could name every mechanism. It is that the feet were important enough to bring me into hospital, important enough to require repeated treatment, important enough to become entangled with neurological medication, and eventually important enough to threaten my ability to continue living — yet I cannot see a point at which the whole problem was taken ownership of and resolved as such.

That is why I am pursuing the case.

The patient-level question is therefore:

> **How did a longstanding foot-pain problem that brought me into medical care become, under ongoing neurological treatment, an intolerable and psychologically dangerous pain state without anyone adequately reconstructing the whole problem and acting on it?**

And the simplest statement of the case, from my perspective, is:

> **My feet were the reason I sought help. I told the hospital they had not dealt with them. Years later the pain became severe enough to threaten my life, and a change in neurological medication produced profound relief. I am pursuing the case because I trusted the people treating me to recognise and act on that progression, and I do not believe they did.**
