11. source records feeding each consequential phrase where system provenance exists;
12. DaTscan request creation/order/result chronology;
13. any ward-round or trainee material capable of fixing the Protest chronology.

## Interpretive outcomes

### Outcome 1 — wording demonstrably predates Protest

Effect:
- materially weakens the post-Protest construction limb;
- leaves integration/stewardship centre intact;
- requires analysis of whether the branch was clinically earned before closure.

### Outcome 2 — wording first appears after Protest

Effect:
- materially strengthens decision-after-notice and construction suspicion;
- raises source-provenance question for each new phrase;
- requires close comparison between bedside evidence and later synthesis.

### Outcome 3 — wording revised after Protest but derives from identifiable prior clinical material

Effect:
- may support ordinary retrospective synthesis rather than improper construction;
- still relevant to what changed after notice and whether Protest was omitted while explanatory closure increased.

### Outcome 4 — wording first appears after Protest with no identifiable contemporaneous clinical source

Effect:
- substantially strengthens the grave construction limb;
- still does not by itself prove dishonest intent;
- requires forensic and expert assessment of significance.

# G. Forensic rule

Do not request evidence merely because it exists.

Every acquisition should answer:

1. which limb of the suspicion it tests;
2. what result would strengthen that limb;
3. what result would weaken or defeat it;
4. whether the evidence is clinical, documentary, provenance, regulatory or credibility evidence;
5. what downstream file must be updated when the result arrives.
