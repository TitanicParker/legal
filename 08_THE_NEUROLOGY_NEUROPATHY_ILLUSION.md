# 08 — The Neurology Neuropathy Illusion

## Status

It does not establish fabrication, record interference, dishonesty, concealment, motive, breach or causation. Those propositions require source-level and expert evidence.

The core question is no longer merely whether peripheral neuropathy was a reasonable possibility. It is how an uncertain neuropathy branch came to acquire enough documentary authority to organise the later record while never becoming a comparably established diagnosis.

---

# 1. The founding clinical problem

The GP sent an integrated problem into hospital: painful abnormal feet, abnormal gait, pathological tone / stiffness, impaired movement and deteriorating function.

During the admission Neurology substantially investigated the Parkinsonian syndrome. It identified a strikingly abnormal gait and a footdrop-like left-lower-limb appearance considered suggestive of dystonic posture rather than true foot drop. The same dystonic-looking gait was later used in the DaTscan / young-onset Parkinsonian diagnostic reasoning.

The feet, however, reached a different documentary endpoint.

The three canonical foot passages are:

1. **history** — foot pain, corns/calluses, fallen arches and leg tension, with an explicit patient distinction between foot pain and leg tension;
2. **examination** — dystonic / footdrop-like left-lower-limb movement, pes planus, hammertoes and mild oedema within a much broader neurological examination;
3. **interpretive disposition** — “corns and callouses and burning” may have been “completely separate” or perhaps secondary peripheral neuropathy, possibly related in some respect to previous alcohol intake, ending “Not clear”.

The founding asymmetry is therefore:

> **The lower limb was neurological enough to be used diagnostically, while the painful-foot relationship was made capable of belonging to a separate peripheral/structural explanatory branch.**

The question is what work earned that partition.

---

# 2. The terminology problem is now part of the mechanism

The record does not simply contain a neuropathy hypothesis. It contains a sequence of terms whose provenance now matters.

The July history is built around:

> **foot pain + leg tension**

Yet the same paragraph closes with:

> **“as the leg pain settled”**

That phrase is conspicuous because the immediately preceding logic was that foot pain had improved while leg tension remained. The current record does not establish whether “leg pain” was patient language, clinician paraphrase, dictation/transcription substitution, later synthesis language or a later edit.

Separately, the word **“burning”** is not identified in the preceding recorded patient history or examination. It appears in the specialist etiological paragraph at the same point as:

> **“completely separate” → secondary peripheral neuropathy → alcohol → “Not clear”.**

The present evidential proposition is therefore not that the neurologist invented “burning”. It is:

> **The current record does not show where “burning” came from, and the first identified July use appears inside the specialist sentence that constructs the possible-neuropathy branch.**

That matters because both **“leg pain”** and **“burning”** later acquire clinical lives in the record.

---

# 3. The documentary schism: clinical chronology is not document-authorship chronology

The completed July discharge product is composite. It contains later results and retrospective synthesis. Paragraph position in the final document cannot, by itself, establish when a phrase was first written.

For present purposes the admission should be conceptually split into two layers.

## Layer A — pre-DaTscan clinical/admission material

Reason for referral → admission history → neurological examination → provisional impressions → inpatient investigations → abdominal ultrasound → MRI brain/cervical spine → internal MRI interpretation.

The later embedded DaTscan result/addendum must be stripped out when reconstructing this earlier clinical state.

## Layer B — later document construction/finalisation

DaTscan referral/indication → DaTscan → DaTscan result/addendum → genetics/clinic-note synthesis and final specialist account.

The patient's alleged Protest belongs documentarily at the boundary between the completed inpatient work and the later finalisation/closure process.

This creates a critical new question:

> **Did the consequential wording about “leg pain”, “burning” and possible secondary peripheral neuropathy already exist before the Protest, or was some or all of it introduced or materially reformulated during later document construction?**

The finished page cannot answer that by itself.

---

# 4. The Protest may be the trigger for documentary closure — but that must be proved

If the patient's recalled Protest is proved, its significance is very specific. He says, in substance, that after an eight-day admission the service had examined everything else but had not dealt with the reason he came — his feet.

That creates an immediate documentary problem for the final specialist account. The service must close the admission and return an authoritative answer to the GP.

The working hypothesis is:

> **If the Protest occurred, and if source-level evidence shows that “leg pain”, “burning” and/or the neuropathy branch were first added or materially reformulated after it, the challenge may have triggered a retrospective act of documentary closure in which the unresolved foot problem was given an explanatory route capable of being sent forward rather than left visibly unfinished.**

This is the most serious current hypothesis in the file. It is also the one most dependent on proof of timing.

The counterfactual — what would have been written if the patient had not protested — cannot presently be known. The patient must not be said to have “caused” the neuropathy branch as a fact. The defensible proposition is narrower:

> **The Protest may have been causally relevant to document construction if the disputed wording is shown to have entered during post-Protest finalisation.**

---

# 5. The operational effect: feet leave the integrated neurological problem in two directions

On the completed specialist account, the patient's foot problem can move forward through two clinically respectable channels:

1. **structural/mechanical management** — Podiatry / GP handling of corns, calluses, pes planus, hammertoes and later focal fifth-MTPJ pathology;
2. **neuropathic explanation/treatment** — possible secondary peripheral neuropathy, later neuropathic-pain vocabulary, pregabalin and later neuropathy-directed investigation.

At the same time Parkinsonism/dystonia remains a separate high-resolution neurological project.

That division is not inherently improper. Structural pathology was real and peripheral neuropathy could be a legitimate differential.

The suspected problem is the relationship:

> **If the foot-specific neurological work was inadequate, the completed document may have allowed the painful feet to be operationally exported to Podiatry / neuropathy management while the Parkinsonian/dystonic significance of the same lower limb remained inside Neurology for diagnostic purposes.**

That is the founding shape of the Neurology Neuropathy Illusion.

---

# 6. Why “Not clear” becomes powerful

“Not clear” can mean either:

- **procedural uncertainty** — the necessary investigation has not yet been done; or
- **substantive specialist uncertainty** — appropriate specialist investigation has been completed and the relationship genuinely remains uncertain.

Inside a highly sophisticated discharge document, the latter meaning can be inherited even if the underlying work did not justify it.

Thus the founding possibility is:

> **an unfinished relationship may have been converted into authoritative specialist uncertainty.**

The neuropathy branch gives that uncertainty a vehicle. It does not have to become a proven diagnosis. It only has to remain sufficiently plausible to keep the painful feet from forcing reconstruction of the Parkinsonian/dystonic relationship.

---

# 7. The longitudinal mechanism

Once the branch exists, later clinicians do not meet an untouched problem. They meet the patient plus an inherited specialist information state.

The resulting chain can be expressed as:

> **possible secondary peripheral neuropathy / “Not clear”**
>
> → neuropathy becomes an authorised explanatory object
>
> → Podiatry and neuropathy-directed management proceed in parallel with Parkinsonian treatment
>
> → later complaints are increasingly recorded as “neuropathic pain”, “? neuropathic pain”, “peripheral neuropathy” and “leg pain”
>
> → pregabalin treatment and neuropathy-directed investigation generate further neuropathy-shaped records
>
> → patient himself eventually adopts the neuropathy vocabulary
>
> → later clinicians receive the complaint already framed through the terminology of the inherited branch
>
> → the original branch appears progressively more established even though it remains conditional.

The rule is:

> **Repeated descendants of a clinical proposition are not automatically independent evidence for their ancestor.**

That is how documentary path dependence can create the appearance of confirmation.

# 8. Burning, leg pain and neuropathy all have futures

The terminology now matters because it does not stay local to July.

The current record later contains:

- **“severe neuropathic pain again in his feet”**;
- **“ongoing ? neuropathic pain to both his feet/legs”**;
- the older **“peripheral neuropathy question”** being explicitly revisited;
- Fabry testing for a Parkinsonism/peripheral-neuropathy overlap;
- **“leg pain and shuffling is off related”**;
- severe bilateral **“burning”** feet and lower legs;
- **“If there is a degree of peripheral neuropathy here”**;
- **“peripheral neuropathic pain symptomatologies”**; and
- pregabalin described as treatment for **“neuropathic pain (of whatever cause)”**.

No single later occurrence proves inheritance from 2017. Some may be genuinely fresh patient descriptions. But the recurrence makes the original provenance of the terms material.

The forensic task is therefore to distinguish:

> **fresh patient evidence** from **clinician-generated terminology** from **language inherited from earlier records**.

---

# 9. PARK9 shows how quickly the neuropathy question became structurally useful

The highly sophisticated young-onset/monogenic Parkinsonian reasoning later entertained rarer genetic explanations including PARK9 as **“the neuropathy question.”**

This is significant because the neuropathy possibility had already become useful at a high level of neurological synthesis even though the surviving directed foot examination is comparatively low-resolution.

The contrast is:

> **thin recorded discrimination of the painful feet → increasingly sophisticated explanatory architecture around “the neuropathy question”.**

That does not prove the hypothesis was improper. It sharpens the question of what examination and evidence supported it.

---

# 10. The branch survives repeated neurological crossings

The later record repeatedly supplies evidence that the feet are not wholly independent of neurological state:

- procyclidine withdrawal worsens feet, gait and tension together;
- the treating neurologist records the outside-left-foot phenomenon returning rapidly during withdrawal;
- a later foot throw resembling foot drop is explicitly classified as Parkinsonian after normal distal power testing;
- later lower-limb symptoms prompt OFF/dystonia reasoning;
- a Parkinson's nurse later records **leg pain and shuffling as OFF-related**;
- by 2025 Sinemet is acknowledged to improve the feet from a pain perspective;
- more Sinemet is expected to provide further pain benefit but is constrained by dyskinesia;
- Sinemet CR is associated with profound relief.

Yet peripheral neuropathy does not have to disappear. The category contracts:

> **possible secondary peripheral neuropathy → neuropathic symptoms → ? neuropathic pain → “if there is a degree of peripheral neuropathy here”.**

This is why the branch is unusually durable. It can lose certainty without losing practical influence.

---

# 11. The Neurology Neuropathy Illusion

The illusion is not that neuropathy was impossible.

It is that an uncertain explanatory object can become **operationally real** before it becomes diagnostically real.

It can:

- provide a destination for unresolved symptoms;
- generate treatment choices;
- generate specialist vocabulary;
- generate investigations;
- shape how later complaints are recorded;
- be adopted by the patient;
- and later appear to possess a longer evidential history than the original basis necessarily warranted.

Meanwhile, contrary neurological evidence can be dealt with locally without destroying the branch:

- a particular foot throw can be Parkinsonian;
- particular pain/shuffling can be OFF-related;
- dystonia can coexist;
- levodopa can improve pain;
- structural pathology can remain real;
- pregabalin can help some pain;
- Fabry can be negative;
- and peripheral neuropathy can still remain possible.

The result is an **asymmetrically falsifiable documentary architecture**: supportive facts can strengthen the branch, while apparently contrary facts can often be accommodated without requiring its abandonment.

---

# 12. The newly identified founding mechanism

The mature formulation now has two layers.

## Layer 1 — established longitudinal mechanism

An uncertain neuropathy branch became durable enough to organise language, referral, treatment and investigation while later neurological-foot evidence was largely absorbed through local updating rather than a global reconstruction of the original feet–gait–tone problem.

## Layer 2 — documentary-construction hypothesis

If both of the following are established:

1. the patient's Protest occurred before final document closure; and
2. source-level evidence shows that “leg pain”, “burning” and/or the neuropathy branch were introduced or materially reformulated only after that Protest,

then a materially more serious proposition arises:

> **The Protest may have triggered the construction of a retrospective explanatory branch which allowed the final specialist document to answer the GP and dispose of the painful feet despite the patient's challenge that those feet had not actually been dealt with.**

The practical effect could then be:

> **foot problem → Podiatry / GP structural management + available neuropathy context → Parkinsonian/dystonic diagnostic project proceeds separately → later dystonia-related or treatment-state foot complaints repeatedly encounter an already-authorised neuropathy pathway.**

That would supply a possible founding mechanism for the longitudinal Neurology Neuropathy Illusion.

It remains **unproved** until document timing and provenance are established.
