# 16 — Correspondence Genealogy and Contemporaneous Witness

## Status and purpose

**Active evidential correspondence audit.**

This file records material correspondence from the patient's Gmail only where it bears on the clinical/documentary case, the patient's contemporaneous understanding, information flow, later complaint/regulatory activity, or the life of the suspicion.

It is not an email dump. The mailbox contains unrelated personal, employment, community and administrative material. Inclusion here requires a demonstrable case function.

The governing distinction is:

> **An email may be evidence that something was said, understood, experienced, transmitted or alleged at a particular time. It is not automatically proof that the underlying medical or legal proposition was true.**

Correspondence should therefore be classified as one or more of:

1. **contemporaneous patient witness evidence** — the patient's own words close in time to the event;
2. **contemporaneous information-flow evidence** — who told whom what and when;
3. **clinician/institutional evidence** — a clinician or institution's own account, decision or explanation;
4. **retrospective patient reconstruction** — later patient account of earlier events;
5. **advocacy/submission history** — what the patient later alleged or asked an external body to examine;
6. **regulatory/process evidence** — what an external body actually did, asked for or decided;
7. **private analytical draft/self-correspondence** — evidence of the development of the patient's theory, not independent evidence of the underlying care.

The public repository should not reproduce private addresses, telephone numbers, patient identifiers, unrelated family identities or irrelevant intimate material.

# 2. September 2017 — contemporaneous acceptance of diagnosis and treatment

## 11 September 2017 — private email

The patient wrote privately that he had been suffering from Parkinson's for a good few years, had eventually received a **“proper diagnosis and suitable medication”**, and was now a **“new man”**. He described the post-treatment state in strongly positive terms and referred to further specialist consultation.

### Evidential class

**Contemporaneous patient witness evidence.** Private correspondence not written for a complaint, regulator or litigation process.

### What it establishes

By September 2017 the patient strongly accepted the Parkinsonian diagnosis and experienced the treatment as transformative.

### Effect on the suspicion

**Weakens broad retrospective theories of an obviously failed or rejected 2017 neurological diagnosis.**

It does not defeat the narrower proposition that one presenting problem — the painful feet and their relationship to the abnormal lower limb — remained unresolved.

The two propositions can coexist:

> **Neurology found and successfully treated something enormously important.**

and

> **the patient may still have believed that the feet for which he sought help had not been dealt with.**

# 3. 4 October 2017 — the most important contemporaneous private narrative found so far

A long private email written less than three months after the admission contains several material pieces of evidence.

## 3.1 “Burning” is demonstrably patient vocabulary by October 2017

The patient describes his feet as **“scorching hot and filled with burning pain”** and later refers again to severe burning foot pain.

### Effect

This materially corrects any formulation implying that `burning` was never the patient's own language.

The correct proposition is now:

> **`burning` is demonstrably authentic patient vocabulary by 4 October 2017. Its provenance in the earlier July specialist synthesis remains unresolved.**

The October email cannot travel backward and prove where the July word came from. But it is important contrary evidence against a stronger allegation that the term could only have been clinician-generated.

## 3.2 The patient's explanatory model had changed by October

The patient says that the feet had previously received the blame for the onset of his problems, at least in his own mind, and that it was only **“very recently”** that he had begun to separate the foot problems as another aspect of his Parkinsonism.

### Effect

This is important to the patient-language genealogy.

It shows that the patient's causal understanding was already changing rapidly after diagnosis and treatment. It therefore weakens a simplistic one-way theory in which the patient merely inherited a clinician-imposed separate-foot model and passively retained it.

It also means the patient's present-day integrated theory must not be projected backward into July 2017.

## 3.3 The patient praised the admission and consultant

The same private narrative describes being admitted with suspected Parkinsonism, undergoing extensive testing, and being glad that his GP described the consultant as thorough.

### Effect

This is genuine contrary evidence to any portrayal of the entire admission as contemporaneously experienced as obviously deficient.

It is compatible with a narrower Protest about the unresolved feet.

## 3.4 The detailed private narrative does not presently mention the Protest

The email recounts the pre-admission progression, admission, diagnosis, treatment and subsequent experience in considerable detail, yet no Protest has been identified in the text reviewed.

### Evidential treatment

This is **limited negative evidence**, not disproof.

A private narrative need not contain every material event. Its omission is nevertheless relevant to weight because the email is detailed, close in time and not composed for litigation.

The correct question is not “why did he omit it, therefore it did not happen?” but:

> **How much weight should a fact-finder place on the absence of the Protest from a detailed private narrative written in October 2017, when assessing the later retrospective patient account?**

That is an evidential question to preserve.

## 3.5 Withdrawal co-variation is independently preserved in private correspondence

During procyclidine withdrawal the patient writes that tension is building, his feet are restless and his walk is falling apart while his eyesight improves.

### Effect

This independently preserves the early treatment-state crossing of feet, gait and tension.

It strengthens the later reopening/maintenance question without proving that all focal foot pain was neurological.

A July 2022 prescription thread shows the patient identifying a PRN Sinemet instruction as coming from a Parkinson-service contact he called “Paddy Brown”. He says that contact separately notified the pharmacy while the GP practice was updating its own repeat prescription.

# 7. 22 March 2023 — direct patient safety language before later institutional translation

In an email seeking help for foot pain, the patient writes:

> **“When I say my feet are killing me I mean that literally. I'm losing the will to live with them at this stage.”**

He says a dosage change had worked for only a day or two and was no longer enough, and that he was available if anything could be done.

The GP practice replied within minutes checking whether he had contacted the GP.

### Evidential class

**Direct contemporaneous patient evidence of severe pain and death-associated language.**

### Significance

This matters because it exists before the later GP referral language about a “death wish”. It demonstrates that the safety concern did not originate solely in a clinician's later summary.

### Control

Do not automatically translate **“losing the will to live”** into a defined suicidal-intent finding. The correct proposition is severe pain with contemporaneous death-associated/psychologically dangerous language requiring appropriate contextual safety assessment.

### Effect on suspicion

Strengthens the proposition that by 2023 the unresolved pain had become a safety object, regardless of the eventual mechanism.

# 10. March 2025 — the patient's pre-complaint model was already mixed

Before the complaint escalated, the patient sent his GP a structured consultation brief about the foot problem.

The brief describes:

- localised sharp left-foot pain, pressure-sensitive and movement-aggravated;
- worse mornings and nights;
- numb sensation at times in the left leg;
- a possible neurological component through Parkinson-related pain perception;
- balance compensation and possible structural/mechanical loading;
- prior Neurontin/Lyrica/analgesic treatment;
- adjusted Sinemet timing;
- an existing prescription for **half a Sinemet CR at night if morning flares persisted**;
- questions about dopaminergic pain modulation, Podiatry/gait correction and alternative neuropathic treatment;
- severe psychological exhaustion and a request for a structured multidisciplinary plan.

### Evidential significance

This is extremely important to the life of the suspicion because it predates the later advocacy formulations.

The patient was not then asserting one settled causal mechanism. He was asking for neurological, structural and neuropathic possibilities to be integrated.

It also establishes that Sinemet CR was already within the treatment plan before later complaint formulations portrayed its eventual benefit as a wholly unconsidered intervention.

### Effect on suspicion

**Weakens a simple “Neurology never considered CR / only one mechanism was possible” theory.**

**Strengthens the narrower ownership question:** the patient was expressly asking for a multidisciplinary reconstruction of a mixed problem because he no longer experienced piecemeal management as sufficient.

# 13. Prof Timothy Counihan — April/May 2025 correspondence clarifies participation status

An original GUH Neurology letter dictated 28 April, typed 29 April and signed/approved 7 May 2025 states to the patient:

> **“As I have not been involved in your care…”**

and says the communication was forwarded to Dr Thomas Monaghan, with Brennan and Monaghan copied.

Dr Monaghan later told the Medical Council that Prof Counihan had advised him in 2017 when the patient's care was reviewed at the Neurology Department weekly Complex Case meeting, but had **no further involvement formally or otherwise** in management.

### Evidential treatment

Do not manufacture a contradiction from these statements.

A plausible distinction is:

- Counihan: no treating/management involvement in the patient's care;
- Monaghan: one-off colleague-to-colleague advice in a departmental complex-case discussion.

### What is now established more precisely

Counihan's relevant capability is not merely abstract departmental reputation. Monaghan's later account places Counihan's advice inside the patient's 2017 case discussion, specifically around caution in starting/escalating levodopa in a young patient.

What remains unproved is **bedside application**: whether Counihan personally examined the patient or whether his specialist expertise was applied to the unresolved painful-foot/dystonic lower-limb relationship.

The controlling rule remains:

> **capability ≠ participation ≠ bedside application.**

# 14. Dr Thomas Monaghan — 29 July 2025 Medical Council response as a separate retrospective evidential object

The original response supplied in the Medical Council correspondence is important because it is the treating neurologist's own later account of the eight-year history.

Among other matters, Dr Monaghan says:

- he believed his management was correct and appropriate but accepted he might be wrong;
- Parkinson's disease was central to his clinical consideration;
- levodopa was deliberately delayed/escalated cautiously because of dyskinesia and behavioural risk in a very young patient;
- other dopaminergic treatments had been used before Sinemet;
- Sinemet had been used since 2021 and its effect on pain was known;
- Sinemet CR was prescribed by the treating service in March 2025;
- Prof Counihan advised him in the 2017 weekly Complex Case meeting but had no further management role;
- Prof Daniel Healy was involved externally because of the patient's young age;
- he had usually seen the patient himself over the years;
- he relied on a working clinical record going back to 2017 when explaining the care;
- his draft Medical Council response began on 28 April 2025, months before the final dated response.

### Evidential class

**Clinician evidence / retrospective explanation / document-provenance evidence for the 2025 response itself.**

### What it strengthens

- same-clinician longitudinal custody becomes substantially more concrete;
- one-off Counihan participation in 2017 becomes clinician-authored evidence rather than mere departmental inference;
- real levodopa/dyskinesia concerns and prior specialist input strengthen innocent/defensive explanations;
- the 2025 response's own authorship chronology becomes partly known: drafting began in April.

### What it does not establish

The response is not independent confirmation of the eight-year clinical interpretation simply because it cites and describes an extensive longitudinal record.

If the case question is whether the founding framework influenced how later evidence was recorded and interpreted, the originating clinician's later synthesis of his own record is a distinct epistemic event.

### Effect on suspicion

**Weakens broad theories of no specialist input, no dopaminergic strategy or no longitudinal clinical reasoning.**

**Strengthens the precision of the retrospective-validation question:** how much of the later defence is new independent evidence and how much is interpretation of the record generated within the same clinician's care?

# 17. 27 August / 3 September 2025 — the actual SIMT process must be described accurately

The source-verified SIMT outcome records that a **Preliminary Assessment Report** concerning the patient's care was presented to SIMT on 27 August 2025.

SIMT used that PAR to conclude that care had been appropriate and that there was **“no deficit in the care provided.”** No further review was initiated.

### Correct process formulation

> **PAR prepared → PAR presented to SIMT → SIMT threshold/outcome decision that no further review was required.**

Do not casually describe this as a full independent re-investigation unless the underlying PAR and evidence genealogy establish that level of work.

### Effect on suspicion

The decision is real contrary institutional evidence.

Its weight on the construction/maintenance hypotheses depends on what the PAR actually examined: founding source evidence, Protest evidence, authorship chronology, later contradictions, clinician explanation, independent specialist input, or inherited summaries.

That question is now the object of the 31 August 2026 Article 15 provenance request.

# 22. What Gmail changes in the case

The correspondence review changes the evidential picture in several concrete ways.

## It weakens some propositions

- the patient strongly accepted the Parkinsonian diagnosis and treatment in 2017;
- `burning` was unquestionably part of the patient's own vocabulary by October 2017;
- the patient himself rapidly came to interpret his feet through Parkinsonism after diagnosis;
- substantial treatment benefit and toxicity were contemporaneously real;
- Sinemet CR was already inside the March 2025 clinical plan before later dramatic response;
- Counihan's 2017 role appears narrower than later advocacy formulations suggested: one-off advisory participation, not established ongoing management;
- “no reassessment” is too broad; the record and correspondence show substantial ongoing clinical activity.

## It strengthens or sharpens others

- the 2017 withdrawal feet–gait–tension co-variation is independently preserved in private correspondence;
- severe 2023 pain and death-associated language are directly preserved in the patient's own contemporaneous email;
- the patient was asking for integrated/multidisciplinary reconstruction before the 2025 complaint became highly theorised;
- same-clinician custody is supported by Monaghan's own later account that he usually saw the patient himself over the eight years;
- the PAR/SIMT process can now be accurately characterised as preliminary-assessment-to-threshold decision rather than assumed full re-investigation;
- Article 18, later SIMT use, and January 2026 continued reliance form a concrete data-processing chronology;
- current Article 15/16 requests are live evidence-acquisition/corrigibility tests rather than background bureaucracy.

## It creates new questions

- if `burning` was patient language by October 2017, when and from whom did it enter the earlier July synthesis?
- what evidential significance should attach to the absence of the Protest from the detailed October private narrative?
- how did the patient's own causal model move from feet-as-cause, to feet-as-Parkinsonism, to later separate/neuropathic understandings, and then toward the present mixed/integrated theory?
- what did the 2025 PAR actually examine?
- what part of later institutional confidence was independently reconstructed and what part descended from the clinical information state already in existence?

# 23. Search discipline and negative findings

The Gmail review used date-bounded and case-term searches across the longitudinal period, supplemented by reading complete threads and source attachments where a result was material.

The relative absence of case-specific email in some years must not be overstated.

A mailbox is not the clinical record. Relevant communication may exist in:

- hospital clinical systems;
- letters;
- GP systems;
- telephone notes;
- pharmacy records;
- correspondence accounts not searched here;
- records no longer retained.

The correct use of Gmail is therefore:

> **additional time-stamped witness and information-flow evidence, not a substitute chronology.**
