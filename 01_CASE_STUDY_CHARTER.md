# 01 — Case Study Charter

## Declaration of purpose

This repository is now the working home of a developing case study.

It has two simultaneous jobs:

1. **to establish what happened in this patient's care with the highest documentary precision available; and**
2. **to preserve the process by which that understanding is developed, challenged, corrected, submitted for external scrutiny and either sustained or defeated.**

The second job matters. This repository is not intended to become a polished retrospective narrative that hides how the formulation was reached. The evolution of the hypothesis, source corrections, competing explanations, expert challenges, submission strategies, institutional responses and changes of mind are themselves part of the case study.

The repository therefore studies both:

> **the clinical/documentary case**

and

> **the making, testing and external examination of the case.**

---

## Governing question

The present case-study question is:

> **Can an authoritative specialist record convert an unresolved clinical problem into a portable explanatory state, shape the later production of clinical knowledge around that state, become increasingly resistant to correction because its downstream effects are treated as confirmation, and thereby create documentary success while patient-centred clinical failure remains insufficiently visible?**

The patient's strongest present suspicion is narrower and more serious:

> **After a pre-discharge challenge that the feet had not been dealt with, did the later document-construction process introduce or materially reformulate symptom language and a peripheral-neuropathy route in a way that achieved documentary closure, supplied an authoritative explanation to the GP and later clinicians, and altered the informational environment of the next eight years of care?**

That suspicion is to be tested at full strength. It is not to be weakened merely because a weaker allegation is easier to defend or submit.

Equally, it is not to be promoted from suspicion to fact without the evidence required for that transition.

The governing discipline is:

> **Do not protect the institution from the hypothesis. Do not protect the patient from contrary evidence.**

---

## What makes this a case study rather than a complaint file

A complaint asks whether a person or service did something wrong.

This case study asks a larger set of questions:

- what the patient originally brought to care;
- what the GP translated that problem into;
- what Neurology actually examined and reasoned about at the bedside;
- what appeared only in later synthesis;
- what the final portable record communicated to downstream readers;
- where clinically consequential terminology came from;
- what information later clinicians inherited rather than independently established;
- how hypotheses gained, retained or lost authority over time;
- whether contradictory evidence caused local adjustment or global reconstruction;
- how the patient's own understanding was shaped by professional terminology;
- whether the record became a source of endogenous validation;
- how complaint and governance review later interpreted the history;
- what would count as genuine error correction;
- what ethical, clinical, legal, regulatory, data-governance and patient-safety frameworks are capable of recognising the phenomenon;
- and what happens when the patient attempts to make the system see a failure that the record itself can narrate as success.

The case study is therefore clinical, documentary, epistemic, ethical, institutional and legal. No one discipline is entitled to collapse all the others into its own vocabulary.

---

## The patient-centred anchor

The study must never become so sophisticated that it loses the original clinical object.

The patient did not enter care because he had already formulated PRKN, dystonia, neuropathy, epistemic injustice or documentary path dependence.

He entered because his feet hurt, his gait and lower limbs were abnormal, and his GP had brought those facts together for specialist neurological assessment.

The patient-centred test throughout the study is:

> **What happened to the problem for which the patient actually sought help?**

The patient's pre-discharge Protest, if accepted as factual in a given analytical exercise, is an especially important error signal because it states the problem in its simplest form:

> **You have not dealt with my feet.**

The patient did not need to know the eventual diagnostic explanation in order to know whether the practical problem had been dealt with.

---

## The two temporal chronologies

Every serious analysis must distinguish:

### 1. Clinical-event chronology

What happened to the patient, in what order, and what information was available at each moment.

### 2. Document-authorship chronology

When each consequential sentence, phrase, inference, addendum and synthesis element was actually created, revised, finalised and transmitted.

The finished order of paragraphs is not evidence of authorship order.

The July 2017 document is a composite product. Its final sophistication must not be projected backward into the bedside period unless source evidence establishes that the reasoning already existed then.

The highest-priority unresolved documentary question remains:

> **When did “leg pain”, “burning”, the possible secondary peripheral-neuropathy branch, the alcohol hypothesis, “Not clear”, and the later high-resolution genetic reasoning first enter the record, from what source, and in whose authorship?**

---

## The present mechanism under examination

The case study presently examines a possible two-sided mechanism.

### Side A — explanatory disposal

An unresolved painful-foot problem may have acquired portable alternative routes:

> **structural/mechanical feet → Podiatry / GP**

and

> **burning / leg pain / possible secondary peripheral neuropathy → neuropathy-directed interpretation, investigation and treatment.**

This could allow Parkinsonism and the dystonic-looking lower limb to remain a high-resolution neurological project while the painful foot attached to the same lower-limb presentation became capable of travelling elsewhere.

### Side B — authority production

The final specialist account became highly sophisticated in young-onset Parkinsonism, recessive genetics, PRKN/PARK2 and rarer monogenic possibilities.

The case-study question is whether that sophistication:

- was returned to the unresolved bedside relationship and used to clarify it; or
- instead supplied epistemic authority to a document capable of making incomplete relational work look like medically earned uncertainty.

The strongest current expression is:

> **The sophistication of the synthesis may have been used not to resolve the incompleteness exposed by the Protest, but to make that incompleteness portable.**

This is a hypothesis for evidence, expert scrutiny and external challenge.

---

## The longitudinal mechanism

The study does not stop in July 2017.

It asks what the final information state did after it entered ordinary care.

The current longitudinal hypothesis is:

> **founding specialist classification → inherited information state → later care conducted inside that state → terminology, referrals, investigations and treatments generated within it → patient adoption of parts of its vocabulary → contrary neurological observations absorbed locally → accumulated record treated as evidence of continuity and reasonableness → complaint answered partly through the historical record generated inside the original frame.**

Key concepts presently under examination include:

- clinical path dependence;
- diagnostic momentum;
- false or uncertain provenance of symptom language;
- authoritative uncertainty;
- endogenous validation;
- institutional self-confirmation;
- epistemic closure;
- asymmetric falsifiability;
- local updating / global non-revision;
- retrospective narrative smoothing;
- process–outcome displacement;
- documentary success masking clinical failure;
- patient testimony as a longitudinal error signal;
- clinical epistemic-governance failure.

These are analytical tools, not conclusions merely because they have names.

## What counts as success in this repository

The repository must not measure success by whether the patient's suspicion is vindicated.

It succeeds if it produces a record from which a serious external reader can determine:

- what is directly proved;
- what the patient says happened;
- what clinicians recorded;
- what is inferred;
- what is disputed;
- what requires expert medical evidence;
- what requires documentary/forensic evidence;
- what is an ethical question;
- what is a legal question;
- what would falsify each important hypothesis;
- and how the formulation changed when new evidence arrived.

A hypothesis that survives because contrary evidence was hidden from the analysis is a failure of this repository.

A hypothesis that is abandoned because the evidence defeats it is a success of this repository.

---

## Evidence classes

Every major proposition should, where practicable, be tagged or written so its status is obvious:

- **DOCUMENTARY FACT** — directly established by the available record;
- **PATIENT EVIDENCE** — the patient's recollection, testimony or contemporaneous account;
- **CLINICIAN EVIDENCE** — clinician-authored observation, recollection or explanation;
- **REASONABLE INFERENCE** — an inference drawn from established material;
- **DISPUTED FACT** — a proposition whose factual status is contested or uncorroborated;
- **EXPERT-DEPENDENT MEDICAL PROPOSITION** — requires appropriate independent clinical expertise;
- **DOCUMENTARY/FORENSIC QUESTION** — requires native files, metadata, audit trail, version history or authorship evidence;
- **ETHICAL QUESTION/OPINION** — requires ethical analysis and does not itself establish legal liability;
- **LEGAL QUESTION/OPINION** — requires the applicable law and legal expertise;
- **UNSUPPORTED ALLEGATION** — a proposition presently lacking a sufficient evidential foundation.

Serious allegations about intention, concealment, manipulation, dishonesty or record interference must never be laundered into documentary fact by repetition.

They may nevertheless be stated clearly as suspicions and tested directly.

---

## Source hierarchy

1. Original/native source document, image, system entry, metadata or audit trail where available.
2. Verified source-to-transcription corrections.
3. `REPAIRED_CANONICAL_V2.md` as the present controlling working transcription where no discrepancy has been identified.
4. Contemporaneous patient and clinician communications.
5. Focused evidence maps and audits.
6. Integrated formulations.
7. Expert analyses and external opinions.
8. Submission documents and advocacy narratives.

No downstream formulation is allowed to overrule a verified primary source.

---

## Workstreams

The case study should now develop through distinct but connected workstreams.

### A. Source and provenance reconstruction

Native documents, authorship, timestamps, amendments, addenda, audit trails, dictation/transcription history, wording provenance and exact versions sent to downstream recipients.

### B. Clinical reconstruction

What was examined, what findings meant at the time, what reasonable movement-disorders practice required, what later treatment-state observations changed, and what mechanism remained mixed or unresolved.

### C. Information-flow reconstruction

What each later clinician actually inherited, what was independently observed, what terminology was copied or adopted, what propositions gained practical authority, and what caused or failed to cause revision.

### D. Ethics and patient-safety analysis

Record integrity, candour, professional authority, epistemic status, patient testimony, diagnostic momentum, error correction, suffering, safeguarding and the ethics of downstream reliance.

### E. Legal and regulatory analysis

Potential duties, breach, causation, record accuracy, disclosure, complaint handling, professional conduct and the evidential significance of later review.

### F. Institutional response analysis

How complaints, reviews, correspondence and governance processes characterise the history; whether they independently test the patient's allegation or rely on the same inherited record; and whether a response itself becomes additional case-study evidence.

### G. External case-study development

Independent experts, regulators, patient-safety bodies, academic/ethics readers and other appropriate external audiences should receive packages tailored to the question they are competent to answer.

---

## Submission principle

There will not be one universal complaint document sent unchanged everywhere.

Each external body should receive a case framed around its actual competence.

Examples:

- a movement-disorders expert receives the clinical/examination/reopening questions;
- an ethics expert receives record integrity, epistemic status, path dependence, patient testimony and professional authority;
- a data-protection route receives provenance, accuracy, source and rectification questions;
- a professional regulator receives individual conduct and record-integrity questions;
- a patient-safety or quality body receives propagation, error correction and system-learning questions;
- an Ombudsman-type reviewer receives fairness and adequacy of complaint investigation rather than being asked to decide specialist neurology;
- legal counsel receives breach, causation, limitation, disclosure and evidential architecture;
- an academic case-study reader receives both the event and the process by which the event was reconstructed and tested.

The submission architecture is maintained separately so that one body is not asked to answer a question outside its remit.

## The process itself is evidence for the case study

From this point onward, the repository should deliberately preserve:

- material hypothesis changes;
- corrections prompted by primary sources;
- major defence propositions;
- expert opinions that weaken the patient's case;
- expert opinions that strengthen it;
- unanswered questions;
- rejected formulations and why they were rejected;
- source requests and whether they were satisfied;
- each external submission;
- each external response;
- whether the response addressed the allegation actually made;
- whether a response relied on inherited records as independent validation;
- and how the controlling formulation changed afterwards.

The case study is therefore recursive:

> **the healthcare record is being studied as an information system, and the process of challenging that information system must itself be documented transparently enough to be studied.**

---

## Required challenge discipline

Every mature version of the case must include its strongest possible failure modes.

At minimum:

- genuine structural foot disease may explain substantial pain;
- peripheral neuropathy may have been a reasonable differential;
- the patient historically distinguished foot pain from leg tension;
- substantial neurological work occurred during the admission;
- undocumented examination may have occurred;
- disputed terminology may derive from unpreserved but legitimate clinical elicitation;
- the Protest may lack independent corroboration;
- later treatment response does not identify one exclusive historical mechanism;
- later clinicians did genuinely reassess individual phenomena;
- pregabalin benefit is real but not mechanism-specific;
- dyskinesia and behavioural risks legitimately constrained dopaminergic treatment;
- later genetics cannot determine the 2017 standard;
- a sophisticated final synthesis may reflect ordinary retrospective clinical reasoning rather than improper documentary construction;
- continuity may represent legitimate care rather than path dependence.

The case study must become stronger by surviving these propositions, not by suppressing them.

## Development record

`09_CASE_STUDY_DEVELOPMENT_LOG.md` records major changes in the case-study formulation from the point of this charter onward.

It should not become a diary of every conversation. Entries should be made when something materially changes:

- a new source is found;
- a prior factual premise is corrected;
- a major hypothesis is introduced, strengthened, weakened or abandoned;
- an expert opinion changes the architecture;
- a submission is made;
- a response produces new evidence or reveals a new institutional mechanism.

---

## End state

There is no requirement that the case study end with a finding against a clinician or institution.

Its proper end state is a transparent, externally examinable account of:

> **what happened; what may have happened; what can be proved; what cannot; how an information architecture affected care, if it did; how the patient challenged it; how external systems responded; and what the case teaches about clinical records, specialist authority, error correction and patient-centred care.**

That is the job of this repository from this point forward.
