# 13 — Live Data-Governance and Provenance Track

## Status and purpose

**Active regulatory/data-governance workstream.**

This file records data-protection activity only insofar as it bears on the evidential case, provenance, historical completeness, information-state corrigibility or the independence of later review.

It is not a substitute for legal advice on GDPR. It does not assume that a request is legally well-founded because it was made, and it does not treat a controller's or regulator's response as proof of the underlying clinical case.

The central distinction is:

> **What was the historical document made from?**

and

> **What information should accompany that historical document when it is processed now?**

Those are different questions.

---

# 1. Why this belongs inside the case study

The repository examines whether an authoritative specialist information state was constructed, maintained and later relied upon.

Data-protection activity now tests that architecture in the real world. It may produce evidence about authorship time, source provenance, versions, audit history, amendment/finalisation, the evidence genealogy of PAR/SIMT, restriction/accuracy markers, supplementary patient information, recipients and current processing.

This is **external testing of the information system**, not decorative regulatory correspondence.

- that every alleged incompleteness is legally unrectifiable;

# 5. 4 August 2025 — Article 18 restriction request before the PAR/SIMT decision

The patient's 4 August 2025 correspondence invoked Article 18 in connection with disputed accuracy and asked that disputed material be restricted/marked, including concern about use within the PAR/SIMT process.

The later governance chronology is source-verified as:

> **4 August Article 18 request → PAR prepared → PAR presented to SIMT on 27 August → SIMT outcome communicated 3 September.**

### What this proves

The accuracy/restriction dispute existed before the SIMT decision.

### What this does not prove

It does not by itself prove that later processing was unlawful or that Article 18 legally prohibited the PAR/SIMT use. That requires the actual controller response, implementation facts and applicable law.

### Evidential questions

- Was a restriction or accuracy marker placed on the relevant data?
- What processing continued?
- What data entered the PAR?
- Did the reviewers know the data were disputed?
- Was the dispute represented neutrally or only through the existing record?

### Data-governance significance

That does not decide whether the processing was lawful. It makes the provenance and restriction history factually important.

# 10. 2 September 2026 — Article 16 supplementary-statement request

**Status:** submitted; no substantive controller response identified as of 11 September 2026.

The patient asks that a supplementary statement be durably attached to or directly cross-referenced with the July 2017 Neurology synthesis so that its existence becomes apparent when the synthesis is later accessed, reviewed, relied upon, disclosed or otherwise processed.

The patient does **not** ask GUH to delete the historical record, alter the historical diagnosis, rewrite the clinician's reasoning or accept the patient's clinical interpretation as established fact.

The request is instead to preserve the patient's competing account alongside the clinician-authored synthesis.

---

# 11. Content and evidential status of the supplementary statement

The statement gives the patient's retrospective account that on Monday 10 July 2017, after learning discharge was planned for the following day:

- four medical students were present;
- he asked them to get Dr Thomas Monaghan because he needed to speak about his feet;
- Monaghan attended and the curtain was drawn;
- the patient used the NCT / bald-tyres analogy to object to discharge without the foot problem having been dealt with;
- he recalls Monaghan replying in substance that it was as easy for the patient to obtain a Podiatry appointment as for him;
- the patient does not recall a further focused neurological reassessment of the foot/lower-limb relationship before discharge.

### Evidential classification

This is:

- direct patient evidence of recollection;
- retrospective evidence about a contemporaneous event;
- not contemporaneous documentary proof;
- not a clinical expert opinion;
- not a legal finding of incompleteness merely because the patient asserts it.

The absence of the Protest from the surviving specialist record is a separate documentary fact.

---

# 12. Why the supplementary-statement request matters

The Article 16 request does not itself prove the Protest or improper documentary closure.

It asks the information system to do something methodologically precise:

> **preserve the clinician-authored historical record while making the patient's competing account visible during future processing.**

That is a live **corrigibility/completeness intervention**.

The controller's response can therefore create new evidence about how the system handles contested historical completeness without rewriting history.

# 16. Relationship to the case hypothesis

Article 15 Object A may reveal source and timing of the July synthesis, including whether consequential wording existed before or after the Protest.

Historic accuracy/restriction activity and present completion requests test whether an established information state can be qualified, corrected, supplemented or restricted after dispute arises.

## Retrospective validation

Article 15 Object B may show what the PAR actually examined and whether the SIMT outcome was built from independent reconstruction or substantially from inherited material.

## Corrigibility

The September 2026 Article 16 request tests whether a competing patient account can travel with the historical synthesis without erasing the clinician's original record.

# 17. Required discipline

For every new DPO/DPC event record:

> **date → actor → right/process → exact information object → response → supplied/withheld/not-held status → what it proves → what it does not prove → effect on construction/maintenance/validation/corrigibility.**

Do not describe silence as refusal before the applicable period has expired.

Do not treat refusal as bad faith merely because it is adverse.

Do not treat regulatory acknowledgement as substantive agreement.

Do not treat Article 15 data as self-interpreting clinical evidence.

Do not treat Article 16 supplementation as retroactive rewriting unless that is what actually occurs.

Do not merge University of Galway and HSE/GUH controller activity.
