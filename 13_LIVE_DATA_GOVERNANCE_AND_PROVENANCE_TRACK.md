# 13 — Live Data-Governance and Provenance Track

## Status and purpose

**Active regulatory/data-governance workstream.**

This file records data-protection activity only insofar as it bears on the evidential case, provenance, historical completeness, information-state corrigibility or the independence of later review.

It is not a substitute for legal advice on GDPR. It does not assume that any request is legally well-founded merely because it has been made, and it does not treat a controller's or regulator's response as proving the underlying clinical case.

The central distinction is:

> **What was the historical document made from?**

and

> **What information should accompany that historical document when it is processed now?**

Those are different questions.

---

# 1. Why this workstream belongs inside the case study

The repository already examines whether an authoritative specialist information state was constructed, maintained and later relied upon.

The present data-governance activity now tests that architecture directly in the real world.

It may produce evidence relevant to:

- authorship time;
- source provenance;
- version history;
- audit history;
- documentary amendment/finalisation;
- the evidence genealogy of PAR/SIMT review;
- whether an accuracy dispute or restriction travelled with the data;
- whether the patient's competing historical account can be durably associated with the clinician-authored record;
- how a health-service controller understands historical accuracy, completeness and continuity;
- how the DPC frames and assesses those questions.

The activity therefore belongs in the case study as **external testing of the information system**, not as decorative regulatory correspondence.

# 4. 31 August 2026 — new Article 15 provenance request to GUH DPO

**Status:** SUBMITTED / awaiting controller response as of 11 September 2026.

**Recipient:** GUH Data Protection Officer.

**Nature of request:** a new, specifically confined Article 15 access request directed to two identified bodies of processing.

## Object A — July 2017 Neurology discharge / clinical synthesis

The request seeks personal data capable of revealing how the July 2017 specialist information object was:

- created;
- dictated;
- transcribed;
- revised;
- amended;
- finalised;
- confirmed;
- signed off;
- distributed;
- sourced from underlying material.

It seeks, where retained, document IDs, creation/modification timestamps, earlier versions, change histories, dictation/transcription workflow data, audit events, source/provenance data, 13 July addendum history, 17 July confirmation/finalisation history and distribution/recipient information.

It also asks specifically for available source/provenance data relating to consequential patient-specific formulations, including the historical foot/leg distinction, statements about the admission and plan, Podiatry disposition and the formal recommendation structure.

### Evidential significance

This request directly operationalises the repository's highest-priority **document-authorship chronology** question.

If responsive data identify when consequential wording first existed and what source supplied it, they can materially strengthen or weaken the construction hypothesis.

A non-holding response is not the same thing as evidence that the events did not occur. The response must distinguish:

- data held and supplied;
- data held but withheld in whole or part;
- data not held;
- data no longer held.

## Object B — 2025 PAR / SIMT review

The request seeks personal data showing how the PAR and SIMT review were assembled, including where retained:

- the completed PAR;
- drafts and revision history;
- terms of reference/review questions;
- source lists/evidence indices;
- clinical and complaint materials reviewed;
- clinician/QPS inputs;
- interviews/statements;
- independent clinical input;
- communications materially affecting the review;
- SIMT papers, agenda entries, presentation material, minutes/notes and conclusions;
- reviewer roles;
- audit/process history;
- recipients;
- any accuracy-dispute, restriction or Article 18 markers present during processing.

### Evidential significance

This request directly tests the **retrospective-validation** limb.

The key question is:

> **Did PAR/SIMT independently reconstruct the underlying evidence and contradictions, or did it substantially inherit the accumulated information state it was being asked to evaluate?**

The answer cannot be assumed from the mere existence of a review or its conclusion.

# 5. 2 September 2026 — Article 16 supplementary-statement request

**Status:** SUBMITTED / awaiting controller response as of 11 September 2026.

**Recipient:** GUH Data Protection Officer.

**Purpose:** completion of the July 2017 Neurology record by means of a supplementary patient statement, without deletion or rewriting of the original clinician-authored record.

The request asks that the supplementary statement be durably attached to, or directly cross-referenced with, the July 2017 Neurology synthesis so that its existence becomes apparent whenever the historical synthesis is subsequently accessed, reviewed, relied upon, disclosed or otherwise processed.

The patient expressly does **not** ask GUH to:

- delete the original record;
- alter the historical diagnosis;
- rewrite the clinician's reasoning;
- accept the patient's clinical interpretation as established fact.

The request instead asks that the patient's own account of a material pre-discharge interaction survive alongside the clinician-authored synthesis.

---

# 6. Content and evidential status of the supplementary statement

The attached statement is dated 2 September 2026.

It records the patient's retrospective account that on Monday 10 July 2017, after learning he was to be discharged the following day:

- four medical students were present at the bedside;
- he asked them collectively to get Dr Thomas Monaghan because he needed to speak to him about his feet;
- Dr Monaghan attended;
- the curtain was drawn;
- the students, Dr Monaghan and the patient were present;
- the patient used the NCT / bald-tyres analogy to object to being discharged without the foot problem having been adequately dealt with;
- he recalls Dr Monaghan replying in substance that it was as easy for the patient to obtain a Podiatry appointment as for him;
- the exchange ended;
- the patient does not recall a further focused neurological examination or reassessment of the foot/lower-limb relationship before discharge.

The statement also explains why the patient considers the existing specialist account incomplete: the surviving synthesis records extensive material about his understanding, discussions, debriefing, clear plan and foot disposition, while not recording this alleged pre-discharge challenge.

## Evidential classification

The supplementary statement is:

- **direct patient evidence** of the patient's recollection;
- **retrospective evidence about a contemporaneous event**;
- **not contemporaneous documentary proof that the exchange occurred**;
- **not a clinical expert opinion**;
- **not a finding that the original record was legally incomplete under Article 16 merely because the patient asserts it**.

The absence of the Protest from the surviving specialist record is a separate documentary fact.

The legal significance of completion by supplementary statement remains a data-protection question for the controller, regulator and, where necessary, legal determination.

---

# 7. Why the Article 16 request matters to the life of the suspicion

The Article 16 request does not itself prove the Protest or prove improper documentary closure.

Its significance is methodological and empirical.

The case study has long asked whether the patient's contemporaneous challenge disappeared from the portable information state while the specialist synthesis travelled forward.

The present request now asks the information system to do something precise:

> **preserve the clinician-authored historical record intact, but make the patient's competing account visible alongside it during future processing.**

That is an attempted **corrigibility intervention**.

The response can therefore generate new case-study evidence about how the institution understands historical completeness and how capable the record system is of carrying competing provenance-aware information states.

# 11. Relationship to the three-stage suspicion

## Construction

The Article 15 Object A request may reveal the source and timing of the final July synthesis, including whether consequential wording pre-dated or post-dated the Protest.

## Maintenance / continuing stewardship

Historic Article 16/18 activity and current completion requests may show whether the information state could be corrected, qualified, restricted or supplemented once its accuracy/completeness was disputed.

## Retrospective validation

Article 15 Object B may reveal what PAR/SIMT actually reviewed, what propositions were independently established, what were inherited, and whether accuracy/restriction warnings accompanied the information during review.

## Corrigibility

The September 2026 Article 16 request now tests whether the patient's account can travel with the historical synthesis without erasing the clinician's original record.

# 12. Required discipline for future updates

For every new DPO/DPC event, record:

> **date → controller/regulator → right/process invoked → exact object of processing → response → what was supplied/withheld/not held → what the event proves → what it does not prove → effect on construction/maintenance/validation/corrigibility.**

Do not describe silence as refusal before a response period has actually expired.

Do not describe a refusal as proof of bad faith merely because it is adverse.

Do not treat regulatory acknowledgement as substantive agreement.

Do not treat an Article 15 response as proof of the clinical meaning of the data it discloses.

Do not treat Article 16 supplementation as retroactive alteration of the historical clinician-authored record unless that is what the controller actually does.

Do not merge University of Galway and HSE/GUH controller activity.

# 13. The new case-study sequence

The regulatory/data-governance layer extends the repository's existing sequence:

> **integrated presentation → inpatient evidence → Protest evidence → document construction → portable specialist information state → same-clinician longitudinal custody → boundary-crossing observations → local updating/global reconstruction question → severe pain/safety endpoint → complaint and institutional review → patient documentary discovery → provenance/completeness challenge → controller/regulator response → new evidence about the corrigibility and genealogy of the information system.**

This is not mission drift.

It is the information-system hypothesis becoming externally testable.
