# The Neurology Neuropathy Illusion

## Reading constraints — how this record must be read

This document cannot be read safely as a simple chronological sequence in which each later clinician encounters the patient afresh. The central analytical task is to distinguish **new patient evidence** from **information inherited through an earlier specialist frame**. Later notes may be clinically sincere and locally reasonable while still operating inside a management architecture created earlier.

The following constraints govern the reading of the whole file:

1. **Start with the integrated referral state.** The patient entered Neurology through painful abnormal feet + abnormal gait + pathological tone/movement + functional deterioration. Do not begin from a retrospective picture of “Parkinson's plus a separate foot complaint.”
2. **Separate bedside examination from later synthesis.** The completed 2017 discharge product is substantially more sophisticated in young-onset/recessive Parkinsonian and PRKN/PARK2 reasoning than the surviving documented neurological examination of the feet. Do not assume that sophistication in the final synthesis proves equivalent phenotype-directed foot examination occurred.
3. **Respect the temporal firewall.** Do not project the final PRKN-rich synthesis backwards into the inpatient period; do not project post-discharge treatment response backwards into the Protest; do not use later PRKN/Parkin clarification to rewrite what was knowable in July 2017.
4. **Treat the Protest as experiential notice.** The asserted pre-discharge complaint is, in substance, “you have not dealt with my feet.” It is not a retrospective critique of PRKN, neuropathy, “Not clear,” or the later discharge wording.
5. **Track provenance, not just vocabulary.** The 2017 surviving patient history does not show the patient originating the Neurology-recorded “burning” descriptor. Its first identified July appearance is inside Neurology's interpretive/etiological paragraph alongside the possible-neuropathy formulation; its underlying source remains unresolved. By 2023 the patient is using the broader terms “peripheral neuropathy” and “neuropathic pain.” Do not count later recycled or descendant language automatically as independent confirmation of the original hypothesis.
6. **Distinguish structural pathology from structural independence.** Corns, callus, fifth-MTPJ pathology, pes planus and hammertoes are real. Their reality does not by itself establish that abnormal neurological posture, tone, dystonia or gait played no part in loading, recurrence or pain.
7. **Read medication changes as treatment-state evidence.** Procyclidine withdrawal, pregabalin exposure, ordinary Sinemet, Stalevo and Sinemet CR do not by themselves prove etiology. They do generate patient-specific information capable of supporting, destabilising or reopening causal hypotheses.
8. **Do not confuse local updating with global reconstruction.** A foot throw becoming Parkinsonian, OFF-dystonia being considered, pain/shuffling being called OFF-related, or levodopa being acknowledged to improve pain are local crossings of the original boundary. The stronger question is whether the original feet–movement relationship was ever globally rebuilt.
9. **Compare patient/evidence change with frame change.** The dominant longitudinal test is management-frame invariance: how much did the patient, treatment state, psychological state and evidence change compared with how much the governing causal architecture changed?
10. **Read “Not clear” differently depending on who is reading it.** For the author of the specialist synthesis, it can operate as a mechanism permitting closure while uncertainty remains. For downstream clinicians, nurses, podiatrists, GPs and the patient, it becomes an inherited information state backed by specialist authority. They inherit the conclusion, not necessarily the examination or reasoning that produced it.
11. **Do not treat repeated descendants as independent corroboration.** Possible neuropathy → neuropathic terminology → neuropathic treatment → patient adoption of neuropathy language → later “? neuropathic pain” → Fabry overlap testing may represent a path-dependent chain. Determine which links are genuinely new observations and which are descendants of the original classification.
12. **Test falsifiability asymmetrically.** Ask not only what supported neuropathy, but what evidence would have been capable of reducing or displacing it. A hypothesis that can gain practical force from supportive evidence while every contrary observation is redescribed as compatible with it may become functionally difficult to falsify.
13. **Treat 2023 as both a current safety object and a historical reconstruction test.** Severe foot/lower-leg pain, high-dose pregabalin, mobility restriction and serious psychological-endurance language require contemporaneous reading; separately, ask whether the reopening reconstructed the original feet–gait–tone genealogy or merely managed current branches.
14. **Read later rebuttals as evidence of the mature explanatory architecture, not automatically as proof that the earlier architecture was sound.** The 2025 responses are important because they show how the historical frame is retrospectively defended: Parkinson's remains central, neuropathy remains non-definitive but reasonable, and levodopa response is treated as compatible with pain “of whatever cause.”
15. **Separate function from intent.** This file may identify a self-preserving documentary or management mechanism without proving that any individual consciously designed it, acted dishonestly, or intended harm. Motive is a different evidential question.
16. **Use the later PRKN/Parkin result correctly.** Later independent genetic clarification changes the retrospective biological understanding of the relationship between Parkinsonism and dystonia. It does not prove what clinicians were required to know in 2017, but it means a present-day analyst should not read the early dystonic-looking lower-limb findings as biologically meaningless coincidence.
17. **Separate clinical chronology from document-authorship chronology.** The completed July specialist product is composite and contains material generated at different stages. Paragraph position in the finished document does not establish when a phrase was first written, by whom, or from what source.
18. **Do not confuse first identified appearance with first historical utterance.** A term may first appear in the surviving final synthesis even if it arose earlier in unpreserved bedside discussion. Conversely, later placement inside a composite document can create the appearance that wording was contemporaneous when it may have been added during synthesis. Source timing must be established.
19. **Treat the Protest-to-document-construction link as a live causal hypothesis, not an established fact.** The patient's suspicion is that the Protest exposed an unfinished foot problem and that the later finalisation process may then have supplied a documentary route for closure. That becomes materially stronger only if provenance evidence shows the consequential wording or reasoning was introduced or materially reformulated after the Protest.
20. **Test selective deployment of sophistication.** The relevant comparison is not merely how sophisticated the final document was, but where that sophistication was applied. The record can be highly sophisticated in young-onset genetics while comparatively thin in relational examination of the painful foot attached to the dystonic-looking limb. Ask what the available expertise did for the unresolved bedside problem.
21. **Distinguish clinical error from misrepresentation of epistemic status.** A wrong or incomplete hypothesis is one category. A record that knowingly makes “we have not adequately worked this out” look like “we have medically considered and safely disposed of this” would be a qualitatively different ethical problem involving record integrity and documentary authority. This stronger proposition is conditional on proof of knowledge and construction history.
22. **Trace upstream informational responsibility separately from downstream good-faith care.** Later clinicians may act conscientiously while inheriting an authoritative information state. If an originating specialist disposition was materially misleading, later good-faith reliance would not erase the distinctive upstream role of the original document; equally, downstream clinicians retain independent duties to reassess when the clinical picture changes.
23. **Preserve the decisive bifurcation test.** If the Neurology-recorded “burning” descriptor, “leg pain,” and the possible-neuropathy branch are shown to pre-date the Protest and derive from identifiable clinical evidence, the intentional-closure suspicion weakens. If they first appear only after the Protest, lack an identifiable patient/examination source, solve the exact problem raised by the Protest, and then propagate as historical clinical context, the suspicion becomes substantially stronger.
24. **Do not equate documentary coherence with evidential confirmation.** A longitudinal record can be coherent because a model is repeatedly confirmed, or because later information is repeatedly assimilated into a flexible inherited model. The correct test is what observations should have reduced the model's authority and whether they actually did.
25. **Distinguish robustness under testing from resistance to testing.** A hypothesis that survives independent challenge is robust. A hypothesis that survives because every contradiction can be reclassified elsewhere is resistant to testing. From the outside those states can look similar; the error-correction history distinguishes them.
26. **Treat the record as a possible source of endogenous validation.** If the founding classification shaped later terminology, referrals, treatments, investigations and patient self-understanding, then the accumulated record is not wholly external evidence about that classification. The system may partly produce the evidence by which it later judges itself.
27. **Keep process goods subordinate to patient-centred clinical ends.** Continuity, documentation, coherent differentials, referral completion and defensible reasoning are genuine goods, but they are instrumental. Their success cannot substitute for the question whether the patient's principal problem became better understood, safer and less burdensome.
28. **Preserve the patient as a longitudinal error signal.** The patient's repeated claim that the feet remained the unresolved practical problem may be clinically imperfect but can still function as a corrective signal against a professional architecture. Do not reduce persistent patient testimony to additional data points processed only inside the existing frame.
29. **Look for retrospective narrative smoothing.** A truthful longitudinal record should be capable of recording that an earlier assumption weakened, a treatment failed, an unresolved question remained, or a formulation changed. A seamless narrative in which every later event appears always to have belonged to a sensible continuous plan may conceal genuine discontinuity in learning.
30. **Do not use absence of correction as evidence that correction was unnecessary.** If downstream clinicians reasonably inherited the same architecture, the absence of formal repudiation may reflect propagation rather than independent validation. “Nobody corrected it” is not an independent answer to a path-dependence allegation.
31. **Separate institutional epistemic success from patient-centred clinical success.** The system may succeed at continuity, intelligibility and professional defensibility while the patient's central problem remains unresolved. The ethical question is whether those two forms of success diverged, and whether the informational success helped make the clinical failure harder to see.
32. **Recognise the possible transition from diagnostic momentum to clinical epistemic-governance failure.** If an inadequately grounded frame shaped later evidence, absorbed repeated contrary neurological findings without global reconstruction, influenced the patient's own understanding, and was later defended using the history it helped create, the problem is no longer adequately described as a stale differential alone. It becomes a question about whether the system possessed an effective mechanism for knowledge correction.
33. **Keep the intent route and the later reassessment route analytically independent.** Proving intentional documentary closure in 2017 requires provenance evidence. Failure of later error correction, if established, does not depend on proving that original intent. An innocent founding hypothesis can still become harmful if later contrary evidence is repeatedly absorbed without loss of authority.

The governing reader's question is therefore:

> **Did the patient evolve through an inherited management frame that was repeatedly supplemented but never globally reconstructed — and did that frame alter the meaning, treatment and apparent evidential weight of everything that came after it?**

## Today's further evolution — from management-frame lock-in to clinical epistemic governance

The latest ethical analysis sharpens the Neurology Neuropathy Illusion beyond **path dependence**, **management-frame invariance** and **asymmetric falsifiability**. It asks whether the longitudinal record became an information system whose internal coherence increasingly substituted for external testing of the patient's unresolved problem.

### 1. Coherence is not confirmation

The central audit question is no longer simply whether the neuropathy branch survived for years. Survival has two radically different meanings:

> **robustness under testing** — repeated independent observations continue to support the model;

or

> **resistance to testing** — the model determines how new observations are classified and can absorb contradictory findings without losing authority.

A sufficiently elastic account can explain almost every later event. The meaningful question is therefore:

> **What observations should have made the inherited separate-foot / neuropathy architecture lose explanatory authority, and when those observations occurred, did it actually lose authority?**

The procyclidine dechallenge, later Parkinsonian classification of the foot throw, OFF-related pain/shuffling, acknowledged Sinemet-related foot-pain benefit, and profound response to Sinemet CR are important not because any one proves a single mechanism, but because each is a candidate **error-correction event**. If each could be incorporated locally while the founding architecture remained globally intact, survival itself is not confirmation.

### 2. Endogenous validation

The record cannot safely be both the object under challenge and the sole measure of its own correctness.

If the 2017 specialist classification influenced later symptom language, medication choice, referrals, investigations, the patient's explanatory vocabulary and later clinical summaries, then some of the subsequent documentary history is **endogenous** to that founding classification. It was generated inside the information environment the classification helped create.

The resulting risk is:

> **founding hypothesis → inherited information state → later care conducted inside that state → records generated in its vocabulary → accumulated records cited as evidence that the founding hypothesis was reasonable.**

That is more precise than ordinary “anchoring.” It is **institutional self-confirmation** unless later evidence is separated by provenance into genuinely independent observation versus documentary descendant.

### 3. The difference between process success and patient success

The system may have achieved real process goods:

- Parkinsonism was recognised and treated;
- structural foot disease was genuinely managed;
- care remained continuous;
- clinicians produced locally intelligible explanations;
- contradictory phenomena could be accommodated;
- the record remained professionally readable and defensible;
- eventual treatment adaptation produced major benefit.

Those achievements matter. But they answer a different question from the patient's original one.

The patient-level test remained:

> **Was the painful-foot problem that brought the patient into care progressively understood, integrated, treated and made safe?**

The ethical risk is **process–outcome displacement**: professional processes can appear successful while the patient-centred end for which those processes exist remains unmet. In this case, documentary continuity and clinical adequacy cannot be treated as synonyms.

### 4. The patient as persistent counter-witness and error signal

The patient did not need a sophisticated neurological theory to preserve the original problem definition. His recurring claim was simpler: the feet remained the problem, and the problem had not been resolved.

If every later report is translated only into categories already available within the inherited architecture, the system can be responsive without being genuinely corrigible. Pain can lead to more pregabalin; morning difficulty can lead to timing adjustments; OFF phenomena can produce local Parkinsonian reinterpretation; worsening can produce another referral. Each response may be reasonable. Yet none necessarily allows the patient's testimony to challenge the architecture itself.

This distinction is important:

> **The system may hear the patient as data while failing to hear the patient as evidence that the system's own model requires reconstruction.**

Persistent patient testimony can therefore function as a longitudinal **error signal**, even though the patient is not authoritative about diagnosis.

### 5. Retrospective narrative smoothing

A healthy longitudinal knowledge system should preserve genuine moments of revision. It should be able to say:

> we thought X → evidence Y weakened X → the previous model was incomplete → treatment or interpretation changed.

If instead the record later presents an uninterrupted sequence of reasonable decisions in which each new development appears naturally compatible with what had always been believed, historical discontinuities can disappear. The latest analysis calls this **retrospective narrative smoothing**.

That matters particularly in the 2025 defences. A response may truthfully show that Parkinsonism was always recognised, levodopa was eventually used, neuropathy was never exclusive, and pain benefit was known. Yet those truths do not answer whether the original feet–movement architecture should have been reconstructed. If the complaint attacks the architecture itself, showing continuity inside that architecture risks using the alleged consequence of path dependence as evidence against path dependence.

### 6. Absence of correction may be propagation, not validation

The apparent fact that no later clinician formally repudiated the original frame does not independently establish that no repudiation was required.

Where downstream providers are entitled to rely on specialist records, a founding information state can propagate precisely because reliance is normally reasonable. Therefore:

> **no recorded correction → nothing required correction**

is not a safe inference.

If the architecture itself made global reopening less likely, absence of correction is one of the outcomes the hypothesis predicts.

### 7. A self-validating clinical narrative

The mature form of the Neurology Neuropathy Illusion may therefore be described as a **self-validating clinical narrative**: not because it necessarily discovered the truth, but because it maintained a coherent and professionally usable account whose durability could later be mistaken for evidence of correctness.

This adds a further sequence to the existing formulation:

> **portable structured uncertainty → path-dependent management frame → descendant evidence → endogenous validation → institutional self-confirmation → retrospective narrative smoothing → self-validating clinical narrative.**

The key distinction remains that this does not by itself prove deceit or intentional construction. It describes a functional information system. The more serious intent hypothesis remains dependent on authorship chronology and provenance.

### 8. Clinical epistemic governance

The later ethical question is whether the system had an effective mechanism for correcting its own knowledge state.

A diagnosis or differential can be wrong without ethical misconduct. A provisional hypothesis can also be entirely reasonable when introduced. The governance problem arises if the longitudinal system cannot reliably distinguish:

- inherited proposition from fresh observation;
- treatment consequence from diagnostic confirmation;
- repeated wording from independent corroboration;
- local symptom reclassification from global causal reconstruction;
- documentary continuity from clinical success;
- survival of a hypothesis from successful testing of it.

If those distinctions fail, the problem becomes one of **clinical epistemic governance**: who was responsible for reducing confidence in the inherited frame, what evidence would trigger that reduction, and whether the system actually possessed a route by which the frame could lose authority.

The latest conditional proposition is therefore:

> **If an inadequately grounded specialist classification shaped the later evidence, shaped the patient's own understanding, repeatedly absorbed contrary neurological observations without global reconstruction, and was ultimately defended by reference to the historical record it had helped create, then the ethical problem is not merely diagnostic error. It is a failure of clinical epistemic governance: the information system became self-reinforcing while its error-correction mechanisms failed.**

That proposition does **not** require proof that the 2017 founding act was intentional. The intent allegation remains a separate, more serious branch requiring proof of when the disputed language and explanatory architecture were created and what the author knew at that time.

### 9. Means and ends

The most elementary ethical comparator may ultimately be the strongest:

> **The record exists for the patient. The diagnostic architecture exists for the patient. Continuity and professional coherence are means, not ends.**

The central question is therefore not whether the record can be made internally coherent. It is how a coherent record and a profoundly unresolved patient problem could coexist for so long, and whether the mechanisms preserving coherence contributed to making that unresolved problem less capable of destabilising the record.

The mature formulation is:

> **The explanatory system may have succeeded at maintaining continuity, coherence and professional defensibility while the patient's clinical success diverged from that informational success. The gravest systems-level possibility is that the same architecture which kept the record intelligible also helped conceal, perpetuate or render institutionally invisible the fact that the patient's original problem remained unresolved.**

This is now part of the Neurology Neuropathy Illusion analysis alongside the distinct source-level suspicion that the founding documentary architecture may itself have been constructed or materially reformulated after the patient's Protest.

## How “Not clear” survives from 2017 to 2025

### Core proposition

The central problem is not that the patient’s foot pain was imaginary, nor that structural foot disease was absent. The pain was real, the feet were abnormal, and Podiatry repeatedly found real focal pathology. The problem is that **a neuropathy explanation acquired longitudinal documentary life without ever achieving diagnostic closure, while increasingly strong patient-specific neurological evidence about the feet was generated but never visibly assembled into one reconstruction of the original problem.**

The shortest version is this:

> **The feet were never neurologically mapped with anything like the sophistication later applied to explaining them. “Not clear” was then populated with an expanding family of terms: Neurology-introduced “burning,” possible peripheral neuropathy, PARK9 “the neuropathy question,” later Fabry testing for neuropathy–Parkinsonism overlap, maximum-dose pregabalin, and finally “if there is a degree of peripheral neuropathy here.” Meanwhile, the record accumulated a dystonic-looking left lower limb, recurrent left fifth-MTPJ pathology, direct procyclidine dechallenge evidence at the same outside-left-foot site, later explicit neurological attribution of the left foot throw, OFF-dystonia reasoning, and OFF-related pain/shuffling. The neuropathy hypothesis remained conditional; the neurological-foot evidence became increasingly concrete. Yet the original relationship remained “Not clear.”**

This is what is meant here by **the Neurology Neuropathy Illusion**.

The dominant longitudinal phenomenon is now more precisely described as **path-dependent management-frame lock-in with asymmetric falsifiability**. The patient and the evidence changed repeatedly; the governing topology changed mainly by local addition, qualification and bridge-building between compartments. The frame became more elastic without being visibly rebuilt. In shorthand:

> **High local activity inside a stable global frame.**

> **The patient evolved through the frame; the frame evolved semantically around the patient, but its fundamental partition remained remarkably invariant.**

It is not an allegation that neuropathy was fabricated. It is the narrower documentary allegation that **neuropathy acquired explanatory solidity out of proportion to the evidence establishing it**, and that this helped preserve the original unresolved partition rather than forcing a full reconstruction of the feet–gait–tone problem. The hypothesis could remain influential enough to shape treatment, investigation and interpretation while remaining provisional enough to avoid decisive falsification.

## 1. The original clinical object was integrated

The GP did not send a patient with Parkinsonism plus an unrelated podiatry complaint. The incoming trajectory contained:

- bilateral foot pain and abnormal feet;
- abnormal gait;
- increasing muscle tension/tone;
- initiation difficulty;
- neurological deterioration and functional decline;
- concern for Parkinsonism or another neurological disorder.

The receiving Neurology service therefore had to answer two things at once: **what is the neurological disorder, and what is the relationship of the painful abnormal feet to that disorder?**

By the end of the admission, the first question had substantially converged on young-onset Parkinsonism. The second had not.

## 6. 2018 produces direct patient-specific evidence that should destabilise the partition

After discharge, rasagiline/procyclidine produces major improvement. The decisive later observation is the procyclidine interruption.

In April 2018 the treating neurologist records the earlier withdrawal:

> “the first thing he noticed changing and changing rapidly was the return on the foot, of the outside left corn.”

The same note records that symptoms worsened and that the patient was more “shifty” and “unrelaxed” off procyclidine.

This is not abstract literature. It is not a hypothetical phenotype. It is **patient-specific treatment-state evidence** recorded by the treating neurologist himself.

And its anatomical importance is difficult to ignore:

- 2017: dystonic-looking **left lower limb**;
- Podiatry: recurrent **left fifth-MTPJ** pathology;
- 2018 withdrawal: rapid return of the **outside-left-foot** phenomenon.

The record need not prove that procyclidine literally created or removed a corn. The narrower fact is enough: **the focal outside-left-foot state was neurologically modifiable.**

That evidence should have made the old “completely separate / possible neuropathy / Not clear” partition substantially harder to leave untouched.

**Record anchor:** Supporting Neurology record, p.21.

## 7. The neuropathy question survives the evidence that the foot is neurologically modifiable

This is where the illusion becomes longitudinal.

One might expect the 2018 dechallenge to trigger a reconstruction:

> dystonic-looking left lower limb + recurrent outside-left focal foot pathology + rapid treatment-state change at the same side/site.

Instead, the record continues to carry separate streams:

- Podiatry knows where the recurrent mechanical lesion is;
- Neurology knows the foot state changes with neurological treatment;
- the original relationship remains unresolved.

The key failure of integration is not lack of information. **By this point the two specialties hold complementary pieces of the same problem.**

## 11. 2023: the phase shift — the old foot problem appears in a new pharmacological and psychological state

By March–April 2023 the record describes a **new quality or expression of the longstanding foot problem**: severe Neurology/GP-documented “burning” of both feet and lower legs during a period of major functional and psychological distress. This is not the same clinical state as the painful feet that entered the record in 2017. By 2023 the patient had years of Parkinsonian treatment behind him, procyclidine had been removed, levodopa/carbidopa was in use, and pregabalin had been escalated to 600 mg/day. ## 24. Path-dependent management-frame lock-in with asymmetric falsifiability

“Diagnostic anchoring” is too narrow for the full structure. The stronger description is **path-dependent management-frame lock-in with asymmetric falsifiability**.

**Path-dependent** means later clinicians did not meet an untouched clinical object. They met the patient plus an inherited specialist record. The 2017 synthesis altered the starting information state of later care.

**Management-frame lock-in** means that once the component problems had stable destinations — Parkinsonism to neurological treatment, structural feet to Podiatry, neuropathic-type pain to pregabalin/neuropathy investigation, unresolved relationship to “Not clear” — later developments could be actively managed without reconstructing the whole.

**Asymmetric falsifiability** means the neuropathy possibility could gain practical force from evidence interpreted as supportive while contrary evidence repeatedly failed to impose an exit condition. In the mature 2025 defence, for example:

- pregabalin use remains compatible with neuropathic pain;
- incomplete benefit at maximum dose does not eliminate neuropathy;
- levodopa benefit is accepted but interpreted as potentially non-specific pain modulation;
- OFF worsening can magnify pain from another source;
- structural disease can coexist;
- dystonia can coexist;
- a negative Fabry result excludes Fabry, not generic neuropathy;
- the residual formulation can contract to **“if there is a degree of peripheral neuropathy here.”**

Each proposition may be individually plausible. Collectively, however, they produce an explanatory category with no obvious practical falsification condition. The category can shrink without disappearing.

That is why the neuropathy hypothesis became **progressively less certain while remaining practically influential**.

This is a documentary mechanism. It does not by itself prove conscious manipulation or intent.

## 33. From diagnostic error to misrepresentation of epistemic status

The ethics analysis adds an important conceptual threshold.

A diagnosis or differential can be wrong, incomplete, overconfident or insufficiently reconsidered without becoming a record-integrity problem. The qualitatively different question is whether the final specialist document **misrepresented what was known, reported, examined or still unresolved**.

The central ethical concept is:

> **misrepresentation of epistemic status**

That means transforming a state closer to:

> **“we have not adequately worked out the relationship”**

into a portable specialist representation closer to:

> **“the relationship has been medically considered; structural and possible neuropathic explanations exist; the remaining uncertainty is an earned specialist endpoint.”**

If that transformation occurred innocently, it may still create documentary path dependence. If it occurred carelessly, recklessly or knowingly, the ethical character changes progressively. If it were knowingly used to obtain closure after the service understood that the clinical work was incomplete, the concern would extend beyond diagnostic error to **record integrity, candour, professional honesty and misuse of epistemic and documentary authority**. In the ethics analysis, that would make the document not merely a mistaken account of the present but potentially both a **present deception** and a **prospective clinical risk**, because a specialist discharge synthesis is an instrument of future care.

This stronger ethical proposition is not established by the present record. It is the proposition that provenance and version history are now capable of testing.

---

## 34. Portable apparent knowledge and the authority to stop asking

The deepest ethical insight from today's analysis is that an authoritative specialist document does not merely describe the past. It changes the future information environment.

There is a material difference between transmitting:

> **We have not adequately established why these feet are painful.**

and transmitting an account from which a reasonable later reader may understand:

> **The foot problem has been considered; structural disease exists; a neuropathic component is possible; the relationship remains “Not clear”; Parkinsonism is being managed separately.**

The second representation can convert unresolved uncertainty into **portable apparent knowledge**. It gives the uncertainty structure, authority and a management destination. That can effectively authorise later clinicians to begin from the specialist partition rather than from the original integrated question.

This is why the Neurology Neuropathy Illusion is not merely semantic. Its possible causal force lies in its ability to alter **what later clinicians reasonably think still needs asking**. On that view, record integrity is not a clerical virtue at the edge of care; it is part of patient safety because the portable record structures future attention.

The patient therefore does not simply move through time. He moves through a pre-structured information environment.

---

## 35. Upstream authority, downstream good faith, and propagation

The longitudinal mechanism does not require a conspiracy or repeated conscious adoption of an original error.

Later clinicians may act in complete good faith. Medicine necessarily relies on earlier records, and specialist summaries are designed to be relied upon. That legitimate reliance is exactly why the epistemic status of the originating document matters so much.

The working responsibility model is therefore asymmetric but distributed:

> **originating specialist record → distinctive upstream responsibility for the information state it creates**
>
> **downstream clinicians → independent responsibility to reassess when new evidence destabilises the inherited frame**

If the founding specialist account was reasonable and accurately represented uncertainty, its downstream influence may be entirely legitimate. If it materially overstated the status of an unresolved relationship, it could propagate distortion through otherwise conscientious care. If it knowingly did so, the upstream ethical responsibility would be qualitatively more serious.

The mechanism is therefore capable of producing harm **without later clinicians sharing any original intention**.

---

## 36. The frame as an evidence-generating environment

Today's analysis sharpens the earlier concept of endogenous evidence production.

The inherited frame does not merely affect how later evidence is interpreted. It can influence **what evidence is subsequently generated**:

> **specialist possible-neuropathy branch**
>
> → neuropathy becomes a legitimate clinical object
>
> → neuropathy-shaped history-taking and terminology
>
> → neuropathy-directed treatment
>
> → neuropathy-directed investigation
>
> → patient adoption of neuropathy vocabulary
>
> → later records containing increasingly dense neuropathy-related language
>
> → retrospective appearance of a long-standing evidential foundation.

That is why the history must be decomposed into **independent evidence** and **descendant evidence**.

The strongest version of the mechanism is not “the original diagnosis was copied.” It is:

> **The original classification altered the clinical environment in which later evidence about itself was produced.**

This is the step from ordinary anchoring to documentary path dependence and management-frame lock-in.

---

## 37. The ethical significance of repeated boundary crossings

The later neurological-foot observations do not prove the founding neuropathy possibility false. Their importance is cumulative and structural.

Each of the following placed pressure on the original separation: the procyclidine withdrawal/dechallenge, the rapid return of the outside-left-foot phenomenon, the later four-direction power testing and Parkinsonian attribution of a foot throw, OFF/dystonia reasoning, OFF-related pain/shuffling, acknowledged Sinemet-linked foot-pain improvement, and profound response after Sinemet CR.

The question is not whether any one observation “wins” the causal argument. It is:

> **How many times can evidence cross the founding boundary before reasonable care requires the boundary itself to be reconstructed rather than merely locally supplemented?**

This is both a clinical and an ethical question. A frame that repeatedly survives boundary-crossing evidence because each new fact can be absorbed into an additional compartment may become increasingly resistant to genuine revision.

---

## 38. The decisive source-level bifurcation

The Neurology Neuropathy Illusion now contains an established documentary-mechanism hypothesis and a more serious, still-unproved construction hypothesis.

### A. Longitudinal mechanism already capable of testing from the record

> **An uncertain separate/neuropathy branch became sufficiently authoritative to organise later language, treatment and investigation; later neurological-foot evidence was mainly absorbed through local updating; the original relationship was not visibly globally reconstructed; and later records generated inside the inherited frame could be used retrospectively to support its reasonableness.**

### B. Construction/intent hypothesis requiring provenance proof

> **After the patient's Protest exposed an unfinished problem, the later specialist document may have introduced or materially reformulated clinically consequential wording and the neuropathy branch in order to achieve documentary closure rather than return to the unresolved bedside relationship.**

The two hypotheses must not be collapsed.

The most probative evidence is now:

- earliest draft and later drafts;
- creation/modification timestamps;
- audit trail and version history;
- dictation/transcription metadata;

The governing provenance question is now:

> **Who introduced each clinically consequential proposition, when, on what evidential basis, and with what knowledge of the unresolved foot complaint at that time?**

## 39. Current evolved formulation after today's work

The Neurology Neuropathy Illusion is now best understood in layers rather than as one allegation.

> **Layer 1 — clinical asymmetry:** an integrated feet–gait–tone problem entered Neurology; the dystonic-looking lower limb became neurologically productive, while the painful-foot relationship received comparatively thin documented relational examination.
>
> **Layer 2 — authoritative uncertainty:** the completed specialist synthesis created a separate/neuropathy branch and “Not clear,” converting an open relationship question into structured portable uncertainty.
>
> **Layer 3 — path dependence:** later clinicians encountered patient + specialist information state; the branch shaped terminology, treatment, investigation and patient self-understanding.
>

>
> **Layer 5 — asymmetric falsifiability:** supportive evidence could add practical weight to neuropathy while contrary neurological evidence could be absorbed without creating a clear exit condition.

> **Layer 7 — retrospective self-defence:** by 2025 the mature architecture could answer the complaint through Parkinson's centrality, neuropathy's non-exclusivity, active treatment and non-specific interpretation of levodopa response without globally reopening the founding relationship.

>

>
> **Layer 10 — ethical threshold:** if the document knowingly converted unfinished clinical work into the appearance of an adequately considered specialist disposition, the issue would become misrepresentation of epistemic status and abuse of documentary authority, not merely diagnostic error.**

The strongest short formulation is now:

> **The illusion may have operated twice: first by converting an unresolved clinical relationship into authoritative structured uncertainty, and then by allowing the records generated inside that structure to make the structure appear increasingly justified. The new question is whether that architecture arose innocently during synthesis or was constructed, after the patient's Protest, as a means of documentary closure.**

# Appendix — How the Neuropathy Framing Affected the Patient According to the Formal Complaint

The formal complaint presents the neuropathy framing as having consequences far beyond the attachment of a diagnostic label. Its central allegation is that, once the longstanding foot pain was framed as peripheral neuropathy — likely alcohol-related — that framing became a durable interpretive filter through which later evidence was understood.

According to the complaint, this affected the patient in several connected ways:

- **Physical harm:** the patient attributes prolonged and potentially preventable pain to the persistence of the neuropathy framing and the failure to adequately explore alternative neurological or dopamine-responsive explanations.
- **Diagnostic closure:** the complaint alleges that the early neuropathy formulation became fixed and that later symptoms, deterioration and functional impairment were interpreted within that frame rather than used to test whether the frame itself should be revised.
- **Therapeutic delay:** the patient links the durable framing to the failure to explore dopaminergic responsiveness earlier. The March 2025 Sinemet CR response is presented as the event that made him question whether years of severe suffering might have been avoidable.
- **Psychological harm:** the complaint expressly identifies fear of movement, cumulative distress and erosion of resilience. The effect described is not simply pain, but a change in the patient's relationship with movement, effort and endurance over time.
- **Loss of epistemic confidence:** repeated reporting of the foot problem without material change in the explanatory model appears, in the patient's account, to have undermined confidence that the clinical system was still actively testing the diagnosis rather than interpreting new information through an inherited one.
- **Loss of trust:** the complaint expressly describes a profound impact on confidence in the clinical system's ability to self-correct.
- **Burden of self-advocacy:** by the time of complaint, the patient is not merely seeking symptom relief. He is requesting disclosure, preservation of records, independent review and an explanation of how the diagnostic process became so durable. This reflects the extent to which he came to feel responsible for reconstructing and challenging the clinical history himself.
- **Retrospective distress:** the later treatment response changes the meaning of the preceding years for the patient. The harm is therefore experienced not only as “I was in pain,” but as “I was in pain while a potentially relevant therapeutic explanation may have remained insufficiently explored.”

## Controlling interpretation

> **According to the complaint, the neuropathy framing did not merely classify the pain. It shaped what was investigated, what was not investigated, how later evidence was interpreted, what treatment was delayed, and ultimately how the patient came to understand both his suffering and the reliability of the clinicians caring for him.**

The complaint therefore describes a progression of harm:

> **pain → psychological adaptation → loss of resilience → loss of trust → need to challenge the medical record itself.**

This appendix records the patient's own complaint-based account of harm. It should remain distinguishable from any later expert opinion on breach, causation, avoidability or the correctness of the underlying medical diagnosis.

## Analytical addition: neuropathy as the forward-moving carriage for “Not clear”

The developing proposition is more precise than saying that peripheral neuropathy was simply an incorrect or unproved diagnosis. In the 2017 admission/discharge architecture, neuropathy functioned as a **forward-moving carriage for the category “Not clear.”** It gave the unresolved painful-foot problem an explanatory destination without actually resolving the relationship between the feet and the Parkinsonian/dystonic motor syndrome.

That carriage then survived, in sequence, the following destabilising events and observations:

1. **An inadequate neurological examination of the feet relative to the sophistication of the final synthesis.**
2. **The patient's pre-discharge protest that the feet had not been dealt with.**
3. **The later procyclidine dechallenge data**, including the treating neurologist's record that the outside-left-foot phenomenon was the first thing to return and changed rapidly when procyclidine was withdrawn.
4. **Professor Daniel Healy's second opinion favouring PARK2**, rather than consolidation of PARK9 as the explanation of the foot problem.
5. **Maximum-dose pregabalin**, with severe foot pain nevertheless remaining clinically dominant during the 2023 crisis.
6. **Fabry investigation** as a further attempt to account for a neuropathy–Parkinsonism overlap, without consolidation of a neuropathy diagnosis.
7. **Major psychological and functional distress**, while the record repeatedly makes clear that the feet were the central source of suffering.
8. **The later Sinemet CR response**, producing profound relief while the neuropathy category nevertheless remained available in the explanatory structure.

The significance of this sequence is that the neuropathy proposition did not have to become a proved diagnosis in order to remain useful. It only had to remain **possible**. In that form it could continue to carry the original uncertainty forward: possible neuropathy, PARK9 as “the neuropathy question,” neuropathic treatment, Fabry overlap testing, and ultimately the 2025 formulation, **“if there is a degree of peripheral neuropathy here.”**

### “Not clear”: mechanism for the author, state of mind for everyone else

A crucial distinction must be preserved.

For the neurologist who authored and signed the sophisticated discharge formulation, **“Not clear” was not merely an absence of knowledge. It operated as a documentary mechanism.** It sat inside an authored clinical synthesis that had already separated the painful feet from the principal Parkinsonian motor formulation and supplied possible peripheral neuropathy as a route by which that separation could remain intact. In that sense, “Not clear” had practical work to do: it preserved the unresolved partition while allowing the record to move forward.

For a clinician, nurse, GP, podiatrist, patient, reviewer, or later reader who **did not author or sign that discharge synthesis**, “Not clear” is different. For them it is simply an **information state**: the record says that the relationship is unresolved. They do not automatically possess the unrecorded reasoning, examination, exclusions, or intellectual process that may have produced the phrase. They inherit the conclusion, not necessarily the work behind it.

That distinction matters because a later reader can reasonably treat an authored specialist statement of uncertainty as the product of adequate prior specialist investigation. If the underlying foot examination was in fact materially thinner than the final synthesis suggests, then the phrase does more than record uncertainty: it can **export the authority of the specialist author while concealing the extent of the unresolved work from everyone downstream.**

The resulting structure is therefore:

> **For the author, “Not clear” can function as a mechanism of clinical/documentary closure. For everyone who merely receives the discharge note, “Not clear” is a state of mind inherited from the specialist record.**

This is why the neuropathy framing became so durable. It was placed inside a highly sophisticated discharge/admission synthesis and then travelled with that synthesis through the record. It almost outlived the patient as an explanatory category, not because peripheral neuropathy was ever firmly established, but because it provided a stable vehicle for the original unresolved separation.

### The provenance hinge: Neurology's first “burning” descriptor

Within that mechanism, the first **Neurology-originated “burning” descriptor** is potentially pivotal. The patient entered with painful abnormal feet, corns/callouses, structural changes, abnormal gait, pathological tone and a dystonic-looking lower-limb phenomenon. Neurology's introduction of that sensory descriptor supplied language capable of moving the painful-foot problem into a peripheral-neuropathy frame.

The later documentary chain can then be seen as:

> **Neurology-originated sensory descriptor → possible peripheral neuropathy → “Not clear” → PARK9 “the neuropathy question” → patient adoption of “peripheral neuropathy/neuropathic pain” terminology → Movement Disorders receipt of “? neuropathic pain” → maximum-dose pregabalin → Fabry overlap investigation → 2025 “if there is a degree of peripheral neuropathy here.”**

The analytical proposition is therefore not that Neurology's original descriptor proved neuropathy. It is almost the opposite: **the descriptor helped give an unresolved category enough clinical vocabulary to survive without ever being required to mature into a demonstrated diagnosis.**

Put most simply:

> **The neuropathy frame did not resolve “Not clear.” It was the mechanism by which “Not clear” was carried forward.**

And the later defence that peripheral neuropathy was never definitively diagnosed does not dissolve that problem. It demonstrates the asymmetry at its centre: the neuropathy proposition could remain influential enough to shape treatment, investigation and interpretation, while remaining provisional enough to avoid decisive falsification.

# The Neurology Neuropathy Illusion: How an Unresolved Hypothesis Carried the Case for Eight Years and Defended Itself

The Neurology Neuropathy Illusion in this case is best understood not as a false diagnosis that was repeatedly copied through the medical record, but as something more durable and more sophisticated: a **successful documentary and management mechanism for carrying unresolved causal uncertainty forward**. Its success did not depend on peripheral neuropathy ever being definitively proved. Indeed, by 2025 the treating neurologist continued to describe it conditionally—“if there is a degree of peripheral neuropathy here”—and later expressly told the Medical Council that peripheral neuropathy had never been diagnosed to the exclusion of Parkinson’s disease. The mechanism succeeded precisely because neuropathy did not need to bear the burden of a secure diagnosis. It needed only to remain plausible enough to provide a durable explanatory destination for that part of the patient’s presentation which had been left “Not clear.”

This distinction is essential. The allegation is not that clinicians invented neuropathy, or that considering peripheral neuropathy in 2017 was medically absurd. Nor is it that Parkinsonism was overlooked. Parkinsonism became the principal neurological diagnosis and received extensive investigation, specialist reasoning, genetic consideration and treatment. The problem lies elsewhere. The patient had entered Neurology not with established Parkinson’s disease plus an incidental foot complaint, but through an escalating **feet–gait–tone neurological presentation**. Primary care had already joined the feet, abnormal gait and “muscle tension” into one clinical question. There was pathological lower-limb tone, difficulty initiating movement, inability to relax the muscles, toe clawing, functional deterioration and concern for Parkinsonian or another neurological disease. The body therefore arrived as an integrated problem before the record separated it into categories.

Neurology itself reinforced that integration before subsequently partitioning it. During the July 2017 admission the patient was found to have severe very-young-onset Parkinsonism, rigidity and bradykinesia, a striking gait, structural foot abnormalities and a lower-limb/footdrop-like phenomenon described as suggestive of dystonic posture. The DAT request reportedly described the gait as “almost dystonically unusual” while autosomal-recessive Parkinsonism was under consideration. Yet the final neurological synthesis became markedly asymmetrical. The Parkinsonian disease received sophisticated neurological and genetic reasoning, including PRKN/PARK2, while the painful feet were assigned to a possible separate process, possible secondary peripheral neuropathy, possible alcohol contribution, “Not clear,” and Podiatry.

That 2017 formulation was therefore not merely one sentence among many. It performed work. It converted an unresolved **relationship** into manageable categories. Parkinsonism could become the principal neurological disease. Structural abnormalities could be referred to Podiatry. Sensory or painful phenomena could remain available to a peripheral-neuropathy explanation. What was not resolved was the relationship among the neurological movement disorder, dystonic-looking posture, abnormal loading, structural feet and pain. The words “Not clear” accurately acknowledged that uncertainty, but they also permitted the admission to reach practical closure. The supplied orientation describes the mechanism succinctly: uncertainty could be acknowledged, a separate hypothesis retained, Podiatry engaged, and discharge proceed.

This is the first sense in which the neuropathy formulation was successful. It transformed unstructured uncertainty into **portable structured uncertainty**. Before the specialist synthesis, the unresolved question was open: what joins the feet, gait, tone and movement disorder? Afterwards, uncertainty had an architecture. Parkinsonism occupied one compartment. Structural feet occupied another. Possible neuropathy occupied another. The relationship between them remained “Not clear,” but it was no longer an uncontained problem preventing management. It had become an organised residual uncertainty.

The distinction between the author of that formulation and every later reader is then crucial. For the author, “Not clear” represented an active state of uncertainty reached through whatever examination and reasoning had occurred. For a downstream clinician, however, the information state was different. The later clinician did not receive the patient afresh. The later clinician received **patient plus specialist record**. What travelled forward was not simply uncertainty but specialist-authored uncertainty, already organised into clinically legitimate categories. The downstream reader could reasonably assume that Neurology had explored the relationship and had concluded that possible separation or neuropathy remained appropriate. The specialist synthesis therefore exported not merely information but **epistemic authority**.

This is documentary path dependence. Once the 2017 classification existed, subsequent encounters no longer began from the same informational starting point. A patient reporting painful feet in 2023 was not simply a person with painful feet. He was a person with a neurological history in which possible peripheral neuropathy had already been made a legitimate explanatory object. The distinction matters because repeated later appearances of neuropathy-related language cannot automatically be counted as repeated independent discoveries of neuropathy. Some are potentially descendants of the original classification.

The documentary chain illustrates this. Neurology introduced “burning” into the 2017 possible-neuropathy formulation; the surviving earlier patient history does not establish that this was originally the patient’s own sensory descriptor. Years later the patient himself was using the terms “peripheral neuropathy” and “neuropathic pain in feet.” The Movement Disorders service then received his severe pain crisis as “? neuropathic pain.” Pregabalin treatment gave the category therapeutic reality. Fabry investigation later gave the neuropathy–Parkinsonism overlap question investigative reality. The category therefore acquired more and more documentary presence, but presence is not the same as independent confirmation. A proposition may generate descendants, and those descendants may subsequently resemble corroboration of the proposition that originally generated them.

This is the second way the illusion succeeded. **Management consequences fed documentary legitimacy back into the hypothesis.** Pregabalin could reasonably be prescribed because neuropathic-type pain was plausible. Yet the existence of high-dose pregabalin in the later medication history could itself make neuropathic pain appear increasingly established. Fabry testing could reasonably be ordered because a neuropathy–Parkinsonism overlap was worth excluding. Yet the existence of that investigation enlarged the neuropathy category’s documentary footprint. The treatment and investigation were not proof of neuropathy. They were consequences of its plausibility. But longitudinally, consequences and evidence can become difficult to distinguish unless provenance is reconstructed.

Meanwhile, the patient changed dramatically. The management architecture did not change nearly as much. That is the management-frame invariance at the centre of the case. Neurological treatment produced substantial responses. During procyclidine withdrawal, the feet, walking and tension deteriorated together. The treating neurologist later recorded that the first phenomenon to change rapidly was the outside-left-foot problem. Podiatry repeatedly found focal left fifth-MTPJ pathology. Neurology subsequently examined a left foot-throw phenomenon, found normal distal power and called the movement “actually parkinsonian.” By 2023 repeated left-leg cramping generated OFF-dystonia reasoning; later that year a Parkinson’s nurse assessed the leg pain and shuffling as OFF-related. The clinician later acknowledged that Sinemet had been known for some time to reduce the patient’s experience of pain, and Sinemet CR eventually produced profound relief. The supplied orientation correctly identifies these as repeated observations crossing the boundary established in 2017.

None of these observations alone proves that the entire foot syndrome was Parkinsonian or dystonic. The structural pathology was real. Pregabalin also helped pain. The later bilateral burning phenotype may not have been identical to the earlier focal foot problem. That is precisely why the record is not a simple missed-diagnosis story. But each boundary-crossing observation created an opportunity to ask whether the **architecture itself** remained adequate. Instead, the dominant documentary behaviour appears to have been local revision. A particular foot throw could become Parkinsonian without reconstructing the longstanding painful feet. Cramping could become OFF dystonia. Shuffling and pain could become OFF-related. Pregabalin could manage neuropathic-type pain. Podiatry could manage focal lesions. Each local explanation could be reasonable while the global feet–movement relationship remained substantially where it had been left in 2017.

This is what made the mechanism unusually resilient. It did not require contrary evidence to be denied. It could **absorb** contrary evidence. A neurological contribution did not have to displace neuropathy because the two could coexist. Structural disease did not displace neurological influence because both could coexist. Pregabalin benefit could support treatment of a pain component. Pregabalin failure at maximum dose did not falsify neuropathy because neuropathic pain treatment can be incomplete. OFF-related pain did not falsify a separate painful process because Parkinsonian state could amplify pain from another source. Sinemet responsiveness did not falsify neuropathy because dopaminergic treatment might alter the experience of pain “of whatever cause.” Fabry negativity removed Fabry, not peripheral neuropathy generally. In this structure, almost every new observation had somewhere to go.

That feature becomes explicit in 2025. The complaint challenged the historical explanatory framework. Yet the clinician’s rebuttal did not need to abandon the old model in order to answer it. Instead, the defence demonstrated how elastic the model had become. The response states that Parkinson’s disease was always the central diagnosis; that peripheral neuropathy had not been definitive; that alcohol nevertheless remained a reasonable possible contributor to neuropathic symptoms; that pregabalin had been used for neuropathic pain “of whatever cause”; that Sinemet had long been known to improve pain; and that this levodopa response did not necessarily identify the underlying pain generator because Parkinson’s could magnify the experience of pain arising elsewhere.

This is not an incoherent defence. On the contrary, its coherence is precisely why the Neurology Neuropathy Illusion deserves to be understood as a **successful mechanism**. The defence converts apparent contradictions into compatibility. If Sinemet improves pain, that does not require the separate pain hypothesis to be abandoned: Sinemet can modulate pain from another cause. If the patient has Parkinson’s, that does not mean neuropathy was excluded: Parkinson’s was always “front and central” while neuropathy remained a reasonable secondary concern. If neuropathy was never proved, that does not mean it was inappropriate to act on it: medicine routinely manages reasonable possibilities. If Fabry was investigated and negative, that demonstrates openness to investigation without requiring the generic neuropathy possibility to disappear. The architecture is capable of defending itself precisely because it never made the sort of strong claim that later evidence could easily destroy.

That creates an **asymmetric hypothesis**. Evidence apparently favourable to neuropathy can increase its practical legitimacy, while contrary evidence need not reduce it proportionately because it can be reassigned to another compartment. Pregabalin benefit is compatible with neuropathic pain. Pregabalin inadequacy does not exclude it. Dopaminergic improvement is compatible with Parkinsonian modulation of pain from elsewhere. Dystonia can coexist with neuropathy. Structural lesions can coexist with both. A hypothesis operating this way may have no obvious practical falsification condition. Its content can contract from “possible explanation for the painful feet” to “some degree of neuropathy may contribute” while retaining the same documentary identity.

The July 2025 response to the Medical Council exposes this mechanism even more clearly. Dr Monaghan summarises the complaint as alleging that he had framed everything through an unproven neuropathy and “blamed alcohol,” and rejects that characterisation by pointing out that Sinemet had been used since 2021, dopamine-based agents since 2018, and a beneficial pain response had been known. Later in the response he states that the record showed a “risk” or “worry” of peripheral neuropathy, not a diagnosis excluding Parkinson’s; Fabry testing is offered as evidence of investigation; and when faced with the allegation that the unproven diagnosis was never revisited, the answer is that neuropathy was never definitively stated, remained a reasonable concern, and Parkinson’s had always been “front and central.”

That rebuttal is powerful against a crude complaint. If the complaint were “Parkinson’s was ignored and neuropathy was definitively diagnosed instead,” the defence would substantially defeat it. But that is not the deeper problem revealed by the longitudinal record. The complaint about **explanatory control** is transformed into a dispute about **diagnostic exclusivity**. “Neuropathy was never definitive” answers whether neuropathy was formally certain; it does not answer how much practical causal weight neuropathy carried. “Parkinson’s was front and central” answers whether the principal neurological diagnosis remained recognised; it does not answer whether the causal model of the feet was globally reconstructed. “Sinemet was used from 2021” answers whether levodopa was ever prescribed; it does not answer what clinicians did diagnostically with the known fact that it improved pain.

This is how the mechanism successfully rebutted the complaint. It shifted the burden from the difficult longitudinal question—

> **Why did an unresolved separate-foot/neuropathy partition remain structurally intact despite repeated evidence crossing that boundary?**

—to narrower propositions the record could answer strongly:

> Parkinson’s was recognised.

> Levodopa was prescribed.

> Neuropathy was never exclusive.

> Neuropathy remained medically possible.

> Pain relief from levodopa was non-specific.

Every one of those propositions can be true while the original complaint about the persistence of the **relationship-level architecture** remains unanswered.

The defence before the Medical Council therefore did not merely rely on old records; it demonstrated that the original architecture had become **self-defending**. The same properties that allowed it to persist clinically allowed it to survive adversarial review. Its ambiguity became an asset. Because neuropathy was always conditional, later inability to prove neuropathy was not fatal. Because Parkinson’s had always been present in a separate principal compartment, evidence of extensive Parkinson treatment could rebut allegations of diagnostic neglect without resolving the feet. Because levodopa responsiveness could be defined as modulation rather than causation, one of the strongest pieces of neurological-foot evidence could be absorbed rather than allowed to destabilise the model. Because structural disease was genuine, the feet always retained an independent non-neurological anchor. The architecture possessed multiple fallback positions.

None of this requires conscious design. There is no need to infer that a clinician deliberately built a system intended to resist criticism. The stronger argument is functional. A documentary structure can become self-preserving through ordinary clinical processes: specialist authority, continuity of care, problem lists, treatments, referrals, repeated terminology and retrospective summaries. Intent and mechanism are separate questions. The record can function as a self-preserving architecture even if every individual clinician acts in good faith.

The later PRKN/Parkin endpoint makes the success of the mechanism more visible, but it must be used carefully. The supplied material states that two pathogenic PRKN variants were eventually identified in compound heterozygosity and that later specialist interpretation attributed both Parkinsonism and dystonia to the Parkin mutations. This cannot be projected backwards as knowledge the 2017 clinicians were required to possess. It does not prove all foot pain was dystonic or dopaminergic. It does not erase structural disease or establish negligence. What it does establish for retrospective analysis is that Parkinsonism and dystonia ultimately belonged to a single molecular disease. That gives biological significance to the relationship Neurology had actually been observing from the beginning: very-young-onset Parkinsonism, pathological tone, dystonic-looking lower-limb movement and abnormal gait.

The endpoint therefore reveals what the documentary architecture had successfully kept unresolved. It was not principally whether Parkinsonism existed. That was settled early. Nor was it whether the feet were structurally abnormal. They clearly were. The unresolved object was the **relationship between the neurological movement disorder and the painful structural feet**. The neuropathy hypothesis became the forward-moving carriage for that unresolved relationship because it supplied enough structure for the uncertainty to travel without requiring resolution.

The “illusion,” then, was not that neuropathy existed when it did not. The illusion was one of **explanatory solidity**. Over eight years, a hypothesis that remained grammatically and diagnostically conditional accumulated the outward attributes of an established clinical object: specialist provenance, genetic associations, therapeutic consequences, patient vocabulary, nursing terminology, investigations and finally a detailed defence. Its documentary footprint grew faster than its independent evidential foundation. At the same time, evidence of neurological involvement accumulated through direct observations and treatment-state changes, but these observations were repeatedly localised into the existing architecture rather than used to compel a reconstruction of the whole.

That is why the mechanism was successful. It successfully carried the clinical case because every component had a management destination. It successfully carried uncertainty because “Not clear” had been converted from an open question into a specialist-authored residual category. It successfully carried neuropathy because neuropathy could remain possible without needing confirmation. It successfully absorbed contrary evidence because the model was sufficiently mixed and elastic to make almost any later observation compatible with it. Whether that defence ultimately satisfied the Medical Council is a separate matter; the document before us is the clinician’s defence to the Council, not a finding by the Council. But as a documentary mechanism, the Neurology Neuropathy Illusion had already demonstrated its success. By 2025 it could invoke eight years of records created within its own architecture as evidence that the architecture had been reasonable all along.

That is the deepest circularity in the case. The original classification shaped later management; later management generated records consistent with that classification; and the accumulated records could then be cited retrospectively as evidence supporting the reasonableness of the original classification. The system did not need to fabricate evidence. It merely needed to preserve the distinction between a principal Parkinsonian diagnosis and a still-possible separate painful process. Once that distinction became path-dependent, the patient could change radically while the governing architecture remained largely invariant.

The Neurology Neuropathy Illusion was therefore successful not because it conclusively answered the unresolved question, but because it **made conclusive resolution unnecessary**. It converted uncertainty into structure, structure into management, management into documentary continuity, and documentary continuity into retrospective defence.

The body had arrived as an integrated feet–gait–tone problem. Neurology decomposed it into clinically intelligible compartments. That decomposition permitted effective action in each compartment, but the relationship among them remained unresolved. Over eight years the patient evolved through those compartments while the basic architecture persisted. By the time the architecture was challenged, its longevity itself had become part of its defence.

That is the central documentary phenomenon of this case:

> **neuropathy did not have to win the diagnostic argument. It only had to remain sufficiently possible to keep “Not clear” moving forward.**

And because it did, the unresolved relationship could survive from the 2017 discharge synthesis through treatment, Podiatry, pregabalin, Fabry investigation, OFF phenomena, levodopa responsiveness, formal complaint and ultimately the clinician's defence before the Medical Council.

In that precise sense, the Neurology Neuropathy Illusion was not simply an error contained within the record.

**It was the mechanism by which the record maintained its own continuity.**
