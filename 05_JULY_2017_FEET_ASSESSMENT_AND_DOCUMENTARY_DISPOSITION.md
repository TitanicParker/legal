# 05 — July 2017 Feet Assessment and Documentary Disposition

## Status and purpose

This is a focused evidential bridge document. It does not decide breach, causation, diagnosis or motive. Its purpose is to isolate the documentary handling of the feet during the 3–11 July 2017 Neurology admission and to identify the precise expert questions created by that record.

The controlling evidential source remains `REPAIRED_CANONICAL_V2.md` subject to any verified correction from an original source document. Quoted material below follows the current working transcription, including apparent source/transcription irregularities where material.

The alleged pre-discharge Protest is **not required for the core analysis in this document**. It remains a separate conditional route elsewhere in the repository.

---

# 1. The originating clinical question

The patient did not arrive at Neurology with an already-separated Parkinsonian problem and foot problem. Primary care had progressively related painful abnormal feet, abnormal gait, impaired movement initiation, increased tone / muscle tension, toe clawing and functional deterioration.

The July admission therefore had to address an integrated lower-limb problem: **what was the relationship between the painful feet, abnormal gait and increased tone / movement abnormality?**

The documentary question at discharge is not merely whether Parkinsonism was diagnosed correctly. It is what work was done on the relationship between the neurologically abnormal lower limb and the painful abnormal foot, and what evidential basis supported the disposition ultimately returned to the GP.

### What the current July record does not document

Within the presently identified July examination material, the record does not document a directed distal-foot neurological discrimination equivalent to:

- dorsiflexion power;
- plantarflexion power;
- inversion power;
- eversion power;
- a plantar response;
- a directed distal sensory examination supporting or excluding peripheral neuropathy;
- a clearly documented ankle-specific reflex assessment directed to the neuropathy question; or
- an explicit examination of fixed versus dynamic / passively correctable foot or toe posture tied to pain and loading.

This is a **documentary statement**, not proof that an undocumented manoeuvre did not occur. The expert issue is what can properly be inferred from the contemporaneous record and what reasonable neurological practice required to be performed and/or documented in this presentation.

# 3. The dystonic lower limb was used diagnostically

The dystonic-looking lower-limb phenomenon was not merely noticed and forgotten.

In the DaTscan indication, the neurologist wrote:

**CLIN-001394–001402 / duplicated at CLIN-001869**

> DAT SCAN please. In very young man age 39. Bilateral Parkinsonism, hard to say which side is worse. He doesn't really notice a tremor, but it comes out in RLL. Grossly bradykinetic. In many respects atypical. Some form of symptoms for up to 13 years now. Progressive now in a more typical fashion. **Walk is almost dystonically unusual.** We suspect an Autosomal recessive "Idiopathic", Parkinsons.

The lower-limb/gait abnormality was therefore clinically productive within the Parkinsonian diagnostic project.

The important asymmetry is not that Neurology overlooked the lower limb. **It used it.**

> **Neurology took ownership of the limb as neurological evidence when its dystonic appearance supported the Parkinsonian investigation, while the final reasoning about the painful-foot relationship did not visibly carry that neurological feature through to an equivalent relational analysis of the foot attached to that limb.**

This is a documentary asymmetry. Whether it was clinically justified is an expert question.

# 4. Final interpretive / disposition paragraph

**CLIN-001142–001149**

> The leg symptoms including corns and callouses and burning may have been completely separate or perhaps a secondary peripheral neuropathy perhaps in somewtiet respect related to the significant original alcohol intake? Not clear as this has improved.
>
> However he has in his own mind - markedly divorced the feelings of tension in the legs which was/is the predominant problem -from the "corns and callouses" issues and Pains in the feet---while corns and callouses remain a major problem, in reality they are much less of an issue since he is on his feet much less in the last 3-4 years.

### What this paragraph does

This is not merely an absence of conclusion. The neurologist **affirmatively introduced an explanatory branch** in which the painful foot / leg phenomena might be:

- "completely separate" from the Parkinsonian/dystonic process; or
- related to a secondary peripheral neuropathy; while
- the ultimate relationship remained "Not clear."

Peripheral neuropathy is itself neurological. The precise proposition is therefore not that the neurologist necessarily said the feet had "nothing to do with Neurology". It is that the document created a causal route in which the painful-foot problem need not belong to the Parkinsonian/dystonic mechanism just identified in the lower limb.

That documentary act exists independently of the later Protest allegation and independently of proving negligence.

# 5. The central July comparison

The July record therefore contains two different uses of the lower-limb presentation.

## Parkinsonian diagnostic use

> abnormal gait / footdrop-like movement → dystonic-looking posture → neurologically meaningful lower-limb sign → DaTscan / young-onset Parkinsonian diagnostic reasoning

## Painful-foot disposition

> corns / callouses / "burning" / patient distinction from tension → possible complete separation / possible peripheral neuropathy → "Not clear"

The core question is:

> **What neurological and clinical work justified introducing a separate / peripheral-neuropathy explanatory branch for the painful feet when the same lower-limb presentation had already generated a dystonic-looking neurological sign used in the Parkinsonian work-up?**

The case does not require the separate branch to be proved wrong. It requires its evidential basis to be identified and tested.

2. a Podiatry description of the **plantar metatarsal area**.

No use of `plantar` has been identified in the July 2017 admission/discharge examination.

In 2020, when Neurology again considered a foot-throw appearance that looked like foot drop, the neurologist documented:

**CLIN-002284–002288**

> Coming back to the foot throw which looks a little bit like a foot drop-this is actually parkinsonian because the power for **dorsiflexion, plantarflexion, inversion and eversion are all normal**.

This later entry is important as an **internal documentary comparator**, not as a device for importing the 2020 standard of care backwards into 2017.

It demonstrates how the same Neurology record documented directed distal motor discrimination when determining the nature of a footdrop-like phenomenon: the appearance was linked to explicit four-direction foot power findings and then classified as Parkinsonian.

Nothing equivalent is documented in the July foot disposition before the possibility of complete separation / peripheral neuropathy / "Not clear" was recorded.

The expert must decide what significance, if any, that comparison carries.

# 7. Procedural uncertainty versus substantive specialist uncertainty

The words "Not clear" can describe materially different epistemic states.

### Procedural uncertainty

> **We do not know because the relationship has not yet been adequately investigated.**

### Substantive specialist uncertainty

> **We have performed the appropriate specialist discriminatory work and, after that work, the relationship genuinely remains uncertain.**

The text "Not clear" does not itself distinguish those states.

The documentary issue is sharpened because the conclusion sits inside an unusually sophisticated specialist account containing extensive Parkinsonian phenomenology, young-onset differential reasoning, genetic reasoning, colleague input and targeted investigation. A recipient may therefore read the uncertainty as the endpoint of specialist inquiry rather than as a marker of unfinished inquiry.

The resulting hypothesis is:

> **If the required relational investigation had not actually been performed, the discharge may have converted procedural uncertainty into authoritative specialist uncertainty.**

That is an inference to be tested, not a proved fact.

No claim about what the GP actually understood should be made without evidence. The objective comparator is instead:

> **What clinical work would a referring GP reasonably expect to lie behind a specialist Neurology disposition that the painful-foot phenomena might be completely separate / neuropathic yet remained "Not clear"?**

# 8. Documentary / informational causation

Regardless of the later recipient's precise subjective understanding, the discharge placed a specialist-authored explanatory possibility into the longitudinal record.

The potential informational sequence is:

> integrated feet–gait–tone referral → specialist identification of Parkinsonism and dystonic-looking lower-limb sign → separate / neuropathy branch introduced for painful feet → branch becomes available for later clinical inheritance and management

If expert evidence establishes that the separate branch was insufficiently earned, the potential causal issue is not merely a missed July diagnosis. It is whether the discharge **changed the information environment** inherited by the GP, patient and later clinicians by making an inadequately investigated relationship appear to have reached a specialist endpoint.

That is documentary/informational causation. It is distinct from medical/legal causation. Any claim that this informational event probably altered later management and caused defined additional harm requires evidence for each subsequent link.

A disciplined causal chain for testing is:

> **integrated referral → inadequate relational investigation, if proved → authoritative specialist partition → downstream reliance/inheritance, if proved → delayed or incomplete reopening, if proved → treatment consequences, if proved → defined additional harm**

No arrow should be assumed.

- the final synthesis introduced complete separation / peripheral neuropathy as possibilities; and

The Protest therefore remains a separate **decision-after-notice** route, not a necessary foundation for the documentary-disposition route.

# 10. Questions for independent movement-disorders expert

The July issue should now be tested through precise questions rather than rhetorical conclusions:

1. Given the referral and July findings, what focused lower-limb / foot examination did reasonable movement-disorders practice in 2017 require before a painful-foot problem could properly be left separate, neuropathic, mixed or "Not clear"?
2. What objective clinical findings would ordinarily support, weaken or leave open a peripheral-neuropathy explanation in that setting?
3. What examination was required to distinguish true distal weakness, pseudo-footdrop, dystonic posturing, fixed structural deformity and mixed neurological-mechanical loading?
4. What work is affirmatively demonstrated by the surviving July record, what can reasonably be inferred, and what cannot safely be inferred?
5. Does the documented July assessment provide a clinically sufficient basis for introducing the possibility that the painful-foot phenomena were "completely separate" from the Parkinsonian/dystonic process?
6. What significance should be attached to the fact that the dystonic-looking lower-limb feature was used in the DaTscan/young-onset Parkinsonian formulation but is not visibly integrated into the final painful-foot disposition?
7. What significance, if any, should be attached to the same neurologist's later 2020 use of dorsiflexion, plantarflexion, inversion and eversion testing when resolving a similar footdrop-like appearance?
8. If the July assessment was incomplete, would the appropriate specialist conclusion have been substantive uncertainty, or should the record instead have identified the relationship as requiring further examination/investigation/follow-up?

For each answer, distinguish:

- what reasonable practice required in **2017**;
- what is proved by the record;
- what is a reasonable inference;
- what remains unknown because of documentation/disclosure limits; and
- what additional source would materially change the opinion.

# 11. Control propositions

The focused July position is:

> **An integrated feet–gait–tone problem reached Neurology. Neurology substantially investigated and diagnosed the Parkinsonian component, identified a footdrop-like lower-limb phenomenon as potentially dystonic, and used dystonic gait language in the DaTscan diagnostic reasoning. The recorded July distal-foot material is comparatively limited. The final specialist synthesis nevertheless affirmatively introduced a branch in which the painful-foot phenomena might be completely separate or secondary peripheral neuropathy and concluded "Not clear." The expert question is what work earned that branch and that uncertainty.**

Shortest form:

> **The limb was neurologically meaningful when it supplied diagnostic evidence; the unresolved question is what clinical work justified treating the painful foot attached to that presentation as potentially separate.**

## Evidential limits

Do not convert this document into allegations that:

- every foot symptom was dystonia;
- structural pathology was unreal;
- peripheral neuropathy was impossible;
- the July Parkinsonian diagnosis was wrong;
- undocumented examination definitely did not occur;
- the 2020 examination defines the 2017 standard;
- "Not clear" is negligent merely as wording;
- the GP in fact interpreted the document in a particular way without evidence; or
- the July document alone proves downstream medical causation.

The evidential task is narrower and stronger: **identify what the record affirmatively shows, identify the explanatory branch Neurology affirmatively created, and ask what clinical work justified it.**
