# 18 — Protest Emergence and Institutional Conditions

## Purpose

This file distinguishes four different evidential questions that must not be collapsed:

1. **When did the underlying bedside Protest allegedly occur?**
2. **When does the patient's later recollection of having made that Protest first appear in surviving material?**
3. **When was that remembered event first put to Galway University Hospital/HSE?**
4. **Under what institutional conditions did each appearance occur?**

The timing of later mention is not self-interpreting. A fact omitted from an earlier document is only meaningfully negative if that document or occasion was one in which the fact would naturally be expected to appear.

The governing distinction is:

> **silence ≠ inconsistency**

and

> **late institutional significance ≠ late memory creation.**

---

# 1. Alleged bedside event — Monday 10 July 2017

The patient's current supplementary account places the event on **Monday 10 July 2017**, after he had been told that discharge would occur the following day.

He says four medical students were present at the bedside; he asked them to get Dr Thomas Monaghan because he needed to speak about his feet before discharge; Monaghan attended; and the patient used the “full NCT / bald tyres” analogy to challenge discharge without the painful foot problem having, in his view, been meaningfully investigated.

## Institutional condition

This was, on the patient's account, **not a complaint, grievance, regulatory submission or medico-legal allegation**.

It occurred:

- during an inpatient admission;
- while the treating relationship was active;
- immediately before planned discharge;
- in a teaching environment involving medical students;
- before the patient possessed the later discharge synthesis or its documentary architecture;
- before any later complaint, DPC or legal theory existed.

Its claimed purpose was practical and clinical:

> **do not discharge me while the feet that brought me here remain unresolved.**

If accepted, its first significance is therefore as a **decision-live clinical communication**, not as an accusation.

---

# 2. 2017–early 2025 — no demonstrated institutional use of the Protest

The correspondence review has not identified a surviving email in which the patient deployed the Protest between July 2017 and the 2025 complaint period.

That finding must be used carefully.

It does **not** establish that the patient forgot the event, never mentioned it privately, or that it did not occur.

Nor is there presently a demonstrated reason why the patient would have treated the exchange as a complaint issue during years in which he strongly accepted the Parkinsonian diagnosis, experienced major treatment benefit and continued to trust the treating neurologist.

The relevant absence is therefore:

> **no presently identified institutionalisation of the event as a grievance.**

That is different from evidential proof of non-occurrence.

---

# 3. 19 March 2025 — first formal hospital complaint does not use the Protest

The patient's original hospital complaint was sent to GUH/HSE on **19 March 2025**.

It focused on diagnostic anchoring, alcohol-related neuropathy, failure to reassess and the significance then attributed to Sinemet CR response. It expressly acknowledged that the patient did not yet have access to every internal note, referral or correspondence and sought disclosure of records.

The bedside Protest was not identified in that complaint.

## Institutional condition

At this stage:

- the patient had newly reinterpreted the longitudinal history after treatment response;
- he was entering the hospital complaints system for the first time on the present dispute;
- he had not yet reconstructed the complete documentary genealogy;
- he was requesting records precisely because he did not know the whole institutional record.

## Evidential significance

This cuts against any simple claim that the Protest was a rehearsed allegation used to launch the complaint.

It does **not** prove the later recollection true.

It does show that a potentially rhetorically powerful event was **not** initially used when the complaint first entered the institution.

---

# 4. 18 April 2025 — detailed post-response supplement still does not use the Protest

After receiving the 15 April complaint response and Dr Monaghan's response, the patient wrote a detailed supplement on **18 April 2025** asking that unresolved questions be kept on the complaint file.

That document addressed the diagnosis, pain, Sinemet CR, peripheral neuropathy, alcohol, lack of independent review, complaint closure, therapeutic contradiction and institutional process.

The Protest was still not used.

## Institutional condition

The patient was now actively criticising the hospital's handling of the complaint and had a clear incentive to identify matters he considered institutionally relevant.

Yet the 2017 bedside exchange had not become part of the surviving complaint presentation.

## Evidential significance

This again does not prove absence of memory.

It suggests that, even after the first institutional response, the Protest had not yet become a consciously central evidential object in the surviving correspondence.

The distinction to preserve is:

> **the event may have been remembered before it was understood as evidence.**

---

# 5. Late April–May 2025 — record acquisition and procedural conflict change the information environment

By late May, correspondence shows that the patient had begun obtaining internal material independently and comparing it with the hospital's handling of the complaint.

On 28 May he wrote that his original March complaint had not been “evidenced” in legal terms because he had lacked access to internal documentation, and that he had subsequently obtained records independently through FOI.

The complaint process itself had also become contested: clinical-judgment issues had been routed through QPS, Monaghan's response had been supplied, and the patient was challenging how the matter had been classified and processed.

## Institutional condition

This is the first clear transition from:

> **I suffered harm and think the diagnosis was wrong**

into:

> **what exactly does the institutional record say happened, and how was my case represented?**

That change in information environment is critical to understanding when the Protest becomes salient.

---

# 6. 6 June 2025 — earliest surviving later reference to the Protest found in Gmail

The earliest source-verified later articulation identified in the Gmail review is **6 June 2025**, in the PDF *A diagnosis of a diagnosis* sent to the **Patient Advocacy Service**.

The relevant passage says, in substance, that the patient wonders whether Monaghan remembers him mentioning the foot pain on the last day in hospital; recalls asking four trainee doctors to bring Monaghan to him; and recalls using the vivid “full NCT / bald tyres” formulation.

## Institutional condition

This is significant because the first surviving later reference was:

- **not addressed to GUH/HSE as an allegation**;
- sent to an **independent patient-advocacy service**;
- produced after the complaint and first hospital response already existed;
- produced after documentary/FOI reconstruction had begun;
- part of a reflective attempt to answer: **what do I now think happened, and where were the inflection points?**

The Patient Advocacy Service was at that moment explaining to the patient how the complaint had been routed and who within the hospital was dealing with clinical-judgment issues.

## Evidential significance

The sequence is materially different from a story first invented for a hospital demand.

The event appears first in surviving material during **post-response reconstruction with an external advocate**, before it is formally put to the hospital as a data/provenance matter.

That is not proof of truth. It is relevant to genesis and motive for articulation.

---

# 7. 7 June 2025 — private self-record repeats the recollection but shows date uncertainty

On **7 June 2025** the patient emailed himself a longer formulation titled *If you ask me*.

It again records the “full NCT / bald tyres” exchange and four trainee doctors, but places the direct challenge on the **last day in hospital / Tuesday 11 July**.

## Evidential significance

This is important for two reasons.

First, it independently shows the recollection was being preserved before the July hospital data request.

Second, it demonstrates a **date-detail discrepancy** between the June 2025 reconstruction and the later, more particularised account placing the exchange on Monday 10 July, after notification of next-day discharge.

That discrepancy must remain visible.

It may reflect ordinary reconstruction of the exact date from surrounding chronology, but it cannot simply be erased in the name of consistency.

The stable core across the later accounts is more important than pretending every peripheral detail was fixed from the outset:

- discharge imminent;
- four trainees/students present;
- patient asks for Monaghan;
- concern is unresolved feet;
- vivid NCT/bald-tyres analogy;
- response points toward Podiatry/chiropody.

---

# 8. 7 July 2025 — first source-verified hospital-facing use of the Protest

The first source-verified later communication identified in Gmail that puts the interaction directly to GUH/HSE is the **7 July 2025 Article 15 request** to the GUH Data Protection Officer, copied to education/quality/CIO functions.

The request describes a bedside teaching interaction on or around 10–11 July 2017, states that the patient raised concern that disabling bilateral foot pain had not been addressed, asked a student to fetch the supervising neurologist, used the “full NCT / bald tires” formulation, and recalls the consultant replying that Podiatry was as easy for the patient to obtain as for him.

## Institutional condition

This is crucial.

The Protest was first put to the hospital not primarily as:

> **accept that this happened and admit negligence**

but as:

> **search for the personal data, teaching records, trainee records, ward records and supervisory traces that may show what happened.**

The institutional object had therefore changed from **clinical complaint** to **provenance/access**.

The patient was now asking the information system to test his recollection.

## Evidential significance

That is reciprocal to the Protest's clinical importance.

The event's later significance arose because the patient had begun comparing his lived memory with an institutional record that extensively described his questions, understanding, planning and disposition but did not contain the exchange he remembered.

---

# 9. 24 November 2025 — first source-verified University-facing use

On **24 November 2025** the patient sent a separate GDPR/academic-governance request to University of Galway leadership and its DPO concerning the University-linked bedside teaching interaction.

The attached request particularised the event as occurring in the morning of 10 July 2017 during the arrival of a new cohort of trainees, and sought University-held teaching, supervisory, trainee and academic-governance material.

## Institutional condition

By this stage the event had moved through several institutional frames:

1. bedside clinical communication;
2. later patient recollection during reconstruction;
3. hospital data/provenance request;
4. separate University teaching/data-controller question.

The University-facing request arose only after the teaching dimension itself had become institutionally salient and after the hospital route had failed to produce the sought teaching traces.

The request was expressly framed as concerning a distinct academic/supervisory information system, not merely as another copy of the hospital complaint.

---

# 10. 2 September 2026 — Protest becomes a supplementary-record completeness claim

The 2026 Article 16 supplementary statement represents another transformation.

The patient no longer asks primarily whether teaching traces can be found. He asks that his own account be durably associated with the intact July 2017 specialist synthesis.

The statement expressly says:

- do not delete or rewrite the clinician-authored historical record;
- retain the original;
- preserve the patient's account of the omitted pre-discharge interaction alongside it;
- make the association apparent whenever the historical synthesis is later accessed, reviewed, relied upon, disclosed or otherwise processed.

## Institutional condition

The patient states that he is no longer under GUH/Monaghan care. The purpose has therefore shifted from current treatment to **historical data completeness and future processing**.

The event has moved from a remembered bedside communication to an object of documentary corrigibility.

---

# 11. What the sequence does — and does not — prove

## It does not prove the Protest occurred

The event remains direct retrospective patient evidence. No contemporaneous clinical entry presently identified records it.

## It does not justify treating pre-2025 silence as impeachment

There is no demonstrated reason to expect an unrecognised bedside exchange to have been repeatedly narrated while the patient accepted the neurological diagnosis, trusted the clinician and did not yet understand the later documentary dispute.

## It does make the genesis of the later account testable

The surviving sequence is:

> **2017 alleged clinical protest → March 2025 complaint without Protest → April 2025 detailed complaint supplement without Protest → record acquisition / institutional response → 6 June first surviving explicit recollection to Patient Advocacy Service → 7 June self-record → 7 July first hospital provenance request about the event → November separate University teaching/governance request → September 2026 supplementary-record statement.**

That genealogy matters.

The strongest fair inference is not “late allegation” or “contemporaneous complaint”. It is:

> **A bedside event which had not been institutionalised as a grievance became evidentially salient only after the patient began reconstructing the documentary history and asking why the authoritative record represented the feet and discharge as it did.**

Whether that reconstruction recovered a genuine old memory or generated a mistaken later memory remains a fact question.

But the institutional conditions of emergence are relevant to assessing both possibilities.

---

# 12. Credibility and reciprocal importance

The Protest should be assessed reciprocally.

If its absence from 2017–early-2025 complaint discourse is said to weaken it, equal attention must be paid to the conditions under which it first appears:

- it did not launch the complaint;
- it was not used in the first detailed post-response supplement;
- it first appears in surviving material to an external patient advocate during documentary reconstruction;
- it was then put to the hospital as something to **search and verify**, rather than simply as a demanded admission;
- only later did it become a formal completeness/supplementary-data claim.

This sequence may be relevant to whether the account looks like strategic allegation, recovered significance, ordinary autobiographical reconstruction, or some mixture of these.

The repository must not decide that question by rhetoric.

It should preserve the sequence so that a fact-finder can decide it from evidence.

---

# Control proposition

> **The evidential importance of the Protest lies not only in when the patient says it occurred, but in the institutional conditions under which it later became speakable as evidence. The surviving correspondence does not show eight years of repeated complaint about the 10 July exchange. It shows years in which the event was not institutionalised, followed by a 2025 transition from treatment-response complaint to documentary reconstruction, then first surviving articulation to an independent advocate, then a hospital provenance search, then a separate University teaching-governance inquiry, and finally a supplementary-record completeness request. That genealogy neither proves nor disproves the Protest; it materially improves the conditions under which its credibility can be assessed.**
