# 1. Controlling analytical proposition

> **A clinically important neurological lower-limb relationship was positively raised by the contemporaneous record. What remains unresolved is whether it was adequately characterised, integrated with genuine structural pathology, and later reconstructed when further evidence repeatedly crossed the boundary.**

This is stronger than saying there merely “might” have been a relationship, but narrower than saying that all foot pain was dystonia or that July 2017 care was necessarily negligent.

The case does **not** depend on proving:

- that the Parkinsonism diagnosis was wrong;
- that all foot pathology was neurological;
- that “Not clear” was itself negligent;
- that later PRKN genetics proves historical breach;
- or that an undocumented examination did not occur.

The central 2017 question remains:

> **What clinical work earned the separation?**

And the refined version is:

> **Was the resolution of the lower-limb phenotype congruent with the sophistication and consequence of the final specialist synthesis?**

---

# 2. Integrated phenotype

The clinical object that entered Neurology was relational:

> **feet + gait + tone + movement initiation + pain + toe posture + function.**

The GP expressly sought neurological opinion on “gait, feet and ‘muscle tension’ issues.” The urgent referral carried the same integration forward through abnormal gait, initiation difficulty, marked tone/stiffness, inability to relax and toe clawing.

The first discipline is therefore:

> **Do not import the later partition backward into the referral state.**

The patient's later distinction between foot pain and leg tension is real and must be preserved. But a phenomenological distinction does not itself establish causal independence.

---

# 3. Neurology itself generated the lower-limb neurological question

Neurology did not merely diagnose Parkinsonism.

It documented:

- markedly increased tone;
- gross bradykinesia;
- a “strikingly abnormal” gait;
- a footdrop-like appearance “somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture — NOT FOOT DROP”;
- a left lower limb that “kicks out like a foot drop”;
- pes planus;
- hammertoes;
- and later, in the formal DAT indication, a walk that was “almost dystonically unusual.”

The factual significance is affirmative:

> **A neurologically meaningful lower-limb phenomenon was positively recognised by the treating team itself.**

“NOT FOOT DROP” cuts both ways. It may support the defence inference that some motor discrimination occurred. But it also moves the observed phenomenon away from ordinary weakness and toward abnormal movement/posturing, sharpening the need to relate that phenomenon to toe clawing, structure, loading and pain.

---

# 4. The founding separation

The final synthesis organised the clinical object into partially separate channels:

- established Parkinsonism;
- painful feet/corns/callouses potentially “completely separate”;
- possible peripheral neuropathy;
- “Not clear”;
- Podiatry.

The argument is not that this allocation was necessarily wrong.

It is that **“completely separate” was a substantive working allocation even though tentative**. It carried possible consequences for ownership, referral and later interpretation.

The distinction to preserve is:

**Open uncertainty:** “We have not established how these things relate.”

**Organised uncertainty:** “Parkinsonism is established; feet may be separate or neuropathic; relationship not clear.”

The second can be clinically proper. But it becomes portable.

---

# 5. The synthesis–examination differential

The 2017 neurological work was substantial. That must remain part of our own case.

Gait was observed and videoed. Tone/reflexes were examined. The pseudo-footdrop appearance was recognised. Structural feet were recorded. The admission was prolonged. The clinician later wrote that the stay allowed the team to “get a lot done,” “explore a lot of questions” and gain a “firmer footing.” The final account also contains sophisticated very-young-onset, recessive and PRKN/PARK2 reasoning.

That positive work is not merely a defence point. It creates an internal evidential comparator.

The surviving record does not clearly show equally explicit resolution of:

- distal lower-limb power;
- directed sensory findings relevant to neuropathy;
- ankle-specific reflexes;
- fixed versus dynamic foot/toe posture;
- passive correctability;
- patterned dystonia;
- structure–loading–posture–pain relationship.

The correct proposition is **not** “those examinations were not done.”

It is:

> **The more comprehensive the surviving specialist account, the less satisfactory it is simply to presume that the omitted discriminative bridge existed.**

That is an evidential discrepancy requiring expert assessment, not proof of negligence by silence.

# 6. Duty

The duty theory concerns **discrimination**, not diagnostic certainty.

Once structural, dystonic-looking, Parkinsonian and neuropathic possibilities were live, the expert question is what reasonable 2017 neurological practice required before residual uncertainty could properly be exported.

Potential discriminators include:

- distal motor examination;
- sensory examination;
- relevant reflex testing;
- fixed/dynamic/passively correctable posture;
- movement-state behaviour;
- loading mechanics;
- relation between structure, pain and abnormal movement.

Whether each was mandatory is an expert question.

---

# 7. Podiatry and ownership

Podiatry was a legitimate destination for genuine structural disease.

It could appropriately own:

- corns/callouses;
- focal pressure lesions;
- footwear;
- offloading;
- local mechanics.

But it could not by itself answer:

- true weakness versus dystonic posturing;
- central motor contribution;
- whether objective evidence supported neuropathy;
- whether abnormal neurological loading was contributing to structural pathology.

Thus:

> **Podiatry may be appropriate while neurological ownership of the relationship remains unresolved.**

The first Podiatry visit is particularly important: the patient reported that his gait had been very bad but was no longer so while Podiatry simultaneously documented a left fifth-MTPJ callus, bilateral toe props and tight gastrocnemius/soleus.

The missing-join argument therefore begins early, not only years later.

# 8. Protest fork — conditional only

If proved, the Protest is simply:

> **“You have not dealt with my feet.”**

It is not a retrospective theory of dystonia, PRKN or the later documentary synthesis.

If adequate relationship-directed work had already occurred, the reasonable response may have been explanation and a defined pathway.

If it had not occurred, the patient was still available for clarification or focused re-examination.

Therefore:

> **The Protest changes decision status, not the underlying facts.**

It adds force if proved but is not necessary to the wider architecture.

# 9. September 2017 — independent PARK2 threshold

This threshold was previously underweighted.

Prof Daniel Healy wrote that he thought the patient **“probably has Park II”** and suggested testing PARK II first. He also recorded dramatic improvement in motor symptoms on procyclidine and rasagiline.

This matters because PARK2 was no longer merely one theoretical gene in a broad list. By later 2017 it had become an independently favoured contemporary diagnostic hypothesis.

At roughly the same stage, Podiatry was observing real mechanical pathology against an improved gait state.

The correct expert question is:

> **Did the emerging PARK2 phenotype, neurological improvement and simultaneous structural pathology require renewed attention to the unresolved lower-limb relationship?**

This does not import 2026 genetics backward.

---

# 10. October 2017–April 2018 — positive reopening signal

During planned procyclidine withdrawal, the contemporaneous record shows tension building, feet becoming restless and the walk deteriorating together.

In April 2018 the treating neurologist himself recorded that the outside-left-foot phenomenon was the first thing to change rapidly during withdrawal, with greater “shiftiness” and an “unrelaxed” state.

The correct formulation is stronger than “this may have been relevant.”

> **A new neurological-treatment-state observation involved the feet and required clinical interpretation against the earlier separation hypothesis.**

Whether reasonable practice required formal reassessment is expert-dependent.

The observation itself is not speculative.

---

# 11. Structural pathology as complementary truth

Podiatry repeatedly documented real focal pathology, including recurrent left fifth-MTPJ lesions, clawing, pronation, offloading and benefit from local treatment.

That evidence should not be minimised.

The medically realistic model is:

> **independent structural pathology, neurologically influenced structure/loading, or mixed mechanism.**

Therefore:

> **Structural pathology is not structural independence.**

The structural evidence prevents an “all neurological” claim and actually strengthens the mixed-mechanism case.

---

# 12. The missing join

The record contains positive information from both sides:

**Podiatry:** recurrent focal mechanical pathology and offloading response.

**Neurology/treatment state:** feet, gait and tension changing with treatment state; later OFF dystonia, OFF-related leg pain/shuffling and dopaminergic modulation.

The strongest formulation is:

> **The longitudinal record repeatedly demonstrates a mixed-information phenotype: one branch establishes mechanical pathology while another establishes neurological state-dependence. The surviving record does not clearly show a durable reconstruction joining those streams.**

This remains a record-wide inference, not proof that no clinician ever thought about the relationship.

Abstract terms such as “information hazard,” “pathway lock-in” and “self-validating continuity” are secondary explanatory tools. They are not needed to carry the case and should not displace the direct clinical chronology.

---

# 13. 2019 reopening evidence

By 2019 primary care was expressly relating feet issues to increased tone and abnormal gait relating to Parkinson's disease and separately recording young-onset Parkinson's with “clawing feet” and pain.

That matters because the boundary crossing did not suddenly appear in 2023.

The expert question is whether this earlier re-integration should have triggered specialist reconstruction.

---

# 14. 2023–2025 — neurological relevance becomes affirmative

By 2023 the neurological record itself was considering lower-limb ON/OFF dystonia, with OFF dystonia considered more likely.

Later in 2023 leg pain and shuffling were assessed as OFF-related and dopaminergic treatment was changed.

In 2024 pregabalin could improve foot pain while toe curling persisted, demonstrating that components could dissociate.

By 2025 the treating neurologist expressly acknowledged that the feet felt better when Sinemet took effect, predicted some additional pain relief from more Sinemet, and balanced that against worsening dyskinesia.

The stronger factual proposition is:

> **By 2023–2025 the treating service itself regarded at least some lower-limb phenomena as dystonic, OFF-related, dyskinetic or dopaminergically modulated.**

That does not establish that the focal pain generator was dystonia. It does establish more than mere speculative neurological relevance.

# 15. 2023 safety route

The 2023 record contains severe bilateral foot/lower-leg pain, urgent GP concern, “death wish” language, “no quality of life,” mobility restriction and medication escalation.

This is a separate contemporary question:

> **Was the combined pain–mobility–medication–psychological state adequately recognised and managed as one safety object?**

Do not infer continuous suicidality beyond the words actually recorded.

Do not use 2023 severity as proof by analogy of 2017 breach.

---

# 16. PRKN genetics

The temporal firewall remains absolute for historical standard of care.

Later genetics cannot establish that:

- clinicians should have known the molecular diagnosis in July 2017;
- every painful foot symptom was dystonia;
- structural pathology was caused by PRKN;
- later confirmation proves historical breach.

But the later evidence does more than supply generic plausibility.

CeGaT identifies pathogenic PRKN variants and describes lower-limb dystonia as characteristic of PARK2, sometimes isolated for years. St Vincent's later clinically attributes **both Parkinsonism and dystonia** to compound-heterozygous Parkin mutations.

The lawful formulation is:

> **The eventual disease-level diagnosis positively unifies Parkinsonism and dystonia within PRKN disease, making the earlier coexistence of very-young-onset Parkinsonism and dystonic-looking lower-limb phenomenology retrospectively coherent without altering the 2017 knowledge state.**

# 17. Harm

The underlying PRKN disease and genuine structural foot pathology are not caused by any alleged breach.

The potentially attributable harm is only the incremental burden, if any, caused by a failure to characterise, integrate or later reconstruct the lower-limb problem when reasonable care would probably have done so.

Distinguish:

> **Baseline harm** — what would have occurred with ideal care because of underlying disease and structure.

from

> **Incremental avoidable harm** — what probably would have been prevented, shortened or reduced absent the proved breach.

The record strongly establishes later severity. It does not itself establish how much was avoidable.

---

# 18. Causation

Causation remains the least secure part of the case and must not be rhetorically upgraded merely because the breach/reopening evidence is stronger.

For each breach actually found:

1. What should have happened?
2. What would proper assessment probably have shown?
3. What management would probably have changed?
4. What later burden would probably have been avoided or reduced?
5. What would have occurred anyway?

Treatment response is mechanistically non-specific. Structural pathology was genuine. Early neurological treatment produced major functional improvement. Cautious levodopa use had a documented rationale, and later dyskinesia demonstrates a real therapeutic constraint.

Those facts belong in the causation analysis.

---

# 19. Strongest defence — preserved intentionally

The case is stronger when it affirmatively retains the defence:

- severe Parkinsonism was correctly identified;
- structural foot disease was real;
- the patient distinguished foot pain from leg tension;
- gait was directly observed and videoed;
- “NOT FOOT DROP” may imply meaningful motor discrimination;
- possible dystonic posture was recognised;
- tone and reflexes were examined;
- the admission and investigation were substantial;
- Podiatry referral was sensible;
- “may,” “perhaps” and “Not clear” were cautious rather than dogmatic;
- treatment responses do not prove one mechanism;
- levodopa restraint had a genuine clinical rationale;
- later dyskinesia created a real treatment constraint.

The vulnerability after conceding all of that is narrower:

> **How much of the necessary discriminative bridge is affirmatively demonstrated, and how much must be inferred?**

# 22. Finite argument set and correction rule

The case is now finite enough to freeze against speculative proliferation.

The freeze does **not** prevent correction where:

- new primary evidence is supplied;
- a canonical CLIN entry was materially overlooked or underused;
- an independent expert resolves an open clinical hinge;
- adversarial scrutiny shows that a formulation overstates or understates the evidence.

What is frozen is theory generation detached from the canonical record.

# 23. Final case statement

> **An integrated lower-limb problem entered Neurology. Neurology correctly identified a major Parkinsonian syndrome and, on its own examination, positively identified a dystonic-looking pseudo-footdrop phenomenon alongside genuine structural foot disease. The final synthesis placed the painful foot/leg phenomena into a working branch where they might be completely separate or neuropathic and were “Not clear.” The central July question is whether sufficient phenotype-directed clinical discrimination occurred to justify that organisation. The surviving record cannot prove that it did not, but its exceptional detail makes the missing discriminative bridge a genuine evidential issue requiring explanation and expert assessment. Independently, by September 2017 PARK2 had become an externally favoured contemporary hypothesis, and subsequent treatment-state, mechanical, OFF/dystonia, toe-curling and dopaminergic-response evidence repeatedly made the lower-limb relationship clinically salient and may have created fresh duties to reconstruct it. Later genetics supplies disease-level retrospective coherence, not retrospective breach. Harm and causation remain confined to the incremental burden that proper assessment or later reconstruction would probably have avoided or reduced.**

The shortest durable formulation is:

> **The neurological relationship was positively visible; the unresolved question is whether it was adequately characterised, owned and reconstructed when reasonable practice required it.**
