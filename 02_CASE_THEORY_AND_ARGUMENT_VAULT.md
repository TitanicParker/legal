**Repository:** `TitanicParker/legal`  
**Status:** Analytical working authority — not primary evidence  
**Purpose:** Preserve the strongest case formulations, distinctions, counterarguments, and evidential uses developed across the project so they are not lost as the litigation/expert bundle is simplified.

This document is deliberately separate from the canonical clinical record. It does not convert analysis into fact. Where a proposition depends on expert opinion, disputed patient evidence, or a later evidential period, that dependency is stated.

# 1. Controlling formulation

> **In July 2017 an integrated feet–gait–tone presentation generated, on Neurology's own examination, both severe young-onset Parkinsonism and a dystonic-looking lower-limb phenomenon alongside genuine structural foot disease. The final specialist synthesis allowed the painful foot/leg phenomena to be separate or neuropathic and ultimately “Not clear.” The surviving record demonstrates substantial neurological work, but it does not clearly demonstrate the focused discriminative examination that would show how those competing lower-limb mechanisms were separated. The central question is whether that work occurred and, if not, what reasonable practice required at the time.**

This is the centre of gravity of the case.

It is intentionally narrower than:

- “Neurology missed dystonia.”
- “The feet were really neurological.”
- “The Parkinson's diagnosis was wrong.”
- “Not clear was negligent.”
- “Later PRKN genetics proves what should have been known in 2017.”

Those formulations are either too broad, hindsight-dependent, or unnecessary.

The controlling formulation asks a more ascertainable question:

> **What clinical work earned the separation?**

Or, in its more refined form:

> **Was the sophistication and consequence of the final specialist formulation congruent with the scope and depth of the documented bedside examination of the painful and dystonic-looking lower limb?**

---

# 2. What the formulation is designed to establish

The formulation has five jobs.

## 2.1 Preserve the clinical object as it actually entered care

The feet must not be retrospectively detached from the neurological referral.

The contemporaneous sequence was not “Parkinsonism plus unrelated corns.” It was an evolving object involving:

- bilateral painful/abnormal feet;
- abnormal gait;
- movement-initiation difficulty;
- increasing tone and stiffness;
- toe clawing / inability to relax;
- functional deterioration.

Primary care ultimately sought Neurology's opinion on the **gait, feet and “muscle tension” issues**.

The first analytical rule is therefore:

> **Do not import the later partition backward into the referral state.**

The body entered as an integrated clinical problem.

## 2.2 Identify the point at which the relationship became a neurological question

Neurology did not merely discover Parkinsonism.

It also documented:

- markedly increased tone;
- gross bradykinesia;
- a “strikingly abnormal” gait;
- a footdrop-like appearance “somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture — NOT FOOT DROP”;
- pes planus;
- hammertoes.

Once structural foot pathology and a dystonic-looking neurological lower-limb phenomenon coexist in the same examination, their relationship becomes clinically material.

The issue is not whether one must displace the other.

> **Structural truth and neurological contribution are not mutually exclusive.**

The relevant possibilities include structural, neurological, neuropathic, compensatory, and mixed mechanisms.

## 2.3 Separate uncertainty from the work needed to reach uncertainty

“Not clear” is not the breach.

A competent specialist can properly reach uncertainty after adequate examination.

The question is whether the record demonstrates the discriminative clinical work by which the uncertainty was reached.

That distinction is essential:

> **The issue is not whether Neurology was entitled to remain uncertain. The issue is whether the surviving record demonstrates the phenotype-directed clinical work by which that uncertainty was earned.**

## 2.4 Turn an accusation into an ascertainable expert question

The case should not assume that an undocumented examination did not happen.

There is positive evidence in Neurology's favour:

- direct gait observation;
- video recording;
- explicit “NOT FOOT DROP”;
- recognition of possible dystonic posture;
- tone and reflex examination;
- structural foot findings;
- extensive young-onset Parkinsonism work-up;
- physiotherapy / occupational therapy;
- Podiatry referral;
- complex-case discussion.

The evidential question is whether that material proves, implies, or leaves unproved the focused discriminators relevant to the feet–gait–tone relationship.

The likely factual/expert hinge includes:

- distal lower-limb power;
- sensory examination;
- ankle-specific reflex findings;
- fixed versus dynamic foot/toe posture;
- passive correctability;
- patterned dystonic posturing;
- gait/loading mechanics;
- relationship between pain, posture and movement.

If adequate work is later demonstrated, the initial 2017 case can narrow. If it is not, the issue becomes sharper.

That makes the theory falsifiable rather than rhetorical.

## 2.5 Allow the later evidence to matter without contaminating 2017

Later evidence is not used to decide what clinicians should have known in July 2017.

It is used for separate purposes:

- retrospective biological plausibility;
- reopening of an earlier boundary;
- treatment-state evidence;
- later duty to reconsider;
- harm;
- counterfactual causation.

That separation is the temporal firewall.

# 3. The founding separation

The founding argument is not that the final specialist account falsely described the patient.

It is that the account **organised the uncertainty**.

Before specialist closure, the clinical object was relational:

> feet + gait + tone + movement initiation + pain + toe posture.

The completed account organised it into channels:

- established Parkinsonism;
- painful feet / corns / callouses potentially “completely separate”;
- possible peripheral neuropathy;
- “Not clear”;
- Podiatry.

That organisation may have been clinically justified.

But the record must be examined for the bridge by which it was justified.

The most economical question is:

> **What examination or clinical reasoning bridges the bedside object to the later allocation?**

The project should preserve the distinction between:

**open uncertainty**  
“We have not established how these phenomena relate.”

and

**organised uncertainty**  
“Parkinsonism is established; the feet may be separate / neuropathic; relationship not clear.”

The latter is not automatically improper. Its importance is that it becomes portable.

---

# 4. The synthesis–examination differential

One of the strongest internal comparators is the difference in resolution between the etiological synthesis and the phenotype construction.

The completed account contains sophisticated reasoning about:

- severe very-young-onset Parkinsonism;
- atypical features;
- symmetry;
- long duration;
- recessive inheritance;
- PRKN/PARK2 and other monogenic possibilities;
- complex-case discussion.

By contrast, the surviving record contains much less explicit resolution of the lower-limb phenomenon that complicates the presentation.

This yields the proposition:

> **The synthesis may have outrun the examination.**

Or more neutrally:

> **Was the evidential foundation visible at the bedside proportionate to the sophistication and consequences of the formulation later produced from it?**

This is not a criticism of sophisticated diagnostic reasoning.

It asks why the record reaches high resolution on **why the Parkinsonism might exist** while leaving lower resolution on **what the unusual painful lower-limb phenomenon consisted of**.

The strongest version is not “poor documentation.”

It is:

> **Where is the clinical bridge?**

---

# 5. Duty

The duty theory should not be framed as a duty to diagnose PRKN or necessarily to diagnose dystonia in July 2017.

It is narrower:

> **Once the presentation itself raised competing explanations for the same lower-limb phenomenon, reasonable neurological assessment had to address that relationship to an appropriate clinical level.**

The duty concerns discrimination, not certainty.

The key historical expert question is:

> **What would reasonable neurological practice in 2017 have required before allowing the painful feet / abnormal lower limb to remain separate, neuropathic or “Not clear”?**

This includes a second ownership question:

> **Which parts properly belonged to Podiatry, and which neurological arrows still required neurological ownership?**

Podiatry can properly own:

- callus;
- focal pressure lesions;
- footwear;
- offloading;
- structural mechanics.

It cannot by itself determine:

- weakness versus dystonic posturing;
- neurological treatment-state dependence;
- central motor contribution;
- whether a neuropathy hypothesis has clinical support;
- whether structural pathology is independent of abnormal neurological loading.

Thus:

> **Podiatry may be appropriate while neurological ownership of the relationship remains unresolved.**

---

# 6. Breach

The candidate breach is conditional.

It is not:

> “They wrote ‘Not clear,’ therefore they breached duty.”

It is:

> **If reasonable practice required focused discrimination of the feet–gait–tone relationship, was that work performed before the relationship was allowed to settle as separate / neuropathic / “Not clear”?**

Three evidential categories must remain separate:

### A. Work affirmatively documented

Examples include:

- gait observed;
- gait videoed;
- tone assessed;
- reflexes assessed;
- pseudo-footdrop appearance recognised;
- dystonic posture considered;
- pes planus and hammertoes documented;
- extensive Parkinsonism work-up.

### B. Work that may reasonably be implicit

For example, “NOT FOOT DROP” may imply some clinical consideration of weakness.

The case must not pretend that such inference has no evidential value.

### C. Work for which the surviving record provides no clear evidence

This is where the potentially decisive phenotype discrimination lies.

The legal/medical significance of C is expert-dependent.

Absence of documentation is not proof of absence of care.

The correct proposition is:

> **The surviving record does not itself demonstrate the complete discriminative bridge.**

# 7. The Protest fork

**Status: conditional patient evidence unless independently corroborated.**

The Protest is not:

- a retrospective theory of dystonia;
- an objection to PRKN;
- an objection to “Not clear”;
- a criticism of a discharge note the patient had not yet seen.

Its epistemic content is simpler:

> **“You have not dealt with my feet.”**

Its importance lies in timing.

The clean three-box chronology is:

### Box 1 — Pre-Protest

Integrated GP referral + inpatient neurological work-up.

### Box 2 — Protest

Express notice that, from the patient's perspective, a major component of the presenting problem remained clinically unfinished.

### Box 3 — Post-Protest synthesis

The later portable specialist account organises the same sequence into Parkinsonism + potentially separate/neuropathic feet + “Not clear” + Podiatry.

The Protest changes **decision status**, not the underlying clinical facts.

If proved, the question becomes:

> **What, if anything, occurred after express notice of incompleteness before the final account was allowed to settle?**

The strongest defence must remain preserved:

- if a proper discriminative examination had already occurred, explanation plus Podiatry / follow-up may have been sufficient;
- if it had not occurred, the patient was still available for clarification or focused re-examination.

Therefore the Protest is a distinct **decision-after-notice** route, not a shortcut to proving the earlier admission inadequate.

# 8. The meaning of Podiatry

Podiatry must never be treated as evidence that structural pathology was imaginary or irrelevant.

It was a legitimate destination.

Its deeper evidential significance is different:

> **Did Podiatry receive a structural problem only, or did it receive a defined question about the relationship between structure, loading, abnormal tone and dystonic-looking movement?**

The later record gives us an especially economical systems formulation:

> **Podiatry knew where it was; Neurology knew it changed with neurological treatment; the record does not show the join.**

This is not proof that no join occurred.

It is a documentary question about whether the complementary streams were ever globally recombined.

The left fifth-MTPJ territory can be treated as an anatomical tracer through the fragmented record because Podiatry repeatedly localises and manages the same mechanical territory while neurological treatment-state evidence later affects a foot phenomenon in that domain.

---

# 9. The durable synthesis

The discharge/clinic synthesis should be treated as a clinical construction, not merely a neutral container.

Its relevance is that it allocates:

- explanatory force;
- diagnostic priority;
- uncertainty;
- future ownership;
- follow-up direction.

The phrase **“completely separate”** presents full separation as a live possibility.

It does not establish separation.

The phrase **“Not clear”** records uncertainty.

It does not by itself create:

- an owner;
- a test;
- a return mechanism;
- an active programme of resolution.

The phrase **“markedly divorced”** is analytically important because clinician-authored language may intensify a more limited patient distinction between “tension” and foot pain and make the separation appear partly patient-authored.

That must be handled carefully. The record supports that the patient distinguished experiences. It does not automatically establish that the patient endorsed full causal independence.

---

# 10. Phenotypic displacement

A particularly strong analytical observation is:

> **The dystonic-looking lower-limb sign survives descriptively while losing explanatory force.**

It is present in the record.

But the subsequent foot allocation does not visibly deploy it to reconstruct:

- the painful feet;
- structural abnormalities;
- PRKN possibility;
- abnormal loading;
- gait.

This is not proof the sign was ignored mentally.

It is a documentary observation about what survives into the final reasoning.

# 11. Post-discharge boundary tests

The later evidence should be arranged by what it tests.

## 11.1 Procyclidine / treatment-state response

Post-discharge improvement and withdrawal evidence is not retrospective proof of breach.

Its significance is that phenomena on the supposedly separate side of the boundary later change with neurological treatment state.

The safest formulation is:

> **A phenomenon previously left on the foot side of the boundary showed dynamic neurological treatment-state behaviour.**

The April 2018 phrase concerning the “return ... of the outside left corn” contains temporal information:

- prior presence;
- intervening improvement/absence;
- recurrence during withdrawal.

It does not prove the mechanism of the corn.

It does show clinician-authored awareness of dynamic change.

This is a **boundary test**, not a retrospective diagnostic verdict.

## 11.2 Reopening duty

Even if the July 2017 assessment was reasonable, later evidence can create a new question:

> **At what point did accumulating treatment-state evidence make the original separation due for reconsideration?**

This is an independent breach route.

The case does not need the initial July allegation to succeed before later failures to reconsider can be examined.

---

# 12. Podiatry as complementary truth

The Podiatry record is not contrary evidence to be minimised.

It is part of the correct model.

It repeatedly demonstrates:

- genuine structural pathology;
- recurrent focal loading;
- clawing;
- fifth-MTPJ involvement;
- need for offloading / insoles / debridement.

That supports a mixed-mechanism model.

The correct question is not:

> neurological **or** structural?

It is:

> independent structural pathology, neurologically influenced structure/loading, or mixed?

Thus:

> **Structural pathology is not structural independence.**

---

# 13. GP reconstruction

Primary care has recurring longitudinal significance.

The GP initially assembled the dimensions together:

- feet;
- gait;
- tone;
- movement;
- function.

Later GP records repeatedly reconnect:

- increased tone;
- abnormal gait;
- Parkinsonism;
- clawing feet;
- pain.

This is important because it shows that the dimensions separated in the specialist architecture can reappear together downstream.

A useful secondary comparator is the same-GP information-state change:

- in 2017: an open integrated feet–gait–tone problem is sent to Neurology;
- by 2025: the same clinician is working within an inherited young-onset Parkinson's frame and presents dystonia largely as a challenge requiring specialist second opinion.

This is not evidence of bad faith or ignorance.

It is a possible illustration of **decision inheritance** and the way specialist synthesis can shape downstream information states.

---

# 14. Care-pathway lock-in and the missing join

The strongest systems argument is not that later clinicians acted irrationally.

It is almost the opposite.

Neurology, Podiatry and primary care can each act reasonably within their local domains while no durable owner reconstructs the whole.

That produces:

> **The pathway has destinations but no guaranteed return route for reconstruction.**

And:

> **The body can remain integrated while the records become modular.**

The information hazard is that clinically decisive information may live **between** modules.

This is why the founding synthesis matters longitudinally even if no information literally disappears.

> **The problem is not that information disappeared. The problem is the shape in which it travelled.**

---

# 15. Self-reproducing uncertainty

“Not clear” can be a legitimate conclusion.

But uncertainty can become self-reproducing when each subsequent clinician begins downstream of the prior synthesis rather than upstream at the original clinical relationship.

The mechanism is:

1. original integrated problem;
2. specialist separation / uncertainty;
3. downstream services inherit separate channels;
4. each local service updates its own channel;
5. later continuity is taken as confirmation of the original architecture;
6. the original relationship is not globally reconstructed.

This should not be overstated as circular reasoning unless the documentary chain supports it in the particular context.

The safe formulation is:

> **Later continuity within an inherited frame is not necessarily independent confirmation of the frame's founding assumptions.**

---

# 16. 2023 safety and misalignment

This is a separate argument, not proof that July 2017 was negligent.

By 2023:

- pain had become severe and endurance-limiting;
- safety and quality-of-life concerns were documented;
- explanatory disagreement was visible;
- OFF-related interpretations were appearing.

The question becomes:

> **What did reasonable contemporary care require once unresolved pain was safety-relevant and the explanatory model was visibly contested?**

This can survive even if the 2017 assessment is judged reasonable.

It is a later duty/reassessment argument.

---

# 17. 2025 clinical consequence and constrained treatment

The later record becomes important not because it proves what should have happened in 2017, but because it shows that mechanism matters to management.

Later features include:

- foot pain improving when dopaminergic medication takes effect;
- persistent toe curling;
- significant morning/initial-step burden;
- treatment limited by dyskinesia;
- requests for dystonia-directed management;
- GP limits on changing specialist neurological treatment.

This creates a **therapeutic window** problem:

> **If pain relief and dyskinesia constrain each other, correctly characterising the lower-limb mechanism becomes more clinically important, not less.**

This evidence can be relevant to harm, treatability, and causation.

It should not be used to declare what the exact 2017 foot mechanism was.

---

# 18. PRKN genetics

Later genetics must remain behind the temporal firewall.

It does **not** establish:

- that all painful feet in 2017 were dystonia;
- that structural pathology was caused by PRKN;
- that clinicians were obliged to know the molecular diagnosis in 2017;
- that breach is retrospectively proved.

Its proper use is **natural-history concordance and biological plausibility**.

The later PRKN/PARK2 material recognises lower-limb dystonia and that such dystonia may remain isolated for years.

This makes the prospectively documented sequence biologically coherent:

> earlier lower-limb phenotype → increasingly evident movement dysfunction → overt severe Parkinsonian syndrome.

The careful formulation is:

> **Later PRKN genetics provides retrospective natural-history concordance with the contemporaneously documented sequence without altering the 2017 knowledge state.**

This strengthens the significance of asking whether the earlier lower-limb phenotype was adequately characterised.

It does not answer the duty question by itself.

---

# 19. Harm

The harm theory must distinguish disease from avoidable consequence.

The underlying PRKN disorder and genuine structural foot pathology are not caused by the alleged breach.

The potentially attributable harm is the **incremental burden**, if any, caused by failure to characterise, integrate, or later reconsider the lower-limb mechanism when reasonable care would probably have done so.

Potential heads of clinically relevant harm include:

- avoidable pain;
- impaired standing/walking;
- occupational limitation;
- delayed access to appropriate neurological/mechanical combination treatment;
- prolonged treatment within an incomplete explanatory model;
- later safety/endurance consequences.

The necessary distinction is:

> **Baseline harm** — what would have occurred with ideal care because of underlying disease and structure.

versus

> **Incremental avoidable harm** — what probably would have been prevented, shortened or reduced absent the relevant breach.

The project must resist claiming the whole subsequent disease course as caused harm.

---

# 20. Causation

Causation requires an explicit counterfactual chain.

The core sequence is:

> **If the required relationship-directed assessment had occurred, what would it probably have shown?**

Then:

> **If it had shown a neurological, structural or mixed mechanism, what would probably have changed in management?**

Then:

> **What later pain, disability, delay or other harm would probably have been avoided or reduced by that changed management?**

Later evidence serves the chain in different ways:

### 2017–2018 treatment response
Supports plausibility that the lower-limb/foot state had neurological treatment-state behaviour.

### Podiatry
Proves a genuine mechanical component and helps define the mixed counterfactual.

### Later OFF/dystonia observations
Show repeated clinical crossing of the earlier boundary.

### 2025 medication response
Potentially supports treatability and clinical consequence.

### 2026 genetics
Supports biological coherence of lower-limb dystonic phenotype in the eventual disease, without proving the historical mechanism.

The causation case must remain expert-dependent.

# 21. The strongest defence — preserved intentionally

The argument vault must contain the defence because the case is stronger when it survives it.

The strongest defence is:

- severe Parkinsonism was correctly identified;
- structural foot disease was real;
- the patient's history supplied reasons to distinguish foot pain from leg tension;
- gait was directly observed and videoed;
- “NOT FOOT DROP” suggests meaningful discrimination;
- possible dystonic posture was recognised;
- reflexes and tone were assessed;
- extensive investigation was undertaken;
- multidisciplinary input occurred;
- Podiatry referral was sensible;
- “may,” “perhaps,” and “Not clear” were cautious rather than dogmatic;
- not every bedside manoeuvre must be itemised in a discharge summary.

That defence prevents any fair analysis from saying the feet were simply ignored.

Its vulnerability is narrower:

> **How much of the necessary discriminative bridge is affirmatively demonstrated, and how much must be inferred from the existence of a sophisticated but incomplete record?**

The case should be prepared to accept a mixed conclusion:

- initial July work may be judged reasonable;
- the Protest may still create a separate closure question;
- later treatment-state evidence may independently require reopening;
- later safety issues may independently establish a later duty point.

The case does not stand or fall on one breach.

# 25. Reserved propositions requiring special proof

Some propositions are useful but should not be promoted to controlling arguments until their evidential foundation is secure.

## 25.1 Departmental capability

Possible proposition: the university Neurology environment contained movement-disorders / Parkin-related expertise relevant to young-onset dystonic-foot presentations.

Use only to show environmental capability/relevance if properly sourced.

Do **not** infer private knowledge, actual consultation or subjective awareness by a particular clinician.

## 25.2 Protest omission as documentary distortion

Use only if the Protest is proved to the required standard.

The omission may then affect how later readers understand the closure state.

Do not treat absence from the note as proof of deliberate omission or concealment.

## 25.3 Self-validating continuity

This is a systems inference, not a fact.

It should be framed as a possibility that inherited narratives can shape later continuity, not as a claim that later clinicians merely copied or failed to think.

## 25.4 Same-GP information-state comparator

Useful as a longitudinal illustration.

Do not use it to allege that the GP was unaware, captured, or acting improperly.

- The corns/callouses were caused by PRKN.

- Absence of a documented manoeuvre proves it was not performed.
- The clinician knowingly ignored the feet.

- All later pain was avoidable.

# 27. Questions that matter most for counsel and experts

The case is mature when it can be reduced to questions capable of changing the answer.

### Historical neurological practice

What focused lower-limb examination was reasonably required in July 2017 once severe young-onset Parkinsonism, structural feet and a dystonic-looking pseudo-footdrop coexisted?

### The factual bridge

What examination actually occurred, including any work not fully recorded in the surviving note?

### Residual uncertainty

If that examination occurred, was “separate / neuropathic / Not clear” a reasonable residual formulation?

### Protest fork

If the patient said “you have not dealt with my feet,” what did reasonable closure require after that notice?

### Handoff

What neurological question, if any, was passed to Podiatry, and what return mechanism existed?

### Reopening

When post-discharge treatment-state evidence later crossed the original boundary, when should the relationship have been reconsidered?

### Harm

Which later burden was baseline disease/structure and which, if any, was incremental and avoidable?

### Causation

What would adequate assessment/reassessment probably have shown, what management would probably have changed, and what harm would probably have been reduced?

# 28. Final case statement

The mature case is not that Neurology had to know the final molecular answer in 2017.

It is not that every painful foot feature had one cause.

It is not that uncertainty was forbidden.

> **An integrated lower-limb problem entered Neurology. Independently, later treatment-state, mechanical, movement-state and genetic evidence repeatedly crossed the original boundary and may have created fresh occasions on which reasonable care required the relationship to be reconstructed.
