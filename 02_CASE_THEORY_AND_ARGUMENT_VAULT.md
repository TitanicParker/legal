# Case Theory and Argument Vault

## Function

This file contains the finite argument set. It is subordinate to `REPAIRED_CANONICAL_V2.md` and should be read with `05_JULY_2017_FEET_ASSESSMENT_AND_DOCUMENTARY_DISPOSITION.md`, `03_CONTROLLING_ARGUMENT_TO_CLIN_MAP.md`, `04_FINAL_EXPERT_QUESTION_REGISTER.md` and `07_GPT_A_INTEGRATED_FORMULATION.md`.

No new theory should enter the case unless it is anchored to the canonical record and can be placed within Duty, Breach, Harm or Causation.

## Controlling theory

The case is not that all foot pain was dystonia. It is that the feet were structurally abnormal, neurologically significant, later pharmacologically modifiable, and never clearly integrated into one durable treatment model.

The July refinement is now more precise:

> **Neurology did not merely fail to connect the painful feet to the movement disorder. It positively identified a dystonic-looking lower-limb phenomenon, used dystonic gait language within the Parkinsonian diagnostic work-up, and then affirmatively introduced a separate explanatory branch in which the painful-foot phenomena might be “completely separate” or secondary peripheral neuropathy, ending “Not clear.” The founding expert question is what clinical work justified that branch and that uncertainty.**

The longitudinal allegation has four principal stages:

1. **Founding assessment and disposition:** the July 2017 feet–gait–tone relationship may not have been adequately discriminated before the separate/neuropathy branch and “Not clear” endpoint were placed into the specialist record.
2. **Reopening:** 2017–2018 treatment-state evidence created positive new neurological information involving the feet.
3. **Continuity and calibration:** Podiatry established genuine mechanics but ends in 2019; later Neurology manages escalating feet/lower-limb symptoms without a clearly demonstrated integrated structural–neurological reconstruction.
4. **Harm and safety:** severe later pain became psychologically dangerous, while the record does not show safeguarding proportionate to the death-associated language.

## A. July 2017 — founding discrimination and documentary disposition

### Proposition

An integrated feet–gait–tone problem entered Neurology. Neurology itself identified severe very-young-onset Parkinsonism and a dystonic-looking pseudo-footdrop phenomenon alongside structural foot disease.

The lower-limb abnormality was not merely observed. The gait was later described in the DAT-scan indication as “almost dystonically unusual,” so the feature was used as neurological evidence within the Parkinsonian diagnostic project.

The final synthesis then stated that the leg symptoms including corns, callouses and “burning” might have been “completely separate” or perhaps secondary peripheral neuropathy, ending “Not clear.”

### Question

What clinical work separated or related:

- true weakness;
- rigidity;
- dystonic posturing;
- neuropathy;
- fixed deformity;
- mechanical loading;
- pain;
- mixed mechanisms?

What work specifically justified introducing a branch in which the painful feet could be separate from the Parkinsonian/dystonic lower-limb process?

### Recorded July comparator

The current July record affirmatively documents a striking abnormal gait, footdrop-like/dystonic-looking left-lower-limb movement, pes planus, hammertoes and mild/minimal oedema within a substantial neurological examination.

The current record does not clearly document directed four-direction distal foot power, plantar response, directed distal sensory findings supporting/excluding neuropathy, or an explicit fixed-versus-dynamic/posture-loading analysis.

A later 2020 Neurology examination provides an internal documentary comparator: when resolving a similar foot-throw/footdrop-like appearance, the neurologist expressly documented normal dorsiflexion, plantarflexion, inversion and eversion and used those findings to classify the phenomenon as Parkinsonian.

### Proper limit

Do not assert that an examination was not performed merely because a manoeuvre is undocumented. The stronger point is that the surviving detailed specialist record does not clearly demonstrate the complete bridge by which the separate/neuropathy branch was earned.

Do not use the 2020 examination to define the 2017 standard of care. It is an internal documentary comparator only.

## B. “Not clear” — authoritative uncertainty

“Not clear” may represent two materially different states:

- **procedural uncertainty:** the relationship is unclear because it has not yet been adequately investigated;
- **substantive specialist uncertainty:** appropriate specialist discriminatory work has been performed and the relationship genuinely remains unclear.

The words themselves do not distinguish those states.

Because they sit inside a highly sophisticated specialist account, they may carry the appearance of an earned specialist endpoint.

The controlled hypothesis is:

> **If the required relational work had not occurred, the discharge may have converted procedural uncertainty into authoritative specialist uncertainty.**

Do not assume what the GP actually thought. Ask what work a referring GP could reasonably expect to underlie such a specialist disposition.

## C. Protest — conditional route

If proved, the patient's pre-discharge statement that the feet had not been dealt with creates a distinct decision-after-notice question.

It does not establish the mechanism of the feet and does not rewrite the preceding admission.

The core July documentary-disposition theory does **not** depend on the Protest being proved.

## D. September 2017 — contemporary PARK2 threshold

By September 2017, PARK2 had become an independently favoured contemporary hypothesis. That does not alter the July standard but raises the question whether the lower-limb phenotype required renewed attention.

## E. October 2017 / April 2018 — pharmacological reopening

This is stronger than a generic later-response argument.

Procyclidine withdrawal produced deterioration in tension, feet and gait together. The treating neurologist later recorded rapid return of the outside-left-foot phenomenon during withdrawal.

The key proposition is:

> **The foot domain was demonstrated to be neurologically treatment-state dependent.**

The expert question is whether reasonable practice then required the earlier separation to be reopened.

## F. Podiatry — structural truth and continuity

Podiatry repeatedly demonstrated genuine focal pathology, particularly the recurrent fifth-MTPJ site, clawing, pronation and benefit from offloading.

That evidence should not be minimised. It proves that part of the problem was mechanical.

But Podiatry's role has temporal limits. The complete Podiatry record ends in 2019. Once the feet remained symptomatic and later became severe, the issue becomes:

> **Who was responsible for re-establishing structural/mechanical assessment alongside neurological reassessment?**

No criticism should be directed at Podiatry merely for treating structural disease. The issue is whether the wider pathway allowed the structural stream to disappear while the foot problem persisted.

## G. 2022–2025 — later reconstruction

The later record progressively strengthens the neurological relevance of the feet:

- 2022: prolonged morning foot “warm-up”;
- 2023: severe bilateral foot/lower-leg pain, OFF/dystonia reasoning, OFF-related pain/shuffling, major functional restriction and serious psychological-endurance language;
- 2024: pain improves with pregabalin while toe curling persists;
- 2025: Sinemet is acknowledged to alter feet/pain; dyskinesia constrains treatment; CR produces profound relief.

The correct proposition is not that one mechanism explains everything. It is:

> **The foot syndrome was repeatedly treatment-state dependent and required integration into neurological treatment planning.**

## H. Medication genealogy

The treatment history should be analysed as one continuous clinical sequence:

> **anticholinergic benefit → anticholinergic withdrawal produces rapid foot/gait/tension deterioration → levodopa-based treatment becomes the governing strategy → severe foot-pain burden develops within that treatment state → dose/formulation becomes clinically consequential → CR produces profound relief.**

The relevant later breach question is therefore not merely whether clinicians recognised that Sinemet could reduce pain. It is whether they adequately calibrated neurological treatment against a foot syndrome already known to be pharmacologically modifiable.

## I. Documentary / informational causation

If expert evidence establishes that the July separate/neuropathy branch was insufficiently earned, the potential causal event is not simply failure to diagnose a specific mechanism.

It may instead be that an authoritative specialist disposition changed the information environment inherited by the GP and later clinicians by making an incompletely investigated relationship appear to have reached a specialist endpoint.

The controlled causal chain is:

> **integrated referral → inadequate relational investigation, if proved → authoritative separate/neuropathy disposition → downstream reliance/inheritance, if proved → delayed/incomplete reopening, if proved → treatment consequences, if proved → defined additional harm.**

Each link requires evidence. Do not assume reliance or harm merely because the wording existed in the record.

## J. Harm

The alleged harm is not the underlying PRKN disease or the existence of longstanding structural foot pathology.

The relevant harm is the **incremental transformation of longstanding foot pain into an intolerable later pain state**, together with loss of function, constriction of life and psychological deterioration.

## K. Medical causation

The principal later causation question is:

> **Did the neurological medication strategy cause or materially aggravate the severe foot-pain state, and did that aggravated pain materially contribute to the patient's subsequent psychological deterioration?**

A precise expert counterfactual is required:

- what should have been changed;
- when;
- what pain/function improvement would probably have followed;
- what burden would have remained because of PRKN and structural disease.

Mechanism may remain mixed. Treatment-state dependence need not.

## L. Safeguarding

The later record contains severe pain together with “death wish,” “no quality of life,” shrinking-life language and a March 2025 statement that the patient was not willing to live this way, with foot pain dominant.

On the complete Neurology record, no documented safeguarding or suicide-risk assessment has been identified in response to that sequence.

The expert question is what reasonable care required once death-associated psychological distress appeared in a severe pain context.

This route is independent of proving that the medication caused the pain. Even if mechanism remained disputed, the risk state still required an appropriate response.

## M. PRKN genetics

Later PRKN confirmation may be used for retrospective phenotype coherence and causation analysis where appropriate.

It must not be used to determine what clinicians should have known in July 2017 or to prove that every foot symptom was dystonia.

## Strongest defence evidence

The case must retain and answer the following:

- genuine structural foot disease;
- historical patient distinction between foot pain and leg tension;
- substantial neurological work in 2017;
- direct gait observation and video;
- “NOT FOOT DROP” as evidence that some discrimination occurred;
- cautious wording: “may,” “perhaps,” “Not clear”;
- legitimate use of Podiatry;
- treatment-response mechanisms may be mixed;
- levodopa treatment was genuinely constrained by dyskinesia;
- early neurological treatment produced substantial functional benefit;
- later Neurology genuinely reassessed individual foot phenomena;
- pregabalin materially reduced pain;
- periods of function were good; and
- the 2023 medication-stop report is contrary evidence.

These points limit overstatement. They do not provide permanent answers to later evidence.

## Arguments not to make

Do not allege:

- every foot symptom was dystonia;
- structural pathology was unreal or caused by PRKN;
- peripheral neuropathy was impossible;
- the July examination definitely did not occur;
- the 2020 examination defines the 2017 standard;
- “Not clear” was negligent merely as wording;
- the GP actually understood “Not clear” in a particular way without evidence;
- Podiatry should never have been involved;
- later genetics proves 2017 breach;
- treatment response proves one exclusive mechanism;
- the Protest is proved merely because it was later reported;
- concealment, dishonesty or bad faith;
- every later pain episode was avoidable;
- the entire later course was caused by July 2017.

## Final finite formulation

> **A genuine young-onset Parkinsonian disorder and genuine structural foot disease coexisted. Neurology used a dystonic-looking lower-limb sign within the Parkinsonian work-up while affirmatively introducing a separate/neuropathy branch for the painful feet and leaving the relationship “Not clear.” The founding question is what clinical work justified that branch and whether the resulting uncertainty was medically earned. The longitudinal questions are when later treatment-state evidence required reopening and reintegration, whether treatment was appropriately calibrated, whether any specialist disposition materially altered later information flow, what incremental harm was avoidable, and whether severe pain was appropriately safeguarded.**

Subject to correction of factual error, new primary evidence or independent expert/legal opinion, this is the finite argument set.
