## Governing instructions

Approach the case as an independent movement-disorders neurologist. Do not assume the repository theory is correct.

For every answer:

- assess each historical decision using the information then available;
- do not use later PRKN genetics to determine the 2017 standard of care;
- accept that genuine structural foot pathology existed;
- accept that Podiatry may have been appropriate for the structural component;
- do not assume an undocumented examination did or did not occur;
- distinguish what the record proves, what can reasonably be inferred, what remains clinically indeterminate, and what additional evidence would change your view;
- separate reasonable practice, breach, medical causation and harm;

The alleged pre-discharge Protest is disputed patient evidence. Address it only conditionally. The core July documentary-disposition questions below do **not** depend on the Protest being proved.

## Controlling formulation

> **An integrated lower-limb problem entered Neurology. Neurology positively identified a dystonic-looking lower-limb phenomenon alongside genuine structural foot disease, used the dystonic-looking gait within the Parkinsonian diagnostic work-up, and then affirmatively introduced a separate/peripheral-neuropathy explanatory branch for the painful feet while concluding “Not clear.” The first expert task is to determine what clinical work justified that branch and that uncertainty. Later neurological treatment demonstrated that the foot state was pharmacologically modifiable; the structural Podiatry stream ends in 2019; the feet later became increasingly disabling; and by 2023–2025 severe pain, OFF/dystonia reasoning, medication-state effects and profound relief after a formulation change made the treatment relationship clinically unavoidable. The broader task is to determine whether that progression was adequately assessed, carried forward, reopened, treated and safeguarded, and what avoidable harm resulted from any failure.**

# Question 1 — What did reasonable neurological practice require in July 2017?

Please address, insofar as clinically relevant:

- distal lower-limb power;
- sensory examination relevant to neuropathy;
- ankle-specific reflexes;
- plantar responses;
- fixed versus dynamic foot/toe posture;
- passive correctability/range/contracture;
- patterned lower-limb dystonia phenomenology;
- gait/loading mechanics;
- relationship of pain to posture, movement and loading;
- objective findings that would support, weaken or leave open a peripheral-neuropathy explanation;
- any other material discriminator.

For each, distinguish essential, desirable but non-mandatory, and unnecessary.
