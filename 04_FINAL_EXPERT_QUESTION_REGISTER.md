# 1. Governing instructions to the expert

Please approach the record as an independent movement-disorders neurologist.

Do not assume that the repository's formulation is correct. The purpose of your opinion is to test it.

Please keep the following distinctions explicit throughout:

- **2017 contemporaneous information** must be assessed using the knowledge and reasonable practice of that time.
- Later treatment response, later dystonia reasoning and later PRKN genetics must **not** be projected backwards to determine the 2017 standard of care.
- Genuine structural foot pathology is accepted.
- Referral to Podiatry may have been entirely appropriate.
- “Not clear” may have been a clinically proper endpoint if reached after adequate assessment.
- The surviving record contains substantial positive evidence of neurological assessment; absence of a documented manoeuvre is not automatically proof that it did not occur.
- Where a conclusion depends on an examination that may have occurred but is not clearly documented, please state that explicitly.
- The alleged pre-discharge Protest is disputed patient evidence. For the Protest question only, please assume hypothetically that the statement occurred as described and explain its clinical significance on that assumption.
- Please separate:
  1. what reasonable practice required;
  2. what the surviving record demonstrates;
  3. what may reasonably be inferred;
  4. what remains indeterminate;
  5. and what additional evidence would change your opinion.
- Please distinguish the **initial July 2017 assessment** from any later **duty to reconsider or reopen** the feet–gait–tone relationship.
- Do not express legal conclusions. Where appropriate, give a clinical opinion on reasonable neurological practice, factual medical significance, counterfactual management and medical causation.

For each question, please identify the level of confidence in your answer and explain any material limitation in the source record.

# 2. Controlling formulation being tested

> **In July 2017 an integrated feet–gait–tone presentation generated, on Neurology's own examination, both severe young-onset Parkinsonism and a dystonic-looking lower-limb phenomenon alongside genuine structural foot disease. The final specialist synthesis allowed the painful foot/leg phenomena to be separate or neuropathic and ultimately “Not clear.” The surviving record demonstrates substantial neurological work, but it does not clearly demonstrate the focused discriminative examination that would show how those competing lower-limb mechanisms were separated. The central question is whether that work occurred and, if not, what reasonable practice required at the time.**

The questions below are designed to test each necessary component of that formulation and the later causation/reopening routes.

---

# Question 1 — What did reasonable neurological practice require in July 2017?

## Question

Given the contemporaneous presentation and examination findings, what focused neurological assessment of the **feet–gait–tone relationship** would reasonable neurological practice in July 2017 have required before the lower-limb/foot symptoms could properly be left as structurally separate, neuropathic, mixed, or “Not clear”?

Please address specifically whether reasonable assessment required any of the following and, if so, to what degree:

- distal lower-limb power;
- sensory examination relevant to peripheral neuropathy;
- ankle-specific reflex assessment;
- fixed versus dynamic foot/toe posture;
- passive correctability / range / contracture;
- patterned lower-limb dystonia phenomenology;
- gait/loading mechanics;
- relationship between pain, posture and movement;
- any other bedside discriminator you regard as material.

Please distinguish between what was **essential**, what was **desirable but not mandatory**, and what would not ordinarily have been required before discharge.

## Proposition being tested

> Once severe young-onset Parkinsonism, a dystonic-looking pseudo-footdrop phenomenon and genuine structural foot disease coexisted, the relationship between those findings became a neurological discrimination problem.

## Principal CLIN material

- **CLIN-000020–000025** — abnormal gait, bilateral foot pain, mallet toes, callouses/pressure areas.
- **CLIN-000068–000073** — movement-initiation difficulty; GP seeks Neurology opinion on “gait, feet and ‘muscle tension’ issues.”
- **CLIN-000851–000862** — urgent referral: unusual festinant/stiff gait, bilateral foot issues, increasing initiation difficulty, stiffness/tone, brisk reflexes and toe clawing.
- **CLIN-000877–000884** — abnormal gait/increased tone affecting function and employment; urgent assessment requested.
- **CLIN-001085–001090** — markedly increased tone and moderately brisk reflexes.
- **CLIN-001097–001120** — “strikingly abnormal” gait; footdrop-like appearance “somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture — NOT FOOT DROP”; videoed exam; rigidity; gross bradykinesia; pes planus; hammertoes.

## Why the answer matters

**Duty / standard:** This establishes what clinical work reasonable 2017 practice required.  
**Breach:** No breach conclusion can safely be reached until the required standard is defined.  
**Causation:** It identifies what a proper examination would have been capable of discovering.

# Question 2 — Does the surviving July 2017 record demonstrate that the required discrimination occurred?

## Question

Taking the July 2017 record fairly and as a whole, does the surviving documentation demonstrate an assessment sufficient to support the later residual formulation that the painful leg/foot symptoms might be “completely separate,” perhaps neuropathic, and were “Not clear”?

Please give full weight to the positive evidence in Neurology's favour, including:

- detailed neurological examination;
- direct gait observation;
- “NOT FOOT DROP”;
- possible dystonic posture;
- reflex and tone examination;
- structural foot findings;
- video recording;
- prolonged admission;
- investigation and multidisciplinary work;
- sophisticated young-onset / recessive Parkinsonism reasoning.

Please then identify:

1. what focused lower-limb examination is **affirmatively documented**;
2. what can **reasonably be inferred** from the documentation;
3. what cannot safely be inferred;
4. whether the final lower-limb formulation was clinically congruent with the examination demonstrated in the record;
5. and whether your overall conclusion is **adequate, inadequate, or indeterminate on the surviving record**.

If indeterminate, please identify the single most important missing fact or document that would resolve the question.

## Proposition being tested

> “Not clear” may be proper, but the issue is whether the surviving record demonstrates the clinical work by which that uncertainty was earned.

This question also tests the **synthesis–examination differential**:

> Was the evidential foundation visible at the bedside proportionate to the sophistication and consequences of the final specialist formulation?

## Principal CLIN material

- **CLIN-001084–001124** — surviving detailed neurological examination.
- **CLIN-001142–001149** — “completely separate” / possible peripheral neuropathy / “Not clear” / “markedly divorced.”
- **CLIN-001429–001439** — longer admission allowed team to “get a lot done,” “explore a lot of questions,” and obtain a “firmer footing.”
- **CLIN-001518–001525** — PRKN/PARK2 and other monogenic reasoning.
- **CLIN-002214–002220** — contemporaneous genetics context, departmental material and acknowledged subspecialty limits.

## Why the answer matters

**Breach:** This is the principal July 2017 adequacy question.  
**Defence:** It expressly preserves the strongest adequacy case rather than assuming omission.  
**Case stability:** An answer that the work was adequate narrows the founding allegation but does not decide later reopening questions.

# Question 5 — Did the 2017–2018 treatment-state evidence require the original boundary to be reopened?

## Question

Irrespective of whether the initial July 2017 assessment was reasonable, what clinical significance should be attached to the post-discharge treatment-state observations in which **feet, gait and tension changed together**?

In particular:

1. What significance should be attached to the October 2017 procyclidine/Kemadrin withdrawal report that tension was building, the feet were restless and the walk was “falling apart”?
2. What significance should be attached to the later clinician-authored observation that, during withdrawal, the first thing changing rapidly was the return of the outside-left-foot phenomenon and that the patient became more “shifty” / “unrelaxed”?
3. Did either event, alone or together, reasonably call for reconsideration of the original feet–neurology relationship?
4. If so, what assessment or management would reasonable practice have required at that stage?

Please distinguish **evidence of temporal association/treatment-state behaviour** from proof of a single causal mechanism.

## Proposition being tested

> Later evidence can reopen a boundary without proving that the original boundary was negligent.

## Principal CLIN material

- **CLIN-001770–001775** — major functional response during procyclidine treatment.
- **CLIN-001782–001784** — withdrawal: tension building, feet restless, walk falling apart.
- **CLIN-001914–001918** — clinician records rapid return of outside-left-foot phenomenon during withdrawal and a more “shifty” / “unrelaxed” state.

## Why the answer matters

**Independent breach route:** This question survives even if July 2017 is judged reasonable.  
**Causation:** It provides near-term evidence about dynamic neurological treatment-state behaviour.  
**Mechanism:** It tests, without deciding, whether a purely static/separate foot model remained sufficient.

# Question 6 — At what later threshold, if any, did the accumulating evidence require substantive reconstruction?

## Question

From 2022 onward, the record contains repeated further crossings of the original structural/neurological boundary.

Please consider whether, and at what point, reasonable movement-disorders practice required a substantive reconstruction of the original feet–gait–tone problem rather than continuing to manage individual components separately.

Please address the cumulative significance of:

- morning feet requiring 20–25 minutes to “warm up”;
- lower-limb cramping raising ON/OFF dystonia, with OFF dystonia considered more likely;
- later assessment that “leg pain and shuffling is off related”;
- persistent toe curling despite improvement of foot pain with pregabalin;
- acknowledged improvement in the feet/pain once Sinemet takes effect;
- and the treatment constraint whereby further dopaminergic therapy may improve pain but worsen dyskinesia.

Please identify the earliest later point, if any, at which you consider substantive movement-disorders reassessment or reformulation reasonably indicated.

## Proposition being tested

> Local updating of individual branches may not be the same as global reconstruction of the original clinical object.

## Principal CLIN material

- **CLIN-002382–002386** — feet take 20–25 minutes to warm up.
- **CLIN-002425–002431** — foot pain/difficulty walking; left-lower-limb cramping raises Parkinsonian dystonia, more likely OFF.

# Question 8 — For any breach found, what is the medical counterfactual and what harm was probably avoidable?

## Question

For **each** departure from reasonable practice that you identify in Questions 1–7, please analyse causation separately.

For each breach, please state:

1. **Date / threshold** — when did the departure occur?
2. **Information state** — what was actually known at that time?
3. **Required action** — what should reasonable practice have done?
4. **Probable finding** — on the balance of medical probability, what would proper assessment/reassessment likely have shown?
5. **Probable management change** — what treatment, referral, monitoring, multidisciplinary management or explanation would probably have changed?
6. **Avoidable harm** — which later pain, functional restriction, treatment delay or other clinical burden would probably have been prevented, shortened or reduced?
7. **Baseline harm** — which burden would probably have occurred anyway because of the underlying PRKN/Parkinsonian disorder and genuine structural foot pathology?
8. **Uncertainty** — where the evidence cannot support a probability conclusion, please say so.

Please do not treat later evidence as proof of earlier breach. It may be used only where medically appropriate to inform the **counterfactual**, biological plausibility, later reopening threshold, treatment response or harm.

# 3. Special instruction on PRKN genetics

The later PRKN evidence should be considered only for its proper evidential purposes.

It may assist with:

- retrospective biological plausibility;
- natural-history concordance;
- the possibility that a lower-limb dystonic phenotype can belong to the same disease process as later Parkinsonism;
- and, where appropriate, medical counterfactual reasoning.

It must **not** be used to say that:

- clinicians in July 2017 should have known the molecular diagnosis;
- every painful foot feature in 2017 was dystonia;
- corns, callouses, pes planus or hammertoes were caused by PRKN;
- or later molecular confirmation proves historical breach.

The relevant CLIN references are:

- **CLIN-001518–001525** — PRKN/PARK2 already considered as a 2017 possibility.
- **CLIN-003227** — later St Vincent's conclusion that Parkinsonism and dystonia are due to compound heterozygous Parkin mutations.
- **CLIN-006192–006202** — later molecular findings.
- **CLIN-006228** — later PRKN/PARK2 natural-history description.

# 5. Questions deliberately excluded

The expert is **not** being asked:

- whether the patient “really had dystonia instead of Parkinson's”;
- whether all foot pain had a single cause;
- whether “Not clear” is negligent as a phrase;
- whether structural foot pathology was genuine;
- whether Podiatry should never have been involved;
- whether later genetics proves what should have happened in 2017;
- whether the Protest historically occurred;
- whether any clinician acted dishonestly or in bad faith;
- or to endorse the repository's theory.

The task is narrower:

> **Determine what reasonable neurological practice required at each identified threshold; what the surviving record demonstrates occurred; whether any clinically material departure resulted; and, for each departure found, what would probably have happened differently and what later harm would probably have been avoided or reduced.**
