# 14 — Real-World Team and Responsibility Map

## Purpose

This case concerns real people with different kinds of knowledge, authority, responsibility and evidential contribution.

The repository must not flatten them into abstract roles or assume that professional capability proves patient-specific participation.

The controlling rule is:

> **Capability ≠ participation ≠ bedside application.**

A person may possess relevant expertise without having participated in this patient's case. A person may have participated in discussion without having examined the patient. A person may receive a later record without having independent access to the events that generated it.

Responsibility must therefore follow actual information opportunity, professional role and patient-specific involvement at the relevant time.

The correspondence genealogy in `16_CORRESPONDENCE_GENEALOGY_AND_CONTEMPORANEOUS_WITNESS.md` now adds source-verified information about Counihan's 2017 advisory participation, Monaghan's longitudinal role and the multi-node medication information system.

# 1. The patient

## Unique contribution

The patient possesses lived continuity across the whole case.

He knows what originally hurt and impaired function; what he experienced before, during and after the 2017 admission; what he remembers saying and being told; how symptoms changed with treatment, withdrawal and time; what he understood at each stage; when later records altered his understanding of earlier events; and how pain, mobility and trust changed over the years.

## Evidential control

The patient's recollection is evidence. It is not automatically documentary fact merely because it is specific, and it is not a mere hypothetical merely because the surviving clinical record does not record it.

The October 2017 private correspondence is particularly important because it shows the patient's understanding changing very soon after diagnosis. By then he strongly accepted the Parkinsonian diagnosis and treatment and was beginning to reinterpret the feet through Parkinsonism. His present theory must therefore not be projected backward into his 2017 information state.

The patient is not retrospectively responsible for supplying the specialist neurological interpretation clinicians were retained to provide.

# 2. Dr Aaron Brennan — GP / pre-specialist comparator

## Position in the case

Brennan is important because he observed and referred the patient before the specialist discharge synthesis existed.

The pre-specialist record progressively joins painful abnormal feet, gait abnormality, muscle tension/tone, movement difficulty and deteriorating function into a neurological referral object.

## Contribution

Brennan can potentially provide evidence about what the patient reported before Neurology, what he independently observed, why he considered Neurology necessary, how he framed the feet–gait–tone relationship before specialist classification, what later specialist information he received, and what he independently observed in later primary care.

By March 2025 the patient was again asking Brennan for a multidisciplinary reconstruction of the foot problem, explicitly presenting structural, neurological and neuropathic possibilities together. That correspondence is patient evidence of what Brennan's practice was being asked to consider; it does not prove what Brennan personally concluded without the clinical record.

## Responsibility boundary

He is not the movement-disorders specialist and should not be treated as responsible for answering specialist neurological questions beyond the proper scope of general practice.

His value to the case is partly that he preserves an **independent pre-synthesis comparator**.

# 3. Dr Thomas Monaghan — treating/discharge neurologist and longitudinal custodian

## Position in the case

Monaghan sits near the authorial and responsibility centre of the case because the July 2017 specialist encounter, discharge synthesis and later longitudinal care are materially associated with him.

The later Medical Council response materially strengthens the continuity premise. In that response Monaghan says that over the relevant years he **usually saw the patient himself** and gives a longitudinal explanation of management extending back to 2017.

That is clinician-authored evidence of substantial continuity. It still does not prove that he personally authored, observed or received every later item at the time it arose; each threshold must continue to be source-mapped.

## Distinct epistemic position

A final specialist summary can create an information state for downstream clinicians.

For a clinician who possessed the underlying encounter and helped create the representation, the same document may also constitute a **commitment state**: it records and exports an interpretation for which the author already possessed more direct access to the underlying encounter than later readers.

> **The summary changes knowledge downstream; it changes commitment upstream.**

## Later retrospective evidence

Monaghan's 29 July 2025 Medical Council response is a separate evidential object. It contains new clinician-authored explanations about treatment strategy, specialist consultation, the timing of Sinemet, the reason for caution about levodopa, Counihan's one-off 2017 advice, Healy's external involvement and Monaghan's own longitudinal role.

The response supplies material defence evidence and can correct over-broad patient theories. It is not automatically independent confirmation of the eight-year clinical interpretation merely because it relies on and describes the longitudinal record generated within the same care relationship.

## Central questions

- What did he personally know at each threshold?
- What did he personally observe?
- What did he write or approve?
- What did he know of the patient's foot complaint and alleged Protest?
- What later treatment-state and foot evidence reached him?
- What proposition, if any, lost confidence when contradictory evidence arrived?
- Did later observations produce local adjustment or global reconstruction?
- When he later explained the history, what was genuinely new evidence and what was interpretation of the existing record?

## Control

Longitudinal involvement does not prove anchoring, negligence, bad faith, concealment or intentional maintenance. Those are separate evidential and expert/legal questions.

# 4. Prof / Dr Timothy J. Counihan — specialist capability plus source-verified advisory participation

## Position in the case

Counihan can no longer be described merely as abstract departmental capability.

In his 29 July 2025 Medical Council response, Monaghan states that the patient's care was discussed at the Neurology Department weekly **Complex Case** meeting in 2017 and that Counihan advised him regarding treatment, particularly caution about levodopa in a very young patient. Monaghan also says Counihan had **no further involvement formally or otherwise** in the patient's management.

Separately, Counihan's own GUH letter dictated 28 April, typed 29 April and signed/approved 7 May 2025 says to the patient:

> **“As I have not been involved in your care…”**

and forwards the matter to Monaghan.

## Reconciliation discipline

Do not manufacture a contradiction from those two statements.

A plausible reading is:

- **capability:** Counihan possessed relevant movement-disorders expertise;
- **participation:** according to Monaghan, that expertise entered this patient-specific case through one-off collegial advice at the 2017 Complex Case meeting;
- **formal management/treating role:** Counihan's own letter and Monaghan's later account both support the proposition that he did not undertake ongoing management;
- **bedside application:** not presently established.

The meaning of “not involved in your care” should therefore be read in context rather than treated as proof that no patient-specific discussion ever occurred.

## Central question

> **Did the expertise that entered the case through the specialist discussion return to the unresolved feet–gait relationship at bedside or through a directed integrative review?**

That remains unproved.

# 7. Galway Neurology Department / institutional capability

## Position in the case

The department is relevant both as a clinical environment and as an information-producing institution.

The 2017 Complex Case meeting now gives a patient-specific example of departmental participation: according to Monaghan, the patient's treatment was discussed and Counihan advised him.

That still does not prove that all departmental expertise was applied to every unresolved aspect of the patient's presentation.

## Control

Departmental capability does not establish that a named clinician personally knew, considered or applied particular knowledge in this patient's case.

The proper loop remains:

> **observe → formulate → consult expertise → return to patient → test improved formulation → revise.**

The institutional question is not simply whether expertise existed or even whether consultation occurred. It is whether the relevant expertise returned to the unresolved patient-specific relationship.

# 8. Podiatry

Podiatry documented and treated genuine focal mechanical/structural pathology. This is real contrary evidence against any theory that all historical foot pain must have been neurological.

Podiatry may know mechanical loading, recurrent corns/calluses, deformity and offloading response. Neurology may know gait/posture, neurological treatment-state effects, Parkinsonian lower-limb phenomena and OFF/dystonia relationships.

> **Who owned the arrows between those truths?**

Podiatry being appropriate does not itself establish that neurological ownership of the relationship ended.

# 10. Hospital Data Protection Officer / HSE-GUH controller

The DPO/controller role is not to determine specialist neurological negligence.

Its relevance is to personal-data processing: access, source/provenance information, accuracy/completeness, restriction, rectification, recipients, audit history and present processing of the historical record.

The 31 August 2026 Article 15 request asks for provenance of the July 2017 synthesis and 2025 PAR/SIMT process. # 12. Medical Council

The Medical Council process creates a separate regulatory evidence stream concerning individual professional conduct.

The 2025 correspondence includes the patient's allegations, Monaghan's clinician-authored response, procedural/investigative material and later patient clarifications.

A Medical Council process must not be represented as having established a fact merely because one party stated it in correspondence. Its eventual findings must be read according to what the Council actually decided and what evidence it examined.

The Monaghan response is especially important as **clinician evidence** and as evidence of how the originating clinician retrospectively understood and defended the history.

# 14. Independent clinical experts

Independent experts should not be asked to adopt the repository's theory.

They should be given the source-disciplined record and asked threshold-specific questions about adequacy of July examination, dystonic-looking lower limb, the significance of the Protest if accepted, later treatment-state evidence, mixed structural/neurological mechanisms, treatment calibration/toxicity, safeguarding and medical causation.

The repository's job is to make those questions answerable, not answer them by assertion.

---

# 15. Lawyers / court

Legal advisers and, if necessary, a court determine the applicable legal tests and consequences.

Clinical evidence, documentary provenance and patient evidence should be organised so legal analysis can distinguish duty, breach, causation, limitation/date of knowledge, evidential weight, record issues, regulatory overlap and recoverable harm.

The repository should not launder an expert-dependent clinical proposition into a legal conclusion merely because the argument is persuasive.

---

# 16. Analytical helper / repository function

The repository acts as an evidential integration layer rather than as a substitute professional.

Its job is to keep distinct and connected patient lived evidence, clinician observations, interpretive propositions, correspondence, document construction, downstream inheritance, independent observations, contradiction/revision, professional responsibility, governance review, regulatory/data-provenance activity, expert questions and legal questions.

The repository succeeds when disagreement becomes precise enough for the correct real-world person or institution to answer it.

---

# 17. Information-opportunity test

For every named person or institutional actor, ask:

> **What information did this actor actually possess at this date?**

> **Was it direct, inherited or independently generated?**

> **What expertise and authority did the actor actually have?**

> **What opportunity did that combination create to examine, revise, correct, escalate, safeguard or communicate?**

> **What action followed?**

That is more reliable than assigning responsibility from title alone.
