# 14 — Real-World Team and Responsibility Map

## Purpose

This case concerns real people with different kinds of knowledge, authority, responsibility and evidential contribution.

The repository must not flatten them into abstract roles or assume that professional capability proves patient-specific participation.

The controlling rule is:

> **Capability ≠ participation ≠ bedside application.**

A person may possess relevant expertise without having participated in this patient's case. A person may have participated in discussion without having examined the patient. A person may receive a later record without having independent access to the events that generated it.

Responsibility must therefore follow actual information opportunity, professional role and patient-specific involvement at the relevant time.

---

# 1. The patient

## Unique contribution

The patient possesses lived continuity across the whole case.

He knows:

- what originally hurt and impaired function;
- what he experienced before, during and after the 2017 admission;
- what he remembers saying and being told;
- how symptoms changed with treatment, withdrawal and time;
- what he understood at each stage;
- when later records altered his understanding of earlier events;
- how pain, mobility and trust changed over the years.

## Evidential control

The patient's recollection is evidence. It is not automatically documentary fact merely because it is specific, and it is not a mere hypothetical merely because the surviving clinical record does not record it.

The patient is not retrospectively responsible for supplying the specialist neurological interpretation clinicians were retained to provide.

# 2. Dr Aaron Brennan — GP / pre-specialist comparator

## Position in the case

Brennan is important because he observed and referred the patient before the specialist discharge synthesis existed.

The pre-specialist record progressively joins painful abnormal feet, gait abnormality, muscle tension/tone, movement difficulty and deteriorating function into a neurological referral object.

## Contribution

Brennan can potentially provide evidence about:

- what the patient reported before Neurology;
- what he independently observed;
- why he considered Neurology necessary;
- how he framed the feet–gait–tone relationship before specialist classification;
- what later specialist information he received;
- what he independently observed in later primary care.

## Responsibility boundary

He is not the movement-disorders specialist and should not be treated as responsible for answering specialist neurological questions beyond the proper scope of general practice.

His value to the case is partly that he preserves an **independent pre-synthesis comparator**.

---

# 3. Dr Thomas Monaghan — treating/discharge neurologist and potential longitudinal custodian

## Position in the case

Monaghan sits near the authorial and responsibility centre of the case because the July 2017 specialist encounter, discharge synthesis and later longitudinal care are materially associated with him.

The same-clinician premise must still be verified event by event. Broad continuity does not prove that he personally authored, observed or received every later item.

## Distinct epistemic position

A final specialist summary can create an information state for downstream clinicians.

For a clinician who possessed the underlying encounter and helped create the representation, the same document may also constitute a **commitment state**: it records and exports an interpretation for which the author already possessed more direct access to the underlying encounter than later readers.

The working distinction is:

> **The summary changes knowledge downstream; it changes commitment upstream.**

## Central questions

- What did he personally know at each threshold?
- What did he personally observe?
- What did he write or approve?
- What did he know of the patient's foot complaint and alleged Protest?
- What later treatment-state and foot evidence reached him?
- What proposition, if any, lost confidence when contradictory evidence arrived?
- Did later observations produce local adjustment or global reconstruction?

## Control

Longitudinal involvement does not prove anchoring, negligence, bad faith, concealment or intentional maintenance. Those are separate evidential and expert/legal questions.

# 4. Prof / Dr Timothy J. Counihan — specialist movement-disorders capability and participation to be proved

## Position in the case

The project identifies relevant movement-disorders/Parkin expertise around Counihan and possible patient-specific participation in the treatment-decision environment.

The exact extent and timing of participation must be source-mapped.

## Required distinctions

Do not collapse:

1. **capability** — relevant expertise existed;
2. **participation** — expertise entered some aspect of this patient's case;
3. **bedside application** — that expertise returned to examination or integrated reconsideration of the unresolved feet–gait relationship.

## Central question

> **Did specialist expertise that enriched the theory return to the body?**

Institutional or individual expertise is a comparator, not proof that the expertise was applied to the disputed patient-specific problem.

# 5. Patrick Browne — Movement Disorders ANP / Parkinson nurse

## Position in the case

Relevant movement-disorders expertise appears in the later longitudinal care environment.

Unless source evidence establishes founding bedside involvement, Browne's early position should be treated as downstream rather than as a creator of the July 2017 information state.

## Central questions

- What information did he receive from the established record?
- What did he independently observe or elicit?
- When did he acquire enough patient-specific information to challenge or refine inherited assumptions?
- What did later severe-pain and movement-state interactions require within his professional role?

## Control

Professional expertise does not establish responsibility for a historical formulation that pre-dated participation.

# 6. Prof Daniel G. Healy — independent external specialist state

## Position in the case

Healy is analytically important because referral initially gives him a downstream information state, but once he independently examines the patient he begins generating new patient-specific evidence.

## Contribution

His assessment can therefore perform two different evidential functions:

- show what information was transmitted to an outside specialist; and
- provide independent clinical observations/opinions generated after his own assessment.

The later independent PARK2/Parkin direction and treatment observations can support the reality of the Parkinsonian disorder while remaining distinct from the unresolved painful-foot relationship.

## Control

Post-discharge independent evidence cannot be transported backward to prove what was known or done before discharge in July 2017.

# 7. Galway Neurology Department / institutional capability

## Position in the case

The department is relevant both as a clinical environment and as an information-producing institution.

It may contain sophisticated expertise in young-onset Parkinsonism, movement disorders and genetics.

## Control

Departmental capability does not establish that a named clinician personally knew, considered or applied particular knowledge in this patient's case.

The proper loop is:

> **observe → formulate → consult expertise → return to patient → test improved formulation → revise.**

The institutional question is not simply whether expertise existed but whether it entered the case and returned to the unresolved patient-specific problem.

---

# 8. Podiatry

## Position in the case

Podiatry documented and treated genuine focal mechanical/structural pathology.

This is real contrary evidence against any theory that all historical foot pain must have been neurological.

## Central contribution

Podiatry may know with high resolution:

- where mechanical loading occurred;
- where corns/calluses recurred;
- what deformities were present;
- what offloading/debridement achieved.

Neurology may know something different:

- how gait/posture changed;
- how the feet changed with neurological treatment state;
- whether a footdrop-like appearance was neurological;
- how OFF state, dystonia or dopamine state affected movement and pain.

The whole-case question is therefore:

> **Who owned the arrows between those truths?**

Podiatry being appropriate does not itself establish that neurological ownership of the relationship ended.

# 9. Quality & Patient Safety / PAR / SIMT reviewers

## Position in the case

Later governance reviewers generate institutional review outputs.

Those outputs are evidence of what the institution concluded.

Their weight as independent validation of the underlying care depends on what the reviewers actually examined.

## Central questions

- What was the review question?
- What primary records were examined?
- Was the Protest evidence before the reviewers?
- Was document-authorship chronology examined?
- Were later contradictions reconstructed independently?
- What clinician inputs were used?
- Was independent specialist expertise obtained?
- Did accuracy/restriction disputes accompany the data during review?

The current Article 15 provenance request seeks evidence capable of answering some of these questions.

# 10. Hospital Data Protection Officer / HSE-GUH controller

## Position in the case

The DPO/controller role is not to determine specialist neurological negligence.

Its relevance is to personal-data processing: access, source/provenance information, accuracy/completeness, restriction, rectification, recipients, audit history and present processing of the historical record.

## Current significance

The 31 August 2026 Article 15 request asks for provenance of the July 2017 synthesis and 2025 PAR/SIMT review.

The 2 September 2026 Article 16 request asks that the patient's supplementary account of the alleged pre-discharge Protest be durably associated with the historical synthesis while leaving the clinician-authored record intact.

These routes may generate new evidence about the information system; they do not themselves decide the underlying clinical case.

# 11. Data Protection Commission

## Position in the case

The DPC is the supervisory authority for data-protection complaints within its jurisdiction.

It can examine GDPR/data-processing issues but should not be treated as a substitute movement-disorders expert, medico-legal expert or court deciding the clinical standard of care.

## Current procedural map

- HSE/GUH complaint C-25-7-2074 remains a distinct DPC matter within the scope identified by the Commission.
- A separate University of Galway complaint carries reference DPC0826812075 and, as of 8 September 2026, had been transferred to CAERU for assessment.

Do not merge controllers, cases or competencies.

# 12. Independent clinical experts

## Proper role

Independent experts should not be asked to adopt the repository's theory.

They should be given the source-disciplined record and asked threshold-specific questions about:

- adequacy of July examination;
- significance of the dystonic-looking lower limb;
- what the alleged Protest would require if accepted;
- when later treatment-state evidence should cause reconsideration;
- mixed structural/neurological mechanisms;
- treatment calibration and toxicity;
- safeguarding and pain-related risk;
- causation and counterfactuals within their expertise.

The repository's job is to make those questions answerable, not answer them by assertion.

---

# 13. Lawyers / court

## Proper role

Legal advisers and, if necessary, a court determine the applicable legal tests and consequences.

Clinical evidence, documentary provenance and patient evidence should be organised so legal analysis can distinguish:

- duty;
- breach;
- causation;
- limitation/date of knowledge;
- evidential weight;
- documentation/record issues;
- regulatory overlap;
- recoverable harm.

The repository should not launder an expert-dependent clinical proposition into a legal conclusion merely because the argument is persuasive.

---

# 14. Analytical helper / repository function

The repository itself should act as an evidential integration layer rather than as a substitute professional.

Its job is to keep distinct and connected:

- patient lived evidence;
- clinician observations;
- interpretive propositions;
- document construction;
- downstream inheritance;
- independent observations;
- contradiction and revision;
- professional responsibility;
- governance review;
- regulatory/data-provenance activity;
- expert questions;
- legal questions.

The repository succeeds when disagreement becomes precise enough for the correct real-world person or institution to answer it.

---

# 15. Information-opportunity test

For every named person or institutional actor, ask:

> **What information did this actor actually possess at this date?**

> **Was it direct, inherited or independently generated?**

> **What expertise and authority did the actor actually have?**

> **What opportunity did that combination create to examine, revise, correct, escalate, safeguard or communicate?**

> **What action followed?**

That is more reliable than assigning responsibility from title alone.
