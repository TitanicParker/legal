## Controlling formulation

In July 2017 an integrated feet–gait–tone presentation reached Neurology. Neurology documented severe very-young-onset Parkinsonism, a striking footdrop-like gait appearance described as “somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture — NOT FOOT DROP,” and genuine structural foot disease. The DAT-scan indication later described the walk as “almost dystonically unusual.”

The final specialist synthesis nevertheless allowed the painful foot/leg phenomena to be “completely separate,” perhaps neuropathic, and ultimately “Not clear.”

The case is not that every foot symptom was dystonia, that structural disease was unreal, or that diagnostic uncertainty was negligent. The case is that a neurologically meaningful lower-limb relationship was positively visible and then repeatedly demonstrated by treatment state, while the record does not clearly show that the relationship was adequately characterised, integrated, carried forward into treatment planning, or later reconstructed.

## Case structure

### 1. July 2017 — founding assessment

The issue is whether reasonable neurological practice required a focused discrimination of weakness, rigidity, dystonic posturing, neuropathy, fixed structural restriction, loading and mixed mechanisms before the painful foot problem could be left separate / neuropathic / “Not clear.”

The surviving record shows substantial neurological work. It also does not clearly show the complete phenotype-directed bridge concerning distal power, sensory findings, ankle-specific reflexes, fixed versus dynamic/passively correctable foot posture, or the relationship between structure, posture, loading and pain.

Absence of a documented manoeuvre does not automatically prove it did not occur. But in an unusually detailed specialist record, the missing bridge is evidentially significant and requires explanation.

### 2. Protest — conditional decision point

If the patient proves that immediately before discharge he said, in substance, “you have not dealt with my feet,” that creates a separate decision-after-notice question. It does not rewrite the preceding admission.

### 3. 2017–2018 — treatment-state reopening

Post-discharge treatment materially changed the evidential position. Procyclidine/rasagiline produced substantial benefit. During planned procyclidine withdrawal, tension increased, the feet became restless and the walk deteriorated together. The treating neurologist later recorded that the outside-left-foot phenomenon was the first thing changing rapidly during withdrawal.

That is a positive neurological-treatment-state observation involving the feet. The unresolved expert question is whether and when reasonable care required the earlier separation to be reopened.

### 4. Podiatry and structural continuity

Podiatry was appropriate for genuine structural pathology and repeatedly documented focal fifth-MTPJ pathology, clawing, pronation, offloading and related mechanical findings.

But Podiatry did not answer the neurological relationship. The complete Podiatry record ends in 2019. After that, the structural/mechanical stream is no longer visibly active even though the feet remain symptomatic and later become severe.

The later question is therefore not simply whether Podiatry was an appropriate referral. It is who was responsible for re-establishing structural/mechanical assessment once the foot problem persisted, while Neurology was also accumulating treatment-state evidence.

### 5. 2022–2025 — treatment calibration and reconstruction

By 2022 the feet required 20–25 minutes to “warm up.” By 2023 there was severe bilateral foot/lower-leg pain, OFF/dystonia reasoning, major functional restriction and serious psychological-endurance language. In 2024 pregabalin reduced pain while toe curling persisted. By 2025 the treating service accepted that Sinemet altered the feet/pain and that treatment was constrained by dyskinesia.

The pharmacological history must be read as one sequence:

> **anticholinergic benefit → anticholinergic withdrawal causes rapid foot/gait/tension deterioration → levodopa-based treatment becomes the governing strategy → severe foot-pain burden develops within that treatment state → dose/formulation changes become clinically consequential → Sinemet CR produces profound relief.**

The later issue is therefore not merely that medication happened to improve pain. It is whether a foot syndrome already known to be neurologically modifiable was adequately carried forward into medication planning, titration and formulation choice.

### 6. Harm and causation

The relevant harm is not the existence of longstanding structural foot disease. It is the later transformation of that pre-existing condition into an intolerable pain state and associated psychological injury.

The key causation question is:

> **Did the neurological medication strategy cause or materially aggravate the severe foot-pain state, and did that aggravated pain state in turn cause or materially contribute to the documented psychological deterioration?**

This requires expert evidence, but it is a concrete counterfactual. The later profound improvement after change in levodopa formulation may be highly relevant to treatability and avoidability even if the exact pain mechanism remained mixed.

### 7. Safety / safeguarding

The later psychological evidence is central to harm. The record contains severe pain together with “death wish” language in 2023, “no quality of life,” later documentation of how small life had become, and by March 2025 a statement that the patient was not willing to live this way, with foot pain identified as the dominant concern.

On the complete Neurology record, no documented safeguarding or suicide-risk assessment has been identified in response to that sequence.

The expert question is what reasonable neurological practice required once severe pain and death-associated psychological distress were documented: risk assessment, safety planning, urgent mental-health input, communication with primary care/family, treatment review, or other action.

This safety route stands independently of the pharmacological causation route. Even if the cause of the pain remained disputed, the risk state still required an appropriate response.

## Evidential discipline

Keep the following limits:

- structural foot disease was genuine;
- Podiatry was legitimate for structural/mechanical care;
- “Not clear” could have been a reasonable endpoint after adequate assessment;
- absence of a documented manoeuvre does not automatically prove it did not occur;
- treatment response does not prove one exclusive mechanism;
- later PRKN genetics does not determine the 2017 standard of care;
- Protest remains a factual allegation unless proved;
- breach and causation must be assessed threshold by threshold.

But these are not permanent concessions. They apply only to the information state in which they were true. Later treatment-state evidence, disappearance of the active Podiatry stream, escalating pain, medication dependence and psychological deterioration require fresh analysis.

## Final positioning

> **An integrated lower-limb problem entered Neurology. Neurology positively identified a dystonic-looking lower-limb phenomenon alongside genuine structural foot disease but left the painful foot relationship unresolved. The July question is whether the necessary discrimination occurred. Independently, neurological treatment later demonstrated that the foot state was pharmacologically modifiable; Podiatry established recurrent mechanics but ceased from the available record after 2019; the feet then became increasingly disabling; and by 2023–2025 severe pain, OFF/dystonia reasoning, medication-state effects and profound relief after a formulation change made the treatment relationship clinically unavoidable. The later case is therefore not simply one of diagnostic uncertainty. It is whether an unresolved mixed foot syndrome was inadequately carried forward into structural follow-up, neurological treatment calibration and safeguarding, causing or materially aggravating an intolerable pain state and associated psychological harm.**

Shortest formulation:

> **The feet were structurally real, neurologically modifiable, later pharmacologically consequential, and ultimately psychologically dangerous. The case asks whether that progression was adequately recognised, owned and treated.**
