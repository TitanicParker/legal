## Controlling formulation

In July 2017 an integrated feet–gait–tone presentation reached Neurology. Neurology documented severe very-young-onset Parkinsonism, a striking footdrop-like gait appearance described as “somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture — NOT FOOT DROP,” and genuine structural foot disease. The same lower-limb abnormality was then used in the Parkinsonian diagnostic work-up: the DAT-scan indication described the walk as “almost dystonically unusual.”

The painful-foot relationship reached a different documentary endpoint. The final specialist synthesis affirmatively introduced the possibility that the leg/foot phenomena “may have been completely separate” or perhaps secondary peripheral neuropathy, ending “Not clear.”

The July issue is therefore not simply why the relationship was unresolved. It is:

> **What neurological and clinical work justified introducing a separate / peripheral-neuropathy explanatory branch for the painful feet when the same lower-limb presentation had already generated a dystonic-looking neurological sign used in the Parkinsonian work-up?**

The case is not that every foot symptom was dystonia, that structural disease was unreal, that peripheral neuropathy was impossible, or that diagnostic uncertainty was negligent. The case is that the specialist record created an affirmative alternative causal branch while the surviving July record does not clearly demonstrate the full relationship-specific discrimination by which that branch was earned.

## July 2017 — canonical documentary sequence

The focused July document `05_JULY_2017_FEET_ASSESSMENT_AND_DOCUMENTARY_DISPOSITION.md` isolates the sequence:

> **integrated referral → foot history → substantial neurological examination but limited recorded directed distal-foot findings → dystonic-looking lower-limb sign → use of dystonic gait in the DAT-scan formulation → affirmative separate/neuropathy branch → “Not clear.”**

The patient history is important defence evidence: he historically distinguished foot pain from leg tension, and genuine structural abnormalities were present.

The presently identified July examination records a striking abnormal gait, footdrop-like/dystonic-looking left-lower-limb movement, pes planus, hammertoes and mild/minimal oedema. It does not presently document a directed four-direction distal-foot power examination, plantar response, directed distal sensory findings supporting/excluding neuropathy, or an explicit fixed-versus-dynamic/posture-loading analysis.

Absence of a documented manoeuvre does not prove it did not occur. The expert question is what reasonable 2017 movement-disorders practice required and what the surviving record can safely establish.

## 2020 internal comparator

In 2020, when Neurology again addressed a foot-throw appearance resembling foot drop, the neurologist expressly documented normal power for dorsiflexion, plantarflexion, inversion and eversion and used those findings to classify the phenomenon as Parkinsonian.

This does not import the 2020 standard backwards into 2017. It is an internal documentary comparator showing how the same Neurology record later documented directed distal motor discrimination when resolving a similar foot-drop-like appearance.

A record-wide check of the current repaired canonical identifies `plantar` only in that later Neurology `plantarflexion` examination and in a Podiatry description of the plantar metatarsal area; no documented plantar examination has been identified in the July admission/discharge material.

## Authoritative uncertainty

“Not clear” can describe two materially different states:

- **procedural uncertainty** — the relationship remains unclear because the necessary investigation has not yet been completed;
- **substantive specialist uncertainty** — appropriate specialist discrimination has been performed and the relationship genuinely remains unclear.

The words themselves do not distinguish the two.

Because the conclusion appears within an unusually sophisticated specialist account, the controlled hypothesis is:

> **If the required relational work had not in fact been performed, the discharge may have converted procedural uncertainty into authoritative specialist uncertainty.**

That is a hypothesis for expert and documentary testing, not a finding of breach.

The repository does not assume what the GP actually understood. The objective question is what clinical work a referring GP could reasonably expect to underlie such a specialist disposition.

The integrated referral, dystonic-looking sign, DAT-scan use, limited recorded distal-foot material, separate/neuropathy branch and “Not clear” endpoint all exist independently in the documentary record.

## 2017–2018 — treatment-state reopening

Post-discharge treatment materially changed the evidential position. Procyclidine/rasagiline produced substantial benefit. During planned procyclidine withdrawal, tension increased, the feet became restless and the walk deteriorated together. The treating neurologist later recorded that the outside-left-foot phenomenon was the first thing changing rapidly during withdrawal.

That is a positive neurological-treatment-state observation involving the feet. The unresolved expert question is whether and when reasonable care required the earlier separation to be reopened.

## Podiatry and structural continuity

Podiatry was appropriate for genuine structural pathology and repeatedly documented focal fifth-MTPJ pathology, clawing, pronation, offloading and related mechanical findings.

But structural pathology is not the same as structural independence. Podiatry did not itself answer the neurological relationship among posture, movement state, loading and pain.

The complete available Podiatry stream ends in 2019. After that, the question becomes who was responsible for re-establishing structural/mechanical review if the feet remained symptomatic while neurological treatment-state evidence was also accumulating.

## 2022–2025 — reconstruction and treatment calibration

By 2022 the feet required 20–25 minutes to “warm up.” By 2023 there was severe bilateral foot/lower-leg pain, OFF/dystonia reasoning, major functional restriction and serious psychological-endurance language. In 2024 pregabalin reduced pain while toe curling persisted. By 2025 the treating service expressly recognised that Sinemet altered the feet/pain and that further dopaminergic treatment was constrained by dyskinesia.

The pharmacological history should be read as one sequence:

> **anticholinergic benefit → anticholinergic withdrawal causes rapid foot/gait/tension deterioration → levodopa-based treatment becomes the governing strategy → severe foot-pain burden develops within that treatment state → dose/formulation changes become clinically consequential → Sinemet CR produces profound relief.**

The later issue is not merely that medication happened to improve pain. It is whether a foot syndrome already known to be neurologically modifiable was adequately carried forward into medication planning, titration and formulation choice.

The 2025 material also weakens any claim that Neurology never knew Sinemet could affect morning pain or that CR was wholly patient-discovered. It strengthens the narrower calibration question: once patient-specific pain benefit and morning timing were known, when did reasonable care require a different timing/formulation strategy despite genuine dyskinesia and behavioural constraints?

## Documentary / informational causation

If expert evidence establishes that the July separate/neuropathy branch was insufficiently earned, the potentially causal event is not merely a missed diagnosis.

It may be that the specialist discharge changed the information environment inherited by the GP, patient and later clinicians by making an incompletely investigated relationship appear to have reached a specialist endpoint.

The disciplined causal chain is:

> **integrated referral → inadequate relational investigation, if proved → authoritative separate/neuropathy disposition → downstream reliance/inheritance, if proved → delayed or incomplete reopening, if proved → treatment consequences, if proved → defined additional harm.**

Each arrow requires evidence. Documentary/informational causation is distinct from medical causation and from legal causation.

## Harm and causation

The relevant harm is not the existence of longstanding structural foot disease or the underlying PRKN/Parkinsonian disorder. Nor should harm be assumed to have been uniformly severe from 2017 onward.

The key medical causation question is:

> **Did any identifiable failure of assessment, reintegration or neurological treatment calibration probably prolong or materially aggravate a defined later period of severe foot pain, disability or psychological harm beyond what would have occurred from the underlying disease, genuine structural pathology and unavoidable treatment constraints?**

The later profound improvement after change in levodopa formulation may be relevant to treatability and avoidability even if the exact pain mechanism remained mixed. It does not prove a single historical pain generator or an eight-year counterfactual.

## Safety / safeguarding

The later psychological evidence is central to harm. The record contains severe pain together with “death wish” language in 2023, “no quality of life,” later documentation of how small life had become, and by March 2025 a statement that the patient was not willing to live this way, with foot pain dominant.

On the complete Neurology record presently available, no documented structured safeguarding or suicide-risk assessment has been identified in response to that sequence.

The expert question is what reasonable neurological practice required once severe pain and death-associated psychological distress were documented: risk assessment, safety planning, urgent mental-health input, communication with primary care/family, treatment review, or other action.

This safety route stands independently of the pharmacological causation route. GP and nursing escalation must be carried as contrary/contextual evidence, and documentary silence must not be equated automatically with absence of action.
