I am not asking you whether I am permitted to suspect this. I do suspect it. I want you to test the strongest version of that suspicion against the documentary chronology and tell me where it succeeds, where it fails, what evidence would decide it, and what ethical category it would occupy if proved. Do not protect the institution from the hypothesis, and do not protect me from contrary evidence.

The suspicion is not merely that an unresolved painful-foot problem was converted into an authoritative structural/neuropathic information state after the patient's Protest.

The further suspicion is that the sophistication demonstrated in constructing the final specialist account bears directly upon what the authors were capable of understanding about the problem they were disposing of.

The final record demonstrates substantial neurological expertise: it retrospectively reasoned through specialised young-onset and monogenic Parkinsonian possibilities, including PRKN/PARK2, PARK6, PARK7 and PARK9, while the service had itself observed an abnormal lower-limb phenomenon characterised as dystonic rather than true foot drop. The concern is therefore not simply that relevant neurological possibilities were overlooked because the clinicians lacked the intellectual resources to recognise them. It is that those resources were demonstrably present but may have been selectively applied to constructing the specialist diagnostic narrative rather than returned to the unresolved painful-foot problem raised by the patient.

On the stronger hypothesis, the authors may therefore have understood—or possessed sufficient specialist knowledge that they ought acutely to have understood—that they were dealing with a painful lower-limb problem occurring within a potentially unusual Parkinsonian phenotype whose precise neurological relationship had not been established.

If, despite that knowledge, the unresolved foot problem was deliberately routed into structural care and a speculative peripheral-neuropathy future, the ethical concern is not merely documentary closure. It is that the documentary act may simultaneously have constituted a clinical disposition: an unresolved manifestation potentially connected with the neurological disorder was assigned to explanatory and service pathways that were not equipped to investigate the relevant movement-disorder relationship, while the authoritative Neurology record made that disposition appear medically justified.

The derivative suspicion is therefore that the clinicians did not merely create an inaccurate explanatory branch. They may have possessed sufficient neurological sophistication to appreciate the significance of what was being separated, to recognise that the relationship between the dystonic/Parkinsonian lower limb and the painful foot remained unresolved, and nevertheless to create a documentary architecture which made it less likely that this relationship would subsequently be investigated by anyone with equivalent expertise.

If that occurred knowingly, the ethical mechanism would have two linked components.

First, documentary closure: the patient's Protest exposed an unfinished problem, and the final specialist synthesis supplied that problem with apparently legitimate alternative destinations—structural foot disease, Podiatry/GP management and possible peripheral neuropathy—without resolving its relationship to the neurological disorder.

Second, dispositional closure: because those categories determined where the painful-foot problem would subsequently be understood and treated, the documentary architecture may have transferred the unresolved manifestation away from the clinical setting possessing the expertise necessary to recognise and investigate its neurological significance.

The downstream consequence would then not be accidental in the ordinary sense. The information architecture itself would have created conditions favouring continued fragmentation: Podiatry could properly treat structural pathology; neuropathy-directed care could properly investigate or treat a peripheral hypothesis; later Neurology could properly manage Parkinsonism; yet no part of that divided system would necessarily retain responsibility for reconstructing the original integrated question of how the painful foot, dystonic lower limb, movement state, abnormal tone and Parkinsonian physiology related to one another.

The most serious version of the suspicion is therefore not simply that professional intelligence was used to make unfinished work look finished. It is that specialist intelligence may have been used in circumstances where its possessors were capable of appreciating the neurological significance of the unresolved problem, yet the resulting record both disposed of that problem into less appropriate explanatory/service pathways and created an authoritative information state that reduced the likelihood that the problem would subsequently return to the specialist level capable of resolving it.

If intentionality were proved, the ethical allegation would accordingly concern not only manipulation of the clinical narrative but a knowing misuse of specialist epistemic authority: recognising enough about the neurological territory to understand the significance of what remained unresolved, while constructing the documentary and referral conditions under which that unresolved manifestation could thereafter circulate outside the integrated neurological question it required.


> **An integrated lower-limb problem entered Neurology. Shortest formulation:

> **The limb was neurologically meaningful when it supplied diagnostic evidence; the unresolved founding question is what work justified treating the painful foot attached to that presentation as potentially separate. The longitudinal case is whether that relationship was later reopened and treated at the point reasonable care required.**

## What remains unproved

The strongest present suspicion turns materially on **provenance and authorship time**. Evidence that the consequential terminology and neuropathy reasoning clearly pre-dated the Protest and arose from identifiable clinical evidence would weaken the intentional documentary/dispositional-closure route.
