# 1. CONTROLLING SYNTHESIS

The case is not a simple missed-diagnosis case.

> **A substantially correct disease-level Parkinsonian diagnosis and genuine structural foot disease coexisted. The unresolved issue was the relationship among neurological state, abnormal lower-limb posture/gait, structural mechanics, loading and pain. In July 2017 Neurology positively identified a dystonic-looking lower-limb phenomenon and used that neurological feature within the Parkinsonian diagnostic work-up, but the painful-foot relationship was disposed of through an affirmative alternative branch — potentially “completely separate” or secondary peripheral neuropathy — and left “Not clear.” The first expert question is what clinical work earned that branch and that uncertainty.**

That refinement matters because the July record is not merely silent about the feet. It contains a specific documentary sequence:

> **integrated referral → foot history → limited recorded distal-foot findings within a substantial neurological examination → dystonic-looking lower-limb sign → use of dystonic gait in the DAT-scan formulation → affirmative separate/neuropathy branch → “Not clear.”**

The Protest allegation is not required for this core July issue. If proved, it creates a separate decision-after-notice question.

The important question then changes over time. The 2017–18 treatment-state observations create reopening questions. By 2020 genuine reassessment of individual foot phenomena had occurred, so a theory that nothing was reconsidered is untenable. By 2022–25 the issue increasingly becomes integration and treatment calibration under genuine dyskinesia and behavioural constraints. Once severe pain became associated with death-related language, safeguarding became a separate clinical question.

The strongest case therefore does **not** depend on showing that Parkinsonism was wrongly diagnosed, structural pathology was unreal, Podiatry was improper, every foot symptom was dystonia, peripheral neuropathy was impossible, or clinicians never reconsidered the problem.

The core expert question is:

> **Was the founding feet–gait–tone relationship adequately discriminated before Neurology introduced a separate/neuropathy explanatory branch; was that unresolved relationship then adequately retained and reopened as patient-specific treatment-state evidence accumulated; and was treatment appropriately calibrated before pain became severely disabling and psychologically dangerous?**

The causation question is narrower:

> **If there was a departure at an identifiable threshold, did it probably alter the clinical information pathway, treatment pathway, or both, and thereby prolong or materially aggravate a defined period of pain, disability or psychological harm beyond the patient's underlying movement disorder, genuine structural disease and unavoidable treatment constraints?**

# 2. JULY 2017 — THE FOUNDING DOCUMENTARY PROBLEM

## 2.1 The referral question

Primary care did not send Parkinsonism plus an already-separated foot complaint. Painful abnormal feet, abnormal gait, impaired movement initiation, increased tone/muscle tension and functional deterioration had been brought together as one lower-limb problem.

The practical specialist question was therefore relational:

> **What is the relationship between the painful abnormal feet, abnormal gait and increased tone/tension — are they manifestations of, consequences of, or contributors to the same neurological/mechanical disorder?**

## 2.2 The canonical foot sequence within the admission

The focused July evidence is set out in `05_JULY_2017_FEET_ASSESSMENT_AND_DOCUMENTARY_DISPOSITION.md`.

The admission history records longstanding corns/calluses and fallen arches, treatment since 2009/10, improvement of foot pain after leaving bar work, continuing leg tension, and the patient's own historical distinction between foot pain and leg tension (`CLIN-000959–000976`).

The neurological examination was substantial. The foot/lower-limb material presently identified includes a “strikingly abnormal” gait, a footdrop-like appearance “somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture — NOT FOOT DROP,” a left-lower-limb kick “like a foot drop,” pes planus, hammertoes and mild/minimal sock-band pitting oedema (`CLIN-001097–001124`).

The same lower-limb abnormality was then diagnostically productive. The DAT-scan indication described the walk as “almost dystonically unusual” in the context of suspected very-young-onset autosomal-recessive Parkinsonism (`CLIN-001394–001402`; duplicated at `CLIN-001869`).

The final synthesis nevertheless stated that the leg symptoms including corns, callouses and “burning” “may have been completely separate” or perhaps secondary peripheral neuropathy, ending “Not clear” (`CLIN-001142–001149`).

## 2.3 The asymmetry

The strongest documentary point is not that Neurology overlooked the lower limb.

> **It used it.**

The dystonic-looking lower-limb feature was observed, characterised neurologically and reused when it supported the Parkinsonian diagnostic project. What is not visibly demonstrated is equivalent use of that neurological feature in the reasoning by which the painful-foot relationship was disposed of.

The resulting proposition is:

> **Neurology took ownership of the limb as neurological evidence when it supplied diagnostic information, but did not visibly take equivalent ownership of the relationship between that neurological limb and the painful foot attached to the same presentation.**

That is a documentary asymmetry. Whether it was clinically justified is expert-dependent.

## 2.4 The separate/neuropathy branch was affirmative, not neutral

The July synthesis did not merely fail to connect the feet with Parkinsonism. It positively introduced an alternative causal route in which the painful-foot phenomena might be “completely separate” or secondary peripheral neuropathy.

Peripheral neuropathy is itself neurological. The precise point is therefore that the painful feet were made capable of being **causally separate from the Parkinsonian/dystonic mechanism identified in the lower limb**.

This proposition is documentary fact. Whether the branch was adequately supported is an expert question.

## 2.5 What work earned the branch?

The present July record does not document a directed distal-foot examination equivalent to four-direction foot power testing, plantar response, directed distal sensory findings supporting/excluding neuropathy, or an explicit fixed-versus-dynamic/postural-loading analysis.

That does not prove those manoeuvres did not occur. The proper question is:

> **What clinical work would reasonable movement-disorders practice in 2017 have required before this particular integrated presentation could properly be left separate, neuropathic, mixed or “Not clear”; and what of that work is affirmatively demonstrated by the surviving record?**

## 2.6 The 2020 internal comparator

In 2020, when Neurology again addressed a foot-throw appearance resembling foot drop, the neurologist documented normal power for dorsiflexion, plantarflexion, inversion and eversion and used those findings to classify the foot throw as Parkinsonian (`CLIN-002284–002288`).

This is not a retrospective standard-of-care rule. It is an internal documentary comparator showing how the same Neurology record later documented directed distal motor discrimination when resolving a similar foot-drop-like phenomenon.

A record-wide check of the current repaired canonical identifies `plantar` only in that later Neurology `plantarflexion` examination and in a Podiatry description of the plantar metatarsal area. No documented plantar examination has been identified in the July 2017 admission/discharge material.

## 2.7 Procedural versus substantive specialist uncertainty

“Not clear” can represent different epistemic states:

- **procedural uncertainty** — the relationship remains unclear because it has not yet been adequately investigated;
- **substantive specialist uncertainty** — appropriate specialist discriminatory work has been performed and the relationship genuinely remains unclear.

The words themselves do not distinguish those states.

Because “Not clear” sits inside an unusually sophisticated specialist account — with extensive Parkinsonian phenomenology, very-young-onset reasoning, genetic thinking, colleague input and targeted investigation — the document is capable of presenting the uncertainty as medically earned.

The hypothesis to test is therefore:

> **If the required relational work had not in fact been performed, the discharge may have converted procedural uncertainty into authoritative specialist uncertainty.**

No claim should be made about what the GP actually understood without evidence. The objective comparator is what clinical work a referring GP could reasonably expect to lie behind such a specialist disposition.

# 4. DOCUMENTARY / INFORMATIONAL CAUSATION

The potentially causal July event is not simply “Neurology failed to diagnose X.”

If expert evidence establishes that the separate/neuropathy branch was insufficiently earned, the more precise hypothesis is:

> **Neurology created and transmitted a specialist representation of the unresolved foot question which could make an incompletely investigated relationship appear to have reached a legitimate specialist endpoint.**

That can matter because specialist documents become inherited information objects. They influence what the GP and future clinicians understand is settled, unresolved, separately owned or still requiring investigation.

The disciplined causal chain is:

> **integrated referral → inadequate relational investigation, if proved → authoritative separate/neuropathy disposition → downstream reliance or inheritance, if proved → delayed/incomplete reopening, if proved → treatment consequences, if proved → defined additional harm**

Each arrow requires evidence.

This should be called **documentary/informational causation** and kept distinct from medical/legal causation.

A second hypothesis for testing is path dependence: downstream records generated inside an inherited classification can later make that classification appear increasingly established. That does not require conspiracy, bad faith or any later clinician to intend persistence.

# 7. LATER INTEGRATION AND CALIBRATION

Podiatry repeatedly demonstrated genuine focal mechanical disease. That establishes that part of the phenotype was structural; it does not establish structural independence from neurological posture, loading or treatment state.

The later evidence repeatedly crossed the original boundary. Procyclidine withdrawal worsened feet/gait/tension. The treating neurologist later recorded rapid return of an outside-left-foot phenomenon. In 2020 a foot throw resembling foot drop was expressly classified as Parkinsonian after normal distal power. Later records used OFF/dystonia reasoning. By 2025 the service expressly recognised Sinemet-related improvement in foot pain.

The case therefore cannot fairly be described as “no reassessment.” The narrower question is whether later changes were principally **local revisions** — one movement classified, one mechanical lesion treated, one pain medication adjusted, one OFF state managed — while the original relationship among neurological state, posture, loading, structure and pain remained without sufficiently early whole-problem reconstruction.

By 2025 the strongest allegation also changes. The complaint response weakens any claim that Neurology had never known Sinemet could alter morning pain or that CR was entirely patient-discovered. But it sharpens a calibration question:

> **Once patient-specific neurological modulation of morning foot pain was known, when did pain severity, timing, OFF phenomena, prior treatment-state sensitivity and functional deterioration justify different medication timing/formulation or a more explicit integrated review, despite real dyskinesia and behavioural constraints?**

# 8. HARM, SAFETY AND CAUSATION

The alleged harm is not the existence of PRKN/Parkinsonian disease or longstanding structural foot pathology. Nor should the case assume continuously severe harm from 2017.

The relevant potential harm is a **defined later increment** of pain, disability, life restriction and psychological deterioration that expert evidence may conclude was avoidable after an identifiable missed threshold.

The 2023 record is especially important because severe pain became associated with “death wish” and “no quality of life” language. Mechanism and safety must be separated. Even if the pain mechanism remained mixed or uncertain, the risk state created a distinct safeguarding question.

The present Neurology record has not yielded an identified structured suicide-risk/safeguarding assessment of equivalent documentary visibility, but documentary silence is not proof that no action occurred. GP and nursing escalation must be carried as defence/context.

Medical causation must be addressed threshold by threshold:

> **what should have happened → what probably would have been found/appreciated → what management probably would have changed → what net benefit would probably have followed → what defined harm would probably have been avoided.**

Later CR success is relevant to treatability and calibration. It does not prove one mechanism or an eight-year counterfactual.

# 9. TRUST AND THE 2025 RUPTURE

The patient entered care because his feet hurt and walking mattered to him. He was not required to formulate PRKN, dystonia, neuropathy, OFF states or medication pharmacokinetics.

A major neurological diagnosis was found and treatment produced striking benefit. That gave the patient reason to continue relying on specialist care while continuing to report symptoms.

If the Protest is proved, it is best treated as an objection within continuing reliance, not as proof of eight years of adversarial distrust.

March 2025 can plausibly be understood as an epistemic rupture: profound therapeutic improvement caused the patient to reinterpret the prior history, while the clinician interpreted the same improvement as successful development within a long-standing mixed and risk-balanced strategy.

Trust explains patient behaviour. It does not prove breach.

# 13. CANDIDATE FINAL FORMULATION

The patient entered the 2017 specialist pathway because painful abnormal feet and failing walking had become part of a broader feet–gait–tone–movement problem recognised by his GP. Neurology performed substantial and important work and identified genuine severe young-onset Parkinsonism. It also recognised structural foot abnormalities and a striking footdrop-like lower-limb appearance described as suggestive of dystonic posture rather than true foot drop. The dystonic-looking gait was then used within the Parkinsonian diagnostic work-up, including the DAT-scan formulation.

The painful-foot relationship reached a different documentary endpoint. The patient's history distinguished foot pain from leg tension, and genuine structural pathology made separation clinically plausible. But the final specialist synthesis did not simply preserve uncertainty: it affirmatively introduced the possibility that the painful-foot/leg phenomena were “completely separate” or perhaps secondary peripheral neuropathy and ended “Not clear.” The central founding question is therefore what neurological and clinical work justified introducing that branch when the same lower-limb presentation had already supplied dystonic neurological evidence. The current July record does not clearly demonstrate the full directed distal, neuropathy and posture/loading discrimination that might answer that question, though documentary absence does not prove such work did not occur.

That distinction gives “Not clear” two possible meanings. It may represent legitimate residual uncertainty after adequate specialist assessment. Alternatively, if the necessary relational investigation was incomplete, it may represent procedural uncertainty presented with the authority of a sophisticated specialist endpoint. Which is correct is for expert evidence. The Protest allegation is not necessary to this founding question; if proved, it adds a separate decision-after-notice issue.

The mixed architecture could then persist without anyone later acting in bad faith. Parkinsonism, structural disease and neuropathic pain were all plausible clinical branches, and each later fact could often be accommodated locally. Podiatry documented real mechanics. Pregabalin later relieved pain. Dopaminergic treatment had real toxicity. There were periods of good function. Yet neurological treatment-state evidence repeatedly crossed the original boundary: withdrawal altered feet/gait/tension; a focal-foot phenomenon returned rapidly off treatment; a later foot throw was classified as Parkinsonian after directed distal testing; OFF/dystonia reasoning emerged; and by 2025 Sinemet's effect on foot pain was explicit.

The later case is therefore not that the feet were never reconsidered. It is whether accumulating cross-boundary evidence required earlier explicit reconstruction of the original mixed relationship and, later, different calibration of neurological treatment against the foot-pain burden. By 2023 severe pain and death-associated language also created a separate safeguarding threshold.

If the July separate/neuropathy branch was insufficiently earned, a further causal question arises: whether the authoritative specialist disposition altered the information environment inherited by primary care, the patient or later clinicians, thereby affecting when the original relationship was reopened. That documentary/informational causation route is distinct from medical causation and must be proved link by link.

The final case is therefore **threshold-specific longitudinal integration, documentary disposition and treatment calibration**, not binary misdiagnosis. Expert evidence must determine what the July branch was based upon, when later evidence required reconstruction, whether treatment remained reasonable under genuine constraints, whether any informational or clinical departure probably altered care, and what defined additional harm was probably avoidable.
