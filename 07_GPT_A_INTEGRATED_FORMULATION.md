# 1. CONTROLLING SYNTHESIS

The three independent reconstructions converge on a narrower and more durable architecture than a simple missed-diagnosis theory.

> **A substantially correct disease-level Parkinsonian diagnosis and genuine structural foot disease coexisted, but the relationship among neurological state, abnormal foot posture/gait, mechanical loading and pain remained less resolved than the component diagnoses themselves. That unresolved relationship then became distributed across clinically legitimate pathways and could persist because later facts were usually explainable within one branch or another.**

The important question changes over time.

July 2017 concerns the adequacy of the founding phenotype and the basis for leaving the painful-foot relationship unresolved. The 2017–18 treatment-state observations create reopening questions. By 2020–23 genuine reassessment had occurred, so a theory that nothing was reconsidered is untenable. By 2022–25 the issue increasingly becomes treatment-state integration and medication calibration under genuine dyskinesia and behavioural constraints. Once severe pain became associated with death-related language, safeguarding became a separate clinical question.

The strongest case therefore does **not** depend on showing that Parkinsonism was wrongly diagnosed, structural pathology was unreal, Podiatry was improper, all foot pain was dystonia, or clinicians never reconsidered the problem.

The core expert question is:

> **Was an initially unresolved mixed neurological–mechanical relationship adequately characterised, retained as a live clinical problem, reopened as patient-specific treatment-state evidence accumulated, and translated into proportionate treatment calibration before the pain became severely disabling and psychologically dangerous?**

The causation question is narrower:

> **If there was a departure at an identifiable threshold, did it probably prolong or materially aggravate a defined period of pain, disability or psychological harm beyond the patient's underlying movement disorder, genuine structural disease and unavoidable treatment constraints?**

# 4. HIGH-VALUE INSIGHTS PRESERVED FROM THE THREE INDEPENDENT RESPONSES

## 4.1 The adverse case survives only if it stops trying to disprove the clinicians' categories

The case becomes stronger when it permits several clinician propositions to be true at once:

- Parkinsonism was real;
- structural pathology was real;
- Podiatry was legitimate;
- pregabalin could help;
- Sinemet could modulate pain without proving its generator;
- dyskinesia could rationally constrain treatment.

**Working consequence:** the alleged problem is the relationship among true categories, not the need to show that one category was fictitious.

## 4.2 “Uncertainty became operationally organised before it became biologically resolved”

This is the clearest description of the suspected mechanism. Provisional uncertainty acquired pathways and responsibilities before the relationship itself was settled.

**Working consequence:** use this as an internal explanatory model, not as a proved fact.

## 4.3 Asymmetrical success

The Parkinsonism/PRKN question became highly productive and sophisticated while the feet–loading–pain relationship remained comparatively unresolved.

**Working consequence:** ask whether the difference in resolution was clinically justified, not whether the sophisticated Parkinsonism work was somehow false.

## 4.4 Branch-level revisions versus whole-problem reconstruction

Later care contains genuine reassessment. The narrower issue is whether local revisions were sufficient or whether the original cross-boundary relationship required deliberate reconstruction at an earlier threshold.

## 4.5 Path dependence without conspiracy

A strong specialist account naturally becomes portable and inherited. That is ordinary continuity of care. The risk is only that an unresolved boundary within a strong portable account may become durable.

## 4.6 “The model was good enough to explain most new facts”

A mixed model can survive because every new datum has a plausible local home: structural lesions, neuropathic-type pain, Parkinsonian movement, OFF symptoms, pregabalin response, Sinemet response and dyskinesia.

**Working consequence:** persistence does not require deliberate anchoring.

## 4.7 Epistemic trust

The patient could continue supplying observations while delegating their medical interpretation. March 2025 may mark the moment when therapeutic success changed what he believed the prior history meant.

# 11. CANDIDATE FINAL FORMULATION

The patient entered the 2017 specialist pathway because of a practical problem that mattered to him before any diagnosis existed: his feet hurt and walking was becoming difficult. Primary care identified genuine structural abnormalities, but it also identified abnormal gait, impaired movement initiation, increased tone, brisk reflexes, toe clawing and deteriorating function. The problem referred to Neurology was therefore not simply “corns” and not simply “Parkinsonism”; it was an open feet–gait–tone–movement problem whose mechanism had not yet been worked out. The patient supplied the symptoms and functional consequences; his GP supplied the professional translation; the patient then relied on specialist care to determine what those findings meant.

Neurology did substantial and important work. It documented a severe young-onset Parkinsonian syndrome, recognised genuine structural abnormalities including pes planus and hammertoes, and described the striking footdrop-like gait as “somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture — NOT FOOT DROP.” The Parkinsonian syndrome then became highly resolved through investigation, specialist reasoning, colleague input, treatment planning and a contemporaneous PARK2 hypothesis later supported by Professor Healy. The painful-foot relationship, however, reached a different endpoint: corns, calluses and burning were capable of being treated as separate or perhaps neuropathic, and the synthesis said “Not clear.” That uncertainty may have been reasonable. The important longitudinal question is what happened to the uncertainty afterwards.

The best reconstruction from the present record is not that the clinicians chose a wholly false explanation. It is that a genuinely mixed presentation became organised into clinically legitimate but asymmetrically resolved pathways before the relationship among them was fully settled. Parkinsonism had Neurology, treatment and genetic reasoning. Structural foot disease had Podiatry. Neuropathic-type pain remained another plausible branch. What the current record does not clearly demonstrate is an equally durable clinical owner, feedback loop or reopening rule for the relationship among neurological state, foot posture, mechanical loading and pain. This is an inference from the record architecture, not proof that no such reasoning occurred.

That architecture could persist precisely because it was not wholly wrong. Podiatry repeatedly demonstrated real focal mechanical disease and provided useful local treatment. The patient sometimes distinguished pain from tension. Later pregabalin materially reduced pain while toe curling persisted. Neurological treatment itself carried real costs, including dyskinesia/chorea and behavioural or impulse-control concerns. There were also periods of substantial recovery and high function. Each fact made a mixed model clinically plausible and prevented any single later observation from proving one exclusive mechanism.

But later evidence repeatedly crossed the original boundary. During procyclidine withdrawal in 2017, the patient's feet, gait and tension deteriorated together. In April 2018 the treating neurologist recorded that an outside-left-foot phenomenon was the first thing changing rapidly during that withdrawal. Those facts did not prove that the structural lesion was neurological or that all pain was dystonia. They did establish that the foot domain could change with neurological treatment state, creating an early question about whether the founding relationship required reopening. Later Neurology did genuinely reassess individual phenomena: by 2020 a foot-throw appearance was classified as Parkinsonian after normal distal power, and later records used OFF/dystonia and pain-modulation reasoning. The case therefore cannot fairly be described as eight years in which the feet were never reconsidered.

The narrower question is whether those later revisions remained mainly local — one movement reclassified, one mechanical lesion treated, one pain mechanism medicated, one OFF state adjusted — without sufficiently early reconstruction of the original mixed feet–gait–tone–loading–pain problem as a whole. That is a hypothesis for expert testing, not a documentary finding. The strongest early candidate reopening point is the 2018 clinician-held treatment-state foot observation; later candidate thresholds include the 2020 Parkinsonian foot classification, the 2022 morning warm-up pattern and the much more serious 2023 pain crisis.

By 2023 the clinical problem had changed in severity and consequence. The GP urgently described extreme bilateral burning pain as “causing death wish”, expressed worry and sought specialist help before “we get into trouble here”; Parkinson's nursing recorded debilitating pain and “no quality of life.” At the same time the evidence remained mixed: the patient reported ten days off Parkinson medication without perceiving a Parkinsonian difference, and pregabalin had some effect. Those contrary facts matter. Whatever caused the pain, however, its severity and the death-associated language created a separate safety question. The present corpus shows urgent escalation by primary care and nursing; it does not presently identify a structured Neurology suicide-risk/safeguarding assessment of equivalent documentary visibility. That is a documentary limitation, not proof that no safeguarding action occurred.

By 2024 the mixed character was even clearer: pregabalin had removed much of the foot pain while toe curling persisted, and activity remained constrained. By March 2025 the feet were again dominant after substantial pregabalin reduction. The clinical note records severe morning pain, improvement once Sinemet reached effect, the neurologist's view that additional Sinemet would probably provide further pain relief, the competing risk of severe dyskinesia, recognition of the overnight medication gap and a plan for controlled-release Sinemet. The 25 March complaint response then states that Sinemet's benefit for morning pain had been known, that pregabalin had been used partly to spare further Sinemet increase, that pregabalin reduction was followed by worsening pain, and that the CR change had produced profound relief.

That 2025 material changes the strongest formulation. It weakens an allegation that Neurology had never recognised neurological modulation of the pain or that CR introduced a wholly unknown therapeutic idea. But, if the clinician's own explanation is accepted, it sharpens a different question: **once Sinemet's effect on morning foot pain was known, when did pain severity, morning timing, OFF phenomena, prior treatment-state sensitivity and functional deterioration justify a different calibration of timing/formulation or a more explicit integrated review, despite genuine dyskinesia and behavioural constraints?** The defence and the criticism therefore arise from the same facts.

The patient's relationship with the clinicians matters to understanding the chronology, though not to proving liability. The treating neurologist later said he had believed the relationship was very good and described conversations on a “Tom and Liam” basis. If the patient's recalled pre-discharge Protest is proved, the later history is more consistent with an objection made within continuing reliance than with the beginning of an eight-year adversarial dispute. The patient continued to report symptoms while largely entrusting their interpretation to clinicians. March 2025 appears to have altered that relationship because the therapeutic result caused him to reinterpret the history. The patient saw profound relief as evidence that an old problem might have been more neurologically modifiable than he had understood; the clinician saw the same improvement as a successful development within a long-standing, risk-balanced treatment strategy. The rupture was therefore, at least plausibly, a dispute over the meaning of the past before it became a medico-legal dispute.

The case is therefore best formulated as a **threshold-specific longitudinal integration and treatment-calibration case**, not as a binary misdiagnosis case. The central expert questions are whether the founding mixed phenotype was adequately characterised; whether later patient-specific treatment-state evidence required the unresolved neurological–mechanical relationship to be explicitly re-phenotyped or re-integrated at an identifiable threshold; whether medication timing and formulation were appropriately calibrated once neurological modulation of the feet/pain was recognised; and, if any departure occurred, whether it probably prolonged or materially aggravated a defined later period of pain, disability or psychological harm beyond the patient's underlying movement disorder, genuine structural disease and unavoidable treatment constraints. Independently, once severe pain became associated with death-related language, the adequacy of safeguarding requires separate assessment.

This formulation does not claim that every foot symptom was dystonia, that Podiatry was wrong, that earlier levodopa would necessarily have helped, that later genetics proves earlier breach, or that profound CR relief proves eight years of avoidable pain. Those matters remain for expert evidence and, in some areas, further documentary disclosure.

# 12. MEDIUM COMPRESSION

The patient entered Neurology in 2017 because painful abnormal feet and failing walking had become part of a broader feet–gait–tone–movement problem recognised by his GP. Neurology performed a substantial assessment and correctly identified a severe young-onset Parkinsonian syndrome, while also recognising genuine structural foot disease and a footdrop-like gait appearance considered potentially dystonic. The Parkinsonian diagnosis then acquired high diagnostic, genetic and treatment resolution, whereas the relationship between painful feet, neurological posture, gait/loading and structure remained capable of being separate or neuropathic and was left “Not clear.”

The strongest reconstruction is not that either branch was simply wrong. Podiatry treated real mechanical pathology; Neurology treated real Parkinsonism; pregabalin later relieved pain; and dopaminergic treatment produced genuine dyskinesia and behavioural constraints. The possible problem lies in the relationship among those true things. Later evidence repeatedly crossed the original boundary: procyclidine withdrawal worsened feet/gait/tension; a focal-foot phenomenon returned rapidly off treatment; a later foot throw was classified as Parkinsonian; OFF/dystonia reasoning emerged; and by 2025 Sinemet was expressly known to improve the feet/pain.

The 2025 record weakens any claim that neurological pain modulation or CR was completely overlooked. It strengthens the narrower expert question of **calibration**: once treatment-state benefit was known, when did morning timing, severe pain, function and OFF evidence justify a different timing/formulation strategy or integrated review despite a real dyskinesia ceiling?

The case is therefore threshold-specific. Expert evidence must determine whether and when the mixed relationship required explicit re-phenotyping/reintegration, whether actual medication calibration remained reasonable, and whether any departure probably added a defined later period of pain, disability or psychological injury. Safeguarding after the 2023 death-associated pain language is a separate question.

---

# 13. SHORT COMPRESSION

> **A genuine young-onset Parkinsonian disorder and genuine structural foot disease were both recognised, but their relationship remained unresolved and was managed through partly separate neurological, Podiatry and pain pathways. Later treatment-state evidence repeatedly showed that the feet, gait and pain were not wholly independent of neurological state, while real mechanical disease, pregabalin benefit, good-function periods and dyskinesia/behavioural toxicity preserved a genuinely mixed picture. The case is not that nothing was reconsidered or that all pain was dystonia. It is whether accumulating evidence eventually required explicit re-integration and different treatment calibration, and whether any missed threshold probably prolonged a defined period of severe pain, disability or psychological harm. The 2023 safety question is independent.**

---

# 14. ONE-SENTENCE CORE

> **The case is whether a legitimately mixed but unresolved neurological–mechanical foot problem became durably divided into workable clinical branches and, as later treatment-state evidence accumulated, was not re-integrated or treatment-calibrated at the point reasonable care required, thereby causing or materially aggravating a defined later period of pain and psychological harm, with safeguarding arising independently once that pain became psychologically dangerous.**

---

# 15. PATIENT-CENTRED CORE

The patient did not enter care with a theory of PRKN, dystonia, neuropathy, OFF states or medication formulation. He entered because his feet hurt and walking mattered to him. His GP translated what the patient could report into a clinical problem that joined feet, gait, tone and movement, and the patient relied on Neurology to work out the specialist meaning.

A major neurological diagnosis was found and treatment produced striking benefit. That gave the patient reason to continue trusting the care even though the feet remained a recurring problem. If his 2017 Protest is proved, it is better understood as an objection that the original practical problem remained unfinished, not as evidence that he then possessed the later medical theory or had withdrawn trust.

Over the following years the patient continued to supply observations: what happened on treatment, off treatment, in the morning, while standing, while walking and as pain worsened. He was not required to know which observations should trigger a reformulation. By 2023 the pain had become associated with severe functional and psychological distress. In 2025 a change in treatment produced profound relief. That experience caused the patient to look backward and ask whether information that had been present while he was trusting the system should have been connected differently.

The medico-legal issue is not whether the patient's retrospective mechanism is necessarily correct. It is whether the professionals responsible for interpreting the evolving record responded reasonably to the information available at each threshold.

---

# 16. CENTRAL SUSPECTED MECHANISM — SEPARATE FROM PROOF

> **Working suspicion:** uncertainty was clinically organised before the mixed relationship was biologically resolved. The Parkinsonian disorder became a high-resolution Neurology/genetics/treatment problem, structural disease became a legitimate Podiatry problem, and neuropathic pain remained an available explanatory branch. Because all of those categories contained truth, later observations could usually be incorporated locally without falsifying the overall architecture. The unresolved relationship among motor state, foot posture, loading and pain may therefore have become path-dependent and comparatively weakly owned, not through deliberate neglect but through ordinary longitudinal inheritance and specialty-specific updating.

**Status:** reasonable synthesis hypothesis only.

**What would materially weaken it:** contemporaneous records demonstrating detailed integrated foot phenotyping, explicit cross-specialty ownership/return criteria and repeated whole-problem reconstruction at the major later thresholds.

---

# 17. FINAL CONTROL RULES

Any future refinement should preserve these rules:

1. Do not make the case depend on disproving true clinician observations.
2. Do not turn “Not clear” into negligence without asking what examination and reasoning earned it.
3. Do not describe later care as “no reassessment.”
4. Do not erase Podiatry or genuine structural pathology.
5. Do not erase dyskinesia or behavioural treatment constraints.
6. Do not erase good-function intervals or the 2023 ten-day medication-stop contrary evidence.
7. Do not treat pregabalin response as proof of neuropathy or Sinemet response as proof of dystonia.
8. Do not use 2026 genetics to decide the 2017 standard.
9. Do not use CR response to prove an eight-year counterfactual.
10. Keep safeguarding separate from pain causation.
11. Keep the patient's trust separate from proof of clinical breach.
12. At every stage ask: **what was knowable then, what changed, what did reasonable care require then, and what additional harm would probably have been avoided?**

# 18. CURRENT SYNTHESIS POSITION

The independent responses have narrowed rather than enlarged the case.

Their strongest common insight is:

> **A disease-level diagnosis can be substantially right while the clinically important relationship among symptoms remains insufficiently resolved.**

Their strongest explanatory insight is:

> **The mixed model may have persisted because it was good enough to explain most new facts locally.**

Their strongest adverse proposition is:

> **Repeated patient-specific treatment-state evidence may have required an unresolved structural–neurological relationship to be explicitly reopened, re-phenotyped and later treatment-calibrated at an identifiable threshold.**

Their strongest defence is:

> **The disease was genuinely mixed, the structural pathology was real, later reassessment occurred, periods of function were good, and medication escalation carried genuine patient-specific harm.**

Their strongest causation discipline is:

> **Any compensable medical harm must be defined as an incremental later harm period supported by expert counterfactual evidence, not assumed to encompass the patient's entire pre-existing foot history.**

Their strongest independent safety proposition is:

> **Once severe pain was accompanied by death-associated and no-quality-of-life language, reasonable safeguarding became a separate question regardless of the pain mechanism.**

This is the proposition set from which the next expert-facing and legal work should proceed.
