# Source-to-CLIN Completeness Audit

## Purpose

This file audits whether the controlling CLIN transcription faithfully preserves the substantive content of the underlying source material.

It exists because a material passage from the 25 March 2025 Monaghan complaint response - the clinician's description of a "very good relationship" conducted on a "Tom and Liam" basis - was present in the supplied source transcription but absent from `01_COMPLETE_RECORD_LEGAL_CANONICAL.md`.

## Authority rule

For evidential wording, use this order:

1. **Original source document** - controlling.
2. **Source-verified correction recorded in this audit** - controls over the older CLIN transcription to the extent of the verified discrepancy.
3. `01_COMPLETE_RECORD_LEGAL_CANONICAL.md` - controlling working transcription where no source discrepancy has been identified.
4. Analytical files - subordinate to all of the above.

A CLIN transcription is therefore an index and working evidential text, not a substitute for a source document where the two differ.

# Completed Audit - Document 21

## Source basis

Compared:

- current canonical Document 21, CLIN-003230 to CLIN-003273; and
- the full source transcription supplied by the patient for Dr Thomas Monaghan's 25 March 2025 response to Quality and Patient Safety.

The final-page relational passage was also visually supplied in the conversation.

The current canonical correctly labels Document 21 as a **substantive transcription**, not a verbatim one. Compression of greetings or repetition would therefore be unimportant. The defects below are recorded because they alter provenance, evidential meaning, defence evidence, patient-clinician relationship evidence, medication chronology, or the service's own account of documentation practice.

## Verified discrepancy register

### D21-C01 - Governance / record-location header omitted

**Source text includes:**

>
> "NOT FOR MR MOLONEY'S CLINICAL NOTES as per Complaints protocols."
>
> "Prepared in the understanding that this may of course be released to Mr Moloney and Independent Reviewers."

**Canonical:** omitted.

**Significance:** material provenance. The response was expressly prepared as complaint/governance correspondence and not for the ordinary clinical notes. Negative findings must therefore distinguish the clinical-note universe from the complaint/governance universe.

### D21-C02 - Enclosed clinic letter / patient-copying practice omitted

**Source text includes:**

**Canonical:** omitted.

**Significance:** directly relevant to communication, informational asymmetry, patient reliance and the clinician's asserted practice of sharing his reasoning.

### D21-C03 - Patient's March 2025 proposition and causal question omitted in sequence

**Source text reproduces the patient's complaint that Sinemet CR produced:**

> "immediate and sustained relief from two decades of disabling foot pain..."

and then the question:

> "why was this possibility not explored earlier given the magnitude of harm endured?"

**Canonical:** the later answer to the question is retained, but the full preceding complaint proposition is omitted.

**Significance:** the response should be read against the proposition actually being answered.

### D21-C04 - 10 March 2025 CR prescription was also intended to help morning pain

**Significance:** material to treatment calibration and causation. The March 2025 event should not be described as though CR's relevance to pain was entirely unforeseen by the clinician. The stronger expert question is why formulation/timing calibration directed at morning pain occurred only at that stage, after the later severity and psychological-risk chronology.

**Significance:** "morning pain" is specifically relevant to medication timing, wearing-off / overnight state, formulation choice and the later CR intervention. Until the canonical is corrected, the source wording **morning pain** controls.

## Material passages correctly preserved

The audit also confirms that the canonical does preserve the principal clinical defence propositions, including:

- Parkinson's can magnify pain from another cause, especially as levodopa wears off;
- Sinemet was understood to lessen the patient's experience of pain;
- dyskinesia and other levodopa risks informed cautious titration;
- peripheral neuropathic pain was framed as potentially contributory rather than necessarily exclusive;
- Monaghan says a Healy second opinion occurred;
- he says he remained open to being wrong and later requested another DAT scan;
- he says Sinemet had been used since 2021;
- pregabalin was used partly to spare further Sinemet increase, had incomplete effect at maximum dose, was later reduced, and pain deteriorated;
- recent Sinemet CR escalation produced "profound relief";
- the letter ends with a professional and deeply personal apology.

The defect is therefore not that the canonical reverses the letter's overall position. The defect is that its compression removes several facts that materially affect both sides of the case.

## Required analytical consequences

Until the source text is fully merged into the canonical record:

1. Do **not** quote CLIN-003264 as "ongoing pain". The source says **"morning pain"**.
2. Treat D21-C04 as affirmative defence/context evidence: Monaghan says the 10 March CR prescription was also intended to help the morning experience including pain.
3. Treat D21-C08 as affirmative defence evidence against any absolute claim that alternative causes were never explored.
4. Treat D21-C09 and D21-C13 as an internal documentary comparator: the clinician says his long letters exist to preserve shared understanding, factual nuance and decision provenance.
5. Treat D21-C14 as direct evidence relevant to trust and reliance.
6. Keep the apology separate from an admission of negligence. The source apologises for the patient's experience; it does not expressly admit breach or causation.
7. Keep the clinical-note record, complaint/governance record, PCS/Evolve record and clinician's own file conceptually distinct when making negative-documentary claims.

## Effect on the current case formulation

The completed audit does **not** require abandonment of the current formulation. It sharpens it in four directions:

- **Trust:** the treating neurologist himself describes a "very good relationship" on a "Tom and Liam" basis.
- **Treatment calibration:** CR was not merely an accidental analgesic observation; Monaghan says his 10 March prescription was also intended to help the morning experience including pain.
- **Documentary inference:** Monaghan expressly says his long letters are designed to preserve shared understanding and how/when/by whom decisions were made.
- **Defence discipline:** Monaghan expressly denies complete anchoring and cites investigation for Fabry disease; that evidence must be included in any fair expert instruction.

The later causation question remains expert-dependent: whether the medication strategy caused or materially aggravated the severe foot-pain state, what component was treatable earlier, and how that pain related to later psychological deterioration.
