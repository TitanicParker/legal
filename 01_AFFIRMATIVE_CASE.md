# 1. The case in one paragraph

An integrated painful-feet–gait–tone/movement problem reached Neurology in 2017: the GP expressly sought neurological opinion on the patient's gait, feet and muscle-tension issues, and Neurology then observed a striking footdrop-like lower-limb phenomenon it considered suggestive of dystonic posture rather than true foot drop. That same dystonic-looking gait was used affirmatively in the Parkinsonian diagnostic project, while the final synthesis placed the painful-foot/leg phenomena into an alternative branch in which they might be completely separate or secondary peripheral neuropathy and left the relationship `Not clear`. Thereafter the record repeatedly generated evidence crossing that separation: feet, gait and tension changed during procyclidine withdrawal; the treating neurologist later recorded rapid return of a focal outside-left-foot phenomenon; a later footdrop-like phenomenon was classified as Parkinsonian after directed distal testing; GP records re-related pain, gait and tone; later Neurology treated leg pain and shuffling as OFF-related; Sinemet was expressly recognised to relieve the feet from a pain perspective; and controlled-release Sinemet was introduced in the setting of severe morning foot pain. The presently available record demonstrates repeated local updating of particular foot phenomena; it does not presently demonstrate a corresponding global reconstruction of the original relationship among painful feet, dystonic/Parkinsonian lower-limb behaviour, structural mechanics, loading and neurological treatment state. That is the centre of gravity. The alleged Protest and unresolved document-authorship chronology may strengthen it substantially, but the case does not need either proposition to establish that centre.

---

# 2. Centre of alleged wrongdoing

> **If independent expert evidence establishes the relevant neurological duty, the centre of the alleged wrongdoing is the specialist partition of an integrated painful-foot/neurological lower-limb problem into separately managed explanatory streams without adequate demonstrated integration, followed by continuing stewardship in which accumulating evidence repeatedly crossed that partition without a presently demonstrated whole-case reconstruction of the founding relationship.**

The apparent wrongs are best understood as stages of one mechanism:

> **inadequate integration → documentary disposition/closure → loss or diffusion of ownership → failure adequately to reopen globally when crossing evidence accumulated → continuing stewardship of the resulting information state → later reliance on that accumulated state.**

Premature documentary construction after the Protest may ultimately support a graver separate allegation if native authorship/version evidence establishes it. It should not presently displace the integration/stewardship case as the principal centre.

# 3. The affirmative evidential sequence

## 3.1 The problem entered Neurology integrated

The GP did not send an already-separated Parkinsonian syndrome and an unrelated foot complaint. Before specialist admission, primary care had progressively joined:

- bilateral painful abnormal feet;
- abnormal/festinant/stiff gait;
- difficulty initiating movement;
- inability to relax / muscle tension;
- increased tone;
- clawing of the toes;
- impaired dexterity;
- deteriorating function.

The referral object was relational.

The specialist problem was therefore not merely “Does this patient have Parkinsonism?” It also included:

> **What is the relationship between the painful abnormal feet and the abnormal neurological lower-limb state?**

## 3.2 Neurology itself recognised the lower limb as neurologically meaningful

During the admission Neurology described the gait as strikingly abnormal and characterised a footdrop-like appearance as suggestive of dystonic posture rather than true foot drop.

This is important because the lower-limb phenomenon was not missed, invisible or merely retrospectively reinterpreted years later.

> **Neurology saw it and neurologically characterised it.**

## 3.3 The same lower-limb feature was diagnostically productive

The dystonic-looking gait was then used within the Parkinsonian diagnostic work-up, including DaTscan wording describing the walk as almost dystonically unusual.

This creates the founding asymmetry:

> **The limb was neurologically meaningful when it supplied diagnostic information.**

The unresolved question is what work justified treating the painful-foot relationship attached to that same presentation as potentially separate.

## 3.4 The final specialist synthesis created an affirmative alternative branch

The July synthesis did not merely remain silent about the painful feet. It affirmatively represented the relevant leg/foot phenomena as potentially completely separate or perhaps secondary peripheral neuropathy and ended `Not clear`.

That matters because the specialist information state transmitted downstream was capable of supporting separate explanatory and service pathways:

> **structural/mechanical foot disease → Podiatry / local foot care**

and

> **burning/leg-pain phenomena → possible peripheral-neuropathy reasoning**

while the Parkinsonian/dystonic disorder travelled through the movement-disorder pathway.

The repository's present record does not clearly demonstrate the complete relationship-directed clinical bridge by which that partition was earned. Whether reasonable neurological practice required more at the time is an expert question.

## 3.5 Early treatment-state evidence crossed the partition

Within months, neurological medication withdrawal was associated with returning bodily tension, restless feet and deteriorating gait. The October 2017 contemporaneous patient account also records a **twisting urge in the left foot** during that withdrawal state.

This is not simply a later theory about the feet. It is early contemporaneous phenomenology linking a foot-specific motor experience to neurological treatment state.

In April 2018, the treating neurologist himself recorded rapid return/change of the outside-left-foot phenomenon during procyclidine withdrawal.

The significance is cumulative:

> **the partition was confronted early by patient-specific evidence in which neurological treatment state and the foot changed together.**

## 3.6 The integrated relationship reappeared longitudinally

Later records repeatedly brought the domains back together.

Primary care again related painful/clawing feet to increased tone, gait abnormality and Parkinson's.

Neurology later examined a footdrop-like `throw`, documented normal distal movements and classified the phenomenon as Parkinsonian.

By 2023, Neurology was expressly reasoning that leg pain and shuffling could be OFF-related and considering dopaminergic timing while also considering non-Parkinsonian pain sources.

By 2025, the treating neurologist recorded that once Sinemet took effect the feet improved from a pain perspective, and that more Sinemet would probably reduce pain further although dyskinesia constrained escalation.

Controlled-release Sinemet then produced profound relief in the relevant morning/overnight pain state.

These events do not need to establish one exclusive mechanism to be important.

Their affirmative significance is:

> **the treating system repeatedly generated evidence that the painful foot domain and neurological treatment state were not clinically independent.**

## 3.7 The record demonstrates local updating

The later record does show genuine reassessment.

Individual phenomena were reclassified; OFF relationships were considered; neuropathy and other causes were reconsidered; treatment was adjusted; mixed mechanisms were entertained.

The case therefore is not that Neurology did nothing after 2017.

The stronger proposition is:

> **Later boundary-crossing evidence changed episode-level interpretation and treatment, but the presently available record does not demonstrate a corresponding global reconstruction of the original whole relationship among painful feet, dystonic/Parkinsonian lower-limb behaviour, structural pathology, loading and neurological treatment state.**

That is a proposition, not merely an unanswered question.

## 3.8 Ownership of the relationship is not clearly demonstrated

Podiatry owned genuine structural pathology.

Neurology owned the Parkinsonian/dystonic neurological state.

Pain/neuropathic treatment became another stream.

What the available record does not clearly identify is durable clinical ownership of the **relationship between those domains** once the original partition existed.

The relevant question is therefore not who treated each component.

It is:

> **Who was responsible for the arrows between them?**

On the present record, durable ownership of those arrows is not clearly demonstrated.

## 3.9 Same-clinician continuity makes this a stewardship case

Later clinician-authored evidence supports that Dr Thomas Monaghan usually continued to see the patient himself across the longitudinal period.

That matters because the later history cannot be analysed only as downstream clinicians inheriting a founding information state created by someone else.

The originating clinician remained substantially involved while evidence accumulated that crossed the original explanatory separation.

The case therefore becomes one of:

> **continuing stewardship of a founding information state by a clinician with repeated opportunities to observe what later evidence did to it.**

## 3.10 Later institutional reliance raises a separate validation question

The originating clinician later used the accumulated longitudinal history to explain why the care had been coherent and reasonable.

PAR/SIMT later concluded that there was no deficit in care.

Those are genuine later evidential events.

But the repository presently has the institutional conclusion without a complete evidential genealogy showing that the later process independently reconstructed:

- the founding integrated referral;
- the relationship-directed July examination;
- the alleged Protest;
- the document-authorship chronology;
- the later crossing evidence;
- and the question of local updating versus global reconstruction.

The affirmative proposition is therefore limited but important:

> **Accumulated longitudinal coherence is not automatically independent validation of the framework that helped organise the longitudinal record.**

# 4. Ten evidential propositions that should carry the case

## 1. Integrated referral

**Proposition:** Neurology received a painful-feet–gait–tone/movement problem, not two pre-separated complaints.

**Function:** establishes the relational object specialist care was being asked to address.

## 2. Neurological lower-limb recognition

**Proposition:** Neurology identified a striking footdrop-like lower-limb phenomenon as suggestive of dystonic posture rather than true foot drop.

**Function:** establishes that the relevant limb was already neurologically meaningful in 2017.

## 3. Diagnostic use of dystonic gait

**Proposition:** the dystonic-looking gait was affirmatively used in the Parkinsonian/DaTscan diagnostic project.

**Function:** establishes documentary asymmetry: the limb supplied neurological evidence when diagnostically useful.

## 4. Affirmative separate/neuropathy branch

**Proposition:** the final synthesis placed the relevant leg/foot phenomena into a branch in which they might be completely separate or secondary peripheral neuropathy and left the relationship `Not clear`.

**Function:** identifies the portable specialist information state that later care could inherit.

## 5. Incomplete visible relational bridge

**Proposition:** the surviving July record does not presently demonstrate the complete directed distal/neuropathy/posture-loading bridge later used to discriminate a similar footdrop-like phenomenon.

**Function:** converts the issue from “why was neuropathy mentioned?” into “what work earned the partition?”

## 6. Early medication-state crossing

**Proposition:** procyclidine withdrawal changed tension, foot phenomena and gait; the treating neurologist later recorded rapid focal foot change during withdrawal.

**Function:** supplies an early reopening threshold.

## 7. Later independent reintegration in primary care

**Proposition:** GP records later again related feet, pain, tone, gait and Parkinson's.

**Function:** shows that the integrated relationship did not disappear from the patient's clinical reality merely because it had been partitioned in specialist synthesis.

## 8. Later Neurology itself crosses the boundary

**Proposition:** Neurology later classified a footdrop-like phenomenon as Parkinsonian and reasoned about OFF-related leg pain/shuffling.

**Function:** makes neurological contribution to the foot/lower-limb domain an internal treating-service proposition, not merely a patient theory.

## 9. Explicit dopaminergic pain relationship

**Proposition:** by 2025 the treating neurologist recorded Sinemet-related improvement in the feet from a pain perspective and expected further Sinemet could reduce pain, constrained by dyskinesia.

**Function:** places pain itself inside dopaminergic treatment-state reasoning.

## 10. Same-clinician stewardship and later reliance

**Proposition:** the originating neurologist remained substantially involved, and later clinician/institutional explanations relied on the accumulated history.

**Function:** transforms the case from simple downstream inheritance into stewardship and later-validation analysis.

# 5. Cumulative inference

The case should not be analysed by requiring every event independently to prove breach.

Read cumulatively:

> **integrated referral → neurological recognition of dystonic-looking lower limb → diagnostic use of that feature → affirmative separate/neuropathy disposition for the painful-foot relationship → early treatment-state crossing → same-clinician focal foot change → later Parkinsonian foot classification → OFF-related pain/shuffling → explicit dopaminergic pain benefit → repeated local management without presently demonstrated whole-case reconstruction.**

The reasonable cumulative inference is:

> **An integrated neurological/foot problem was partitioned; the same clinical system subsequently generated repeated evidence reconnecting neurological state and the feet; individual episodes were recognised and treated, but the presently available record does not demonstrate a corresponding explicit global reconstruction of the relationship that had originally been partitioned.**

# 6. The Protest and document-construction limb

The alleged pre-discharge Protest is not required to establish the affirmative centre above.

If accepted as historical fact, it materially strengthens the case by converting unfinished integration into a **decision-after-notice** problem immediately before closure.

The separate construction suspicion asks whether consequential wording — including `leg pain`, `burning`, possible secondary peripheral neuropathy, alcohol linkage and `Not clear` — already existed before the Protest or was first added or materially reformulated afterwards.

That question is important because it may show not merely incomplete integration but post-notice documentary construction of the pathway by which the unresolved problem became portable.

At present that graver limb remains dependent on native authorship/version/provenance evidence.

It is therefore an aggravating or potentially independent documentary limb, not the load-bearing premise of the merits case.

# 7. Harm and safety consequence

By 2023 the foot/lower-leg pain had become severe enough to produce direct death-associated language from the patient and urgent GP concern.

That creates a safety question independent of the exact mechanism of the pain.

The causation case should remain threshold-specific:

> **If expert evidence identifies a point at which reasonable integration or reconstruction should have altered management or information flow, what additional period of pain, disability or psychological harm probably followed from the failure at that point, over and above underlying disease, genuine structural pathology and unavoidable treatment constraints?**

# 8. Expert issue

The repository can establish the documentary and longitudinal predicates.

Independent movement-disorders expertise is required to answer the medical duty questions, including:

- what relationship-directed assessment reasonable neurological practice required in July 2017;
- whether the separate/neuropathy branch was adequately earned;
- which later crossing events required formal re-phenotyping or whole-case reconstruction;
- whether mixed structural and neurological mechanisms increased rather than reduced the need for integrated ownership;
- and whether treatment and safeguarding were appropriately calibrated at the relevant thresholds.

# 9. Controlling proposition

> **An integrated painful-feet–gait–tone problem entered Neurology in 2017. Neurology identified the lower-limb abnormality as potentially dystonic and used that feature within the Parkinsonian diagnostic work-up, while the final specialist synthesis placed the painful-foot phenomena into a potentially separate structural/peripheral-neuropathy branch and left the relationship unresolved. The surviving record does not presently demonstrate the full relationship-directed clinical work by which that partition was established. Over the following years, the same treating system — and substantially the same originating neurologist — repeatedly encountered evidence crossing that partition: treatment-state foot change, a focal foot phenomenon rapidly returning during withdrawal, later Parkinsonian classification of a footdrop-like phenomenon, OFF-related pain/shuffling, Sinemet-related pain benefit and further dopaminergic responsiveness. Those observations produced repeated local reassessment and treatment, but the presently available record does not demonstrate a corresponding global reconstruction of the original relationship among neurological state, abnormal lower-limb posture/gait, structural mechanics, loading and pain. If independent expert evidence establishes that reasonable neurological care required such integration or reconstruction at one or more of those thresholds, that failure — not an allegation that every foot symptom was neurological or that the Parkinsonian diagnosis was wrong — is the central alleged breach.**
